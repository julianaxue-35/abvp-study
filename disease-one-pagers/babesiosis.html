Babesiosis

Vector-borne · Protozoal · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Tick-borne protozoal parasite of red blood cells → immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly.

Aetiology

  • Agent — tick-borne intra-erythrocytic protozoa (piroplasms) that parasitise red blood cells
  • Large Babesia = Babesia canis
  • Small Babesia = Babesia gibsoni — also B. conradae and B. vulpes among the clinically important small species
  • B. gibsoni prevalence ~1%

Transmission

  • Ixodid (hard) ticks — Rhipicephalus / Dermacentor for B. canis
  • B. gibsoni also spread by dog-bite — higher prevalence in American Pit Bull Terriers suggests breed susceptibility (possibly due to fighting, per Juliana's note)+
  • Also blood transfusion and transplacental spread

Clinical Signs

  • IMHA (immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia)
  • Thrombocytopenia (low platelet count)
  • Lethargy · fever
  • Splenomegaly (enlarged spleen)
  • Pale mucous membranes
  • Pigmenturia (discoloured urine from haemoglobin / bilirubin)

Diagnosis

  1. Blood-smear examination (microscopy)
    • Look for intra-erythrocytic piroplasms inside red blood cells
  2. PCR (polymerase chain reaction)
    • Species-level identification; ↑ most sensitive test

Treatment

  1. B. canis — imidocarb dipropionate / diminazene aceturate
  2. B. gibsoni — atovaquone + azithromycin
    ⚠ Triple combination: Malarone (atovaquone-proguanil) 13.5 mg/kg PO q24h + azithromycin 10 mg/kg PO q24h + artesunate 12.5 mg/kg PO q24h for 10 days improved haematology / biochemistry to reference range within ~14 days, stayed stable to day 56, with no relapse of parasitaemia at days 180 / 360 / 540 / 720 — successful elimination in chronically infected dogs.

Prevention

  • Tick control
  • Screen & quarantine imported and fighting-breed dogs
  • Screen blood donors

📄 Journal Articles

  • De Ritis quotient & renal azotaemia (B. canis): canine babesiosis can cause pre-renal and renal azotaemia and raise the De Ritis quotient (AST/ALT). AST activity and AST/ALT were higher in azotaemic dogs, and higher still with renal than pre-renal azotaemia, correlating with renal indices — so raised serum AST in azotaemic B. canis dogs may be of renal origin, and AST/ALT is a simple, convenient marker to recognise renal azotaemia in canine babesiosis.
  • Babesia gibsoni — a European perspective: large Babesia = B. canis, small = B. gibsoni; the clinically important small species are B. gibsoni, B. conradae and B. vulpes. B. gibsoni prevalence ~1%, with higher prevalence in American Pit Bull Terriers suggesting breed susceptibility.
  • Malarone + azithromycin + artesunate for B. gibsoni: a tick-borne protozoal parasite causing IMHA, thrombocytopenia, lethargy and/or splenomegaly. The 10-day triple combination improved haematology/biochemistry to reference range within ~14 days and eliminated parasitaemia with no relapse at days 180–720 in chronically infected dogs.
