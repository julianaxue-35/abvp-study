Canine Parvovirus (CPV)

Infectious Disease · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Non-enveloped ssDNA virus targeting rapidly dividing intestinal crypt cells → villous atrophy, malabsorption & endotoxaemia. Environmentally hardy; the classic young, unvaccinated, over-crowded-shelter presentation.

Virology

  • CPV-2a & 2b — can also infect cats; milder form
  • CPV-2c → leukopenic form in dogs
  • Non-enveloped, single-stranded DNA virus
  • Hardy in the environment — hard to kill, survives long

Transmission & Pathogenesis

  • Faecal–oral route
  • Targets rapidly dividing cells → small-intestinal (SI) crypts → villous atrophy → malabsorption + loss of mucosal barrier
  • → endotoxaemia (bacterial translocation across the damaged gut)+

Risk Factors

  • Age < 6 months (< 5 months per ASV — Association of Shelter Veterinarians)
  • Unvaccinated · warm season · purebred dogs
  • Overcrowding · inappropriate / inadequate isolation
  • Poor biosecurity & sanitation · rural areas

Incubation

  • 4–14 days
  • Shedding begins ~4–5 days after infection

Diagnosis

  1. Point-of-care SNAP (faecal antigen ELISA)
    • ↑ Specificity — a positive is a true positive
    • ↓ Sensitivity — ↑ false negatives
    • False neg: low faecal viral load, mild signs, poor sampling, high faecal antibody binding the antigen
  2. qPCR (gold standard)
    • High sensitivity (94.4%), high specificity
    ⚠ Trap: recent modified-live vaccine (MLV), days 3–28 post-vaccine, can read as a true positive (vaccine virus shed).
  3. Haematology / biochem
    • Leukopenia (hallmark!) · anaemia · coagulopathy
    • Electrolyte changes · hypoglycaemia · hypoalbuminaemia
  4. Necropsy (confirm: SNAP / qPCR / histopath)
    • Subserosal SI haemorrhage (tan → dark red), esp. around Peyer's patches
    • Best tissue sample: duodenum, jejunum / ileum

Clinical Signs forms

  • Subclinical — silent shedder!
  • Subacute — gastroenteric (vomiting, haemorrhagic diarrhoea)+
  • Myocardial (neonates, rare)+

Treatment

  1. IV fluid therapy (IVFT) — correct hypovolaemic shock; add KCl; treat hypoglycaemia with 25% dextrose @ 1–2 mL/kg IV
  2. ± Antibiotics — resistance shown to metronidazole and 3rd/4th-gen cephalosporins; use with caution + culture & sensitivity (C&S)
  3. Antiemetics
  4. Pain relief
  5. Nutrition (early enteral feeding)+
  6. CPMA (canine parvovirus monoclonal antibody) → mortality rate ↑ but not significant; ↓ hospital stay; ↑ recovery; ↓ overall cost

Inpatient vs Outpatient

  • Inpatient — $$$ · IV fluids · success 80–90%
  • Outpatient — $ · subcutaneous (SQ) fluids 30–60 mL/kg q6–12 h+ · success 75–83%

Outbreak Management by risk

  • HIGH — < 5 mo, regardless of vaccine hx → vaccinate immediately + isolate / quarantine
  • MODERATE — ≥ 5 mo, no vaccine hx → antibody titre testing
  • LOW — ≥ 5 mo, full vaccine hx → proceed with placement

Biosecurity

  • AVOID quaternary ammonium compounds (QUATs) — parvo is resistant + correction: your notes say "QUADS" — the disinfectant class parvo resists is QUATs (quaternary ammonium compounds).
  • Use accelerated hydrogen peroxide (AHP) or bleach (sodium hypochlorite)
