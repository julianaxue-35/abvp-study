Brucellosis (Brucella canis)

Zoonotic · Bacterial · Community & Public Health · Shelter Medicine

One of the most prevalent zoonoses worldwide. Points below are compiled from your summary + standard references (the AVMA one-health review full text is paywalled). Reproductive-tract zoonosis; chronic intracellular carriage means treatment rarely clears it — confirmed shelter cases are often euthanised.

Aetiology

  • Brucella canis — gram-negative, intracellular coccobacillus; causes reproductive & systemic disease in dogs.
  • US one-health species: B. canis (dogs), B. suis (swine), B. abortus (cattle/bison); B. melitensis not endemic but a risk to international travellers.
  • Eradicated from US domestic livestock, but persists in companion animals (B. canis) & wildlife reservoirs (B. suis/abortus); enzootic internationally.

Transmission & Zoonosis

  • Venereal — semen, vaginal discharge, urine (mating / artificial insemination).
  • Aborted fetuses, placenta & birth fluids carry a very high organism load; also fomites / shared equipment.
  • NOT airborne — requires direct contact with infectious secretions.
  • Human exposure (zoonotic) — unpasteurised dairy; occupational (vets, lab diagnosticians, animal-care staff).

Clinical Signs

  • Reproductive failure — late abortion (~day 45–55), stillbirths, weak neonates, failure to conceive.
  • Males — epididymitis, testicular atrophy, scrotal dermatitis, infertility.
  • Systemic — discospondylitis (painful back / paresis), uveitis, lymphadenopathy, fever.
  • Many are asymptomatic carriers shedding intermittently under stress / immunosuppression.

Diagnosis

  • RSAT (rapid slide agglutination) — in-clinic screen; sensitive but ↑ false positives.
  • Confirm with TAT / ME-TAT, AGID (agar gel immunodiffusion), PCR or culture.
  • Culture / PCR: notify the lab first — Brucella is a biosafety hazard.
⚠ Trap: a positive RSAT MUST be confirmed before any disposition decision (high false-positive rate).

Treatment & Setting

  • Chronic intracellular carriage → antimicrobials rarely clear infection; relapse is common.
  • Confirmed shelter cases are often euthanised; at minimum permanent isolation + neuter.
  • Screen imported dogs from endemic regions (Mexico, Central/South America, E. Europe, parts of Asia).

Biosecurity / Reportable

  • Reportable zoonosis — notify authorities.
  • PPE + careful handling of reproductive tissues/fluids; routine disinfectants are effective.
  • Public-health message: avoid unpasteurised dairy; counsel at-risk owners & staff.

📄 Journal Article

  • One-health review of brucellosis in the US: brucellosis is one of the most prevalent zoonoses worldwide. The species of greatest US one-health relevance are B. canis (dogs), B. suis (swine) and B. abortus (cattle/bison); B. melitensis is not endemic but risks international travellers. Though eradicated from US livestock, B. canis persists in companion animals and B. suis/abortus in wildlife reservoirs, with enzootic presence internationally. Human exposure is mainly unpasteurised dairy + occupational contact (lab staff, vets, animal carers). Diagnosis & treatment are challenging (assay limits; nonspecific, insidious signs; immune / antimicrobial evasion) — so prevention is key.
  • Brucella prevalence in stray cattle, dogs & cats (systematic review): stray-dog seroprevalence was highest in Jordan (38%) and Pakistan (38%), with none detected in Brazil, north Colombia, Cyprus, South Korea or the USA; all stray-cattle studies were from India (4.3–64.3% seroprevalence).
  • Reverse-complement PCR to distinguish B. canis: a specific reverse-complement sequence differentiates B. canis from other Brucella species, enabling more comprehensive PCR identification. (molecular method — likely low exam yield)
