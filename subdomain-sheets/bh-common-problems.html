Exam cheatsheet for the Common Behaviour Problems subdomain — highest-yield traps, numbers, and decision rules; clarified points marked +.

Medical and Neurological Contributors, and Senior Pets

  • Brain regions — (limbic · forebrain · brainstem — signs when damaged)
  • Specific contributors — neoplasia · seizures · toxicosis · degenerative · inflammatory · urogenital
  • CCDS (canine cognitive dysfunction syndrome) — onset, pathology, reassess
    • DISHA screen: Disorientation · altered Interaction · Sleep-wake change · Housesoiling · altered Activity+
  • Treatment of CCDS — selegiline (MAOB-I, ↑ dopamine) · diet (Hill's b/d) · always add enrichment
  • Rule out medical/neuro before labelling behavioural — change precedes severe neuro signs by weeks–months; brain = 2nd commonest neoplasia site
  • Tumours: primary (meningioma) = insidious/slow; secondary/metastatic = ACUTE; dogs >7y but age can't exclude
  • CCDS onset: dogs ≥11y (spatial memory 6–9y, frontal lobe atrophies first); cats 10–11y; reassess q6mo
TrapSelegiline NEVER with MAOIs, SSRIs or TCAs (also tramadol, SAMe, tryptophan, buspirone) → serotonin syndrome. Anxiolytic disinhibition can paradoxically ↑ aggression in a senior that would otherwise have retreated.

Compulsive Disorders and Stereotypies

  • Origins — normal behaviour → maladaptive (grooming, ingestion, predation, locomotion)
  • Risk factors — early environment · enrichment gaps · conflict · punishment
  • By category — locomotor · ingestive (pica) · self-trauma (ALD acral lick dermatitis) · hyperesthesia
  • Pharmacology
    • Clomipramine = most serotonin-selective TCA (tricyclic antidepressant); SSRI/TCA (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor) first line — continue ≥ 6 months+
  • Treatment principles — reward not punish · predictable routine
  • Onset prepubertal 3–6mo or pre-social-maturity 12–36mo, after conflict/change/distress; mechanism = abnormal serotonin → SSRI/clomipramine 1st line
  • SSRI/TCA monotherapy ↓ compulsive behaviour 50–75% by 4wk; may DOUBLE dose at 4–6wk if insufficient (watch serotonin syndrome)
  • Dopamine AGONISTS (apomorphine, amphetamines) INDUCE stereotypy; antipsychotics block them
  • Feline hyperesthesia hallmark: rippling THORACOLUMBAR skin + EPAXIAL spasm, can't interrupt; feline pica material order wool→cotton→synthetic→rubber→paper
TrapIf a compulsive disorder responds to meds, continue ≥6 months (often lifelong); premature taper triggers immediate relapse.

Separation Anxiety and Destructive Behaviours

  • Separation anxiety: diagnosis
    • Separation anxiety — distress signs bound to owner departure/absence (vocalising, destruction at exits, housesoiling, salivation)+
  • Prognosis and pharmacology — (cross-ref Behaviour Medications hub)
  • Four-pillar management — shape independence · ↓ departure/homecoming anxiety · decouple departure cues · anxiety reduction
  • Destructive behaviours — canine chewing · canine digging · feline scratching (visual mark + interdigital SCENT)
  • SA = dx of exclusion; 3 classic signs occur ONLY in owner's absence: vocalisation · destruction (at exits) · housesoiling
  • Meds: daily SSRI/TCA + benzo (alprazolam/diazepam) or clonidine 1h PRE-departure; fluoxetine/clomipramine 75% vs amitriptyline 56%
  • Mock departures ALWAYS shorter than the dog's anxiety latency; keep homecomings low-key, ignore until calm
TrapConfinement frustration mimics SA. True SA is consistent and occurs ONLY on departure — suspect another cause if signs occur while the family is home or only randomly.

Housesoiling and Marking

  • Canine housesoiling — incomplete house-training · submissive/excitement urination (never punish)
  • Feline housesoiling: three-way split
    • House-soiling triage: (1) medical → (2) inappropriate toileting (litter/box aversion, substrate/location) → (3) marking / spraying+
  • Marking (urine spraying) — conflict / anxiety driven
  • Marking treatment — ↓ stimuli · Feliway diffuser · SSRI/clomipramine
  • Punishment WORSENS housesoiling in both species; never punish submissive/excitement urination (improves with age)
  • Litterbox rule: 1 per cat +1; ≥1.5× cat length; scoop 1–2×/day; weekly hot-water clean (no strong disinfectant)
  • Marking posture: VERTICAL surface, tail ERECT, treads hind legs, sprays; postpuberty. Rx: fluoxetine 1mg/kg; clomipramine 0.25–0.5mg/kg = only cat-registered drug in AU
  • Feliway = FACIAL pheromone (environmental); Felifriend = ALLOMARKING (cat-to-cat intro) — different indications
TrapCastration ↓ spraying in 90% of males; spaying ↓ spraying in 95% of females.

Canine Aggression

  • Three umbrella categories — affective/social (offensive vs defensive) · predatory (moving stimulus, no arousal) · play (play-bow)
  • The 13 aggression types
    • Resource guarding — possessive aggression over food, objects, resting sites, or people+
    • Barrier frustration — aggression at a fence/window/leash barrier (frustration / redirected)+
  • Behavioural pathology criteria · Risk assessment and prognosis
  • Treatment plan (7 steps) — medical → educate → safety → management → BMOD → tools → meds; NO physical punishment
  • Fear body-language: tail DOWN, ears BACK, weight shifted AWAY; can look offensive behind a barrier or at distance
  • Resource-guarding onset ~16wk; adult onset if ↑appetite (reducing diet, hyperadrenocorticism); control usually in 2–3mo
  • Household intraspecific: same-sex, esp FEMALES; spayed females over-represented. Territorial (home/yard/car) vs protective (needs family PRESENT — friendly to strangers when family absent)
  • 6 prognostic factors: latency to arousal · environment · complexity · harm potential · predictability · emotional stability
TrapDominance is the MOST over-diagnosed type (needs confident temperament + assertive signalling + aggression to family WITHOUT fear). Most owner-directed aggression is really CONFLICT-related or RESOURCE GUARDING. Caution: OHE in females can ↑ reactivity/aggression toward family and unfamiliar dogs.

Feline Aggression

  • The 10 aggression types — (petting-induced, redirected, play, fear, territorial, inter-cat …)
  • Pharmacology — SSRI/TCA; buspirone can DISINHIBIT → ↑ aggression; benzos → HEPATOTOXICITY reports in cats
  • Play = MOST COMMON aggression toward owners; SILENT stalking; peaks 9–12mo; hand-raised highest risk; chase toys NOT hands (a same-age cat can be curative)
  • Petting-induced = 2nd most referred; bites over the LOWER BACK once stroking exceeds tolerance; respect the threshold, never restrain
  • Redirected = classic ACUTE hostility between amicable housemates; settling takes hours–days; SEPARATE + gradual reintro (scent before sight), never "fight it out"
TrapTwo amicable housemate cats suddenly fight → most likely REDIRECTED aggression from an outside trigger (cat at window, strange noise, visitor); identify and block the trigger.

Behaviour Forensics and Counselling

  • Behaviour forensics — fighting dogs · hoarder and puppy-mill dogs · evaluation protocol
  • Counselling and history-taking — history-taking protocol
  • Decision frameworks — 4-tier: management → BMOD → meds+BMOD → euthanasia; meds alone rarely durable for anxiety problems
  • Fighting dogs: LESS aggressive to PEOPLE, test dog–dog; OPPOSITE-sex stimulus FIRST then same-sex; intro through fence barrier, leashes slightly taut
  • 83% of ex-puppy-mill breeding dogs still show problems (fear, housesoiling, compulsive) after rehoming
  • Problem history = 5 Ws (what/where/when/who/why); adopter counselling ↑ success ~27.5%; 51% report >1 problem at 1wk; 2wk = problem-emergence window
TrapFake-dog test with a full-mouthed bite + hold/shake = unsafe around animals; with no evidence of BMOD efficacy, euthanasia is the recommended outcome.

📄 Journal Article

  • Androgen-secreting adrenocortical tumours in 8 cats (case series): affected cats showed urine marking, aggression and other behavioural concerns; serum androgens (androstenedione or testosterone) were abnormally high, and adrenal tissue showed adrenocortical adenoma/carcinoma — consider endocrine disease in cats presenting with abnormal behaviour.
