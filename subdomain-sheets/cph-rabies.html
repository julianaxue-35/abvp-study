Rabies

Rabies · Community & Public Health (11%) · Shelter Medicine

Rabies — virtually 100% fatal once clinical; highest-yield topic in this domain. Exam focus: what counts as exposure, the 10-day rule, quarantine durations, PEP + RIG, DFA testing. Pre-filled points marked +.

Overview & Virology

  • Virtually 100% fatal once clinical — a neurotropic (nerve-seeking) encephalitis; family Rhabdoviridae, genus Lyssavirus, enveloped negative-sense RNA virus — envelope makes it easily inactivated by soap & water, bleach, alcohol (contrast the hardy, non-enveloped parvo).+
  • G protein = cell entry + sole virus-neutralising-antibody target = the antigen in every licensed vaccine (N, P, M, L are the other four).
  • US wildlife reservoirs: raccoon (east), skunk (central/CA), fox (TX/AK), bats (all 49 states) — bat variants cause most US human deaths; dog-maintained rabies eliminated in the US.
  • Cross-protection is complete among RABV variants → variant ID is epidemiology only, never changing vaccine or PEP choice.
Trap Cats (not dogs) are the #1 reported rabid domestic animal in the US — cat vaccination lags. Dogs remain #1 globally (>95% of ~59,000 human deaths/yr, Africa & Asia).

Pathophysiology & Transmission

  • Exposure = saliva or neural tissue into a wound / mucous membrane — bite is the dominant route; blood, urine, feces, and contact with intact skin are NOT exposures and do not require PEP (post-exposure prophylaxis).+
  • Myocyte replication → retrograde axoplasmic transport up nerves (~50–100 mm/day) → CNS → centrifugal spread to salivary glands & skin.
  • Face/neck bites incubate faster (shorter route to CNS); incubation 3–12 wk typical, range 2 wk–6 mo (rarely 1 yr).
  • Centrifugal (salivary-gland) phase = why saliva is infectious & enables ante-mortem nuchal-skin biopsy / saliva RT-PCR.

Clinical Signs & Diagnosis

  • Three stages (dog/cat): prodromal (behaviour change, mydriasis) → furious (aggression, pica, hypersalivation, seizures) → paralytic/"dumb" (jaw drop, dysphagia, ascending LMN paralysis, death) — not always in order.
  • First clinical sign → death = 2–10 days; an animal alive & well >10 days is unlikely rabid (but can't be excluded without DFA).
  • Cattle/large animals often present paralytic-only: pharyngeal paralysis, bloat, choke-mimic → keep rabies on any large-animal CNS differential.
  • DFA on fresh brain (brainstem, cerebellum, hippocampus) = gold standard (~near 100%); Negri bodies only ~70–80% sensitive; RT-PCR (saliva/nuchal skin/CSF) for ante-mortem.

The 10-Day Observation Rule

  • 10-day bite quarantine — dogs, cats & ferrets ONLY — a healthy animal at day 10 was not shedding virus in saliva at the time of the bite; the rule is not valid for wildlife or most livestock (they cannot be observed — test instead).+
  • Healthy dog/cat/ferret that bites a person → 10-day observation instead of euthanasia/testing; if it sickens or dies → euthanize + submit for DFA immediately.
  • Rationale: these species shed in saliva only a few days before signs & die within days → healthy at day 10 = non-infectious saliva at the time of the bite.
  • Do NOT vaccinate during the 10-day quarantine (it confounds the observation record); vaccinate once the window closes.
Trap The 10-day rule & observation quarantine do NOT apply to wildlife or most livestock — a bat, raccoon, skunk, fox or coyote bite is never observed: euthanize + submit brain for DFA, start PEP pending results.

Shelter Exposure Protocol

  • Unvaccinated exposed animal = long strict quarantine OR euthanasia — 4 mo / 120 days for dogs & cats, 6 mo for ferrets (owner's choice vs. euthanasia + DFA (direct fluorescent antibody test)); a currently-vaccinated exposed animal gets an immediate booster + 45-day observation.+
  • Vaccinate an unvaccinated exposed animal within 96 h of exposure, at the start of its isolation quarantine.
  • Overdue but ≥1 prior USDA-licensed rabies vaccine → booster + 45-day observation (manage as vaccinated); no documented prior vaccine = unvaccinated pathway.
  • Key numbers: 10 d bite-obs · 45 d vaccinated-exposed obs · 96 h vaccinate window · 120 d / 4 mo unvacc dog/cat · 6 mo unvacc ferret.

Staff Exposure Management

  • Bite/scratch → wash wound with soap & water ≥10–15 min immediately (major risk reduction alone); report + seek medical eval; notify health dept (mandatory, all states).
  • PrEP-vaccinated staff bitten → 2 vaccine doses (days 0 & 3), NO RIG; unvaccinated → full PEP series + RIG.
  • At-risk staff (vets, techs, ACOs, wildlife rehabbers) = CDC Category II → 3-dose PrEP (days 0, 7, 21–28) + titre check ~q2yr.
  • PEP decision = attending physician + local health dept (species, vax status, testability, regional risk).

PEP & Vaccines

  • PEP is ~100% effective if prompt & complete — unvaccinated person: wound wash + RIG (rabies immune globulin, 20 IU/kg, infiltrate into/around wound) on day 0 + vaccine days 0, 3, 7, 14; previously-vaccinated person: 2 doses (days 0 & 3), NO RIG.+
  • Vet vaccines are all killed or recombinant — no licensed MLV rabies vaccine for dogs/cats in the US; 1st dose ≥12 wk, booster at 1 yr, then q1 or q3 yr.
  • 1-yr vs 3-yr products are immunologically identical (label/regulatory difference, not protection); a 1-yr product 13 mo out = overdue → treat as unvaccinated.
  • Human vaccine into deltoid (adult) / anterolateral thigh (child) — NEVER gluteus (poor response); RIG never in the same site/syringe as vaccine.
  • Cats: give rabies in the right distal hind limb (FISS amputability); non-adjuvanted recombinant preferred.

Public Health & Law

  • NASPHV Compendium (National Association of State Public Health Veterinarians — Compendium of Animal Rabies Prevention & Control) is the authoritative reference for quarantine periods, vaccine schedules & exposure management; rabies is notifiable in all US states.+
  • Report suspected/confirmed animal rabies + all human exposures to the local/state health dept.
  • Quarantine authority rests with the health dept / animal control, not the shelter vet (the vet advises & supplies clinical info only).
  • Euthanasia for DFA = IV pentobarbital or deep-anaesthesia IC only; gunshot destroys the brainstem → non-diagnostic specimen.
  • National surveillance flows via states → CDC; published as the annual JAVMA rabies surveillance report.

One Health Framework for Rabies

  • Zero by 30 (WHO/WOAH/FAO): end dog-mediated human rabies deaths by 2030; sustaining ≥70% dog-vaccination coverage breaks the transmission cycle.
  • Four pillars: dog vaccination · responsible ownership & population management · accessible human PEP · wildlife ORV + integrated surveillance.
  • ORV (oral rabies vaccine baits) contains wildlife rabies — e.g. blocks westward spread of the raccoon variant.
  • Shelters = vaccination points (HVLC clinics, TNR w/ rabies vax) + surveillance nodes; shelter vet = clinician + public-health + educator.
  • Global ~59,000 deaths/yr (mostly children, Africa/Asia); dog vaccination beats expanding PEP alone on cost-effectiveness.
