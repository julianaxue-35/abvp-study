Reportable & Emerging Disease

Reportable & Emerging Disease · Community & Public Health (11%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet for the Reportable & Emerging Disease subdomain — mandatory reporting, notifiable zoonoses, emerging pathogens, outbreak response & surveillance; clarified / added points marked +.

Disease Reporting Framework

  • Mandatory reporting — a shelter vet has the same legal duty as any licensed vet; report animal diseases to the state veterinarian / USDA APHIS (Animal & Plant Health Inspection Service)+
  • Tiered system — local → state (state vet = animals; health dept = humans) → federal (USDA APHIS animals · CDC NNDSS humans) → international WOAH (formerly OIE)
  • NLRAD (Nat'l List of Reportable Animal Diseases) est. 2023 — first unified federal list; states still retain authority to add diseases
  • WOAH-listed & shelter-relevant: rabies, anthrax (all spp.), echinococcosis; Brucella canis is not WOAH-listed
Trap No single federal list overrides state law — a disease reportable in one state may not be in another. For animal disease, always start with the state veterinarian.

Who Reports and to Whom

  • Vet (animal disease) → state veterinarian / state animal-health official
  • Physician (human) → local/state health dept (NNDSS-compatible)
  • Lab → state health or agriculture dept; electronic lab reporting (ELR)
  • State animal-health official → USDA APHIS / WOAH; 24 h requirement for foreign animal diseases
  • Duty is the vet's — can't delegate to animal control / management; failure to report = licensing violation

Reporting Timelines & Priority Categories

  • Foreign animal diseases — FMD (foot-and-mouth disease), HPAI (highly pathogenic avian influenza), Newcastle: clinical suspicion alone triggers an immediate call — do not wait for lab confirmation+
  • Immediate (≤24 h) — FADs, HPAI, FMD, anthrax, plague, tularemia → state vet emergency line
  • 24–72 h — confirmed rabies, brucellosis, leptospirosis, most zoonotic clusters
  • Weekly / monthly — endemic trend-surveillance conditions (WNV, heartworm)
Trap "Suspect vs confirmed": most endemic diseases report on lab confirmation; FADs report on clinical suspicion — don't wait.

High-Priority Reportable Zoonoses

  • Immediately reportable — rabies, plague, tularemia, anthrax (CDC Category A bioterrorism agents); cats are uniquely susceptible amplifying hosts for plague & tularemia+
  • Plague (Y. pestis) — western US, flea vector; cats septicemic + pneumonic (transmit to humans); dogs resistant / subclinical
  • Tularemia (F. tularensis) — Tier 1 select agent; notify the lab before submitting suspect specimens
  • Lepto — MAT gold standard (4-fold paired titre); doxycycline clears renal carriage; killed by routine disinfectants + drying (unlike CPV)
  • Q fever (C. burnetii) & Salmonellosis — lab-confirmed reportable; Salmonella animal clusters (not isolated cases) trigger a state report even though only human cases go straight to health depts
Trap Cats > dogs for both severity and human transmission in plague/tularemia — a tech bitten by a sick cat from an endemic area puts both in the differential.

Brucella canis — Shelter Detail

  • Not curable — antibiotics suppress but don't clear infection; confirmatory-positive dogs generally not adoptable without informed consent — euthanasia a common shelter decision+
  • Spread via reproductive secretions (semen, vaginal discharge, aborted fetus / placenta) + urine; shelter risk = intact dogs, puppy mills, hoarding
  • Signs: abortion at 45–59 d gestation, orchitis / epididymitis, discospondylitis; many chronically infected but clinically normal
  • Dx: RSAT screen (sens high, spec low) → confirm with TAT/2-ME, AGID, or blood culture / PCR; lab-confirmed reportable in most states
Trap RSAT is a low-specificity screening test — confirm before any euthanasia decision; never act on a single positive screen.

HPAI in Shelter Contexts

  • H5N1 in cats — clade 2.3.4.4b infects cats via raw poultry / infected birds → severe neuro + respiratory signs, high mortality; immediately reportable to USDA APHIS & the state vet+
  • Cat signs: ataxia, blindness, seizures + respiratory; 2024 shelter outbreaks tied to raw-poultry diet + backyard-flock exposure
  • USDA / CDC advise against feeding raw poultry to pets in HPAI-affected regions
  • Staff PPE = N95 + gloves + eye protection; unprotected exposure → occ health + local health dept (± oseltamivir)
Trap No sustained human-to-human spread as of 2025, but H5N1 causes severe human disease — a pandemic-potential priority pathogen; treat every suspect cat as a zoonotic hazard.

Emerging & Re-Emerging Pathogens

  • EID = newly appeared or rapidly rising in incidence / geographic / host range; drivers = land-use change, trade + travel, climate (vector expansion), AMR, dense populations
  • Canine influenza — H3N8 (2004, equine origin) & H3N2 (2015, avian origin); near-100% attack in naive shelters; vaccine available
  • Sporothrix brasiliensis — cat-transmitted (bite / scratch) emerging zoonosis
  • SARS-CoV-2 — reverse zoonosis, cats > dogs, mink amplifier / spill-back
  • Mpox — reverse zoonosis documented (dog infected by mpox+ owners, Italy 2022); NASPHV: keep exposed animals home + monitored, consult state health/ag before collecting specimens+
  • AMR — MRSP (not MRSA) = dominant resistant staph in dogs · ESBL E. coli/Klebsiella · carbapenem-resistant organisms; shelters are AMR reservoirs → antimicrobial stewardship is a public-health duty
  • Vector-borne — Chagas (T. cruzi, kissing bug, southern-US hounds, cardiac arrhythmia/sudden death) · Leishmania infantum (sandfly, NE-US foxhound reservoir) · Heartland/Bourbon viruses (tick-borne, south-central/upper Midwest; dogs/cats seroconvert as tick-exposure sentinels, not direct transmitters)+
Trap CIV is not seasonal like human flu — it circulates year-round; MRSP ≠ MRSA (different epidemiology + zoonotic significance); mpox/Heartland/Bourbon are reverse-zoonosis or sentinel scenarios, not dog/cat-to-human transmitters.

Outbreak Response

  • Outbreak = cases in excess of expected baseline; frame with a case definition, index case, attack rate
  • Epi curves — point source = single sharp peak (1 incubation); propagated = successive peaks ~1 incubation apart; continuous source = plateau
  • Control bundle — isolate sick + cohort exposed + ± stop intake + emergency vaccinate (won't protect incubating) + directional flow (clean→dirty, healthy→sick)
  • Report a cluster even when single cases wouldn't trigger; notify state vet for novel / unexplained mortality
Trap Point source: peak − minimum incubation = exposure time. Know incubations — CPV 4–14 d, CDV 3–6 d, FPV 2–10 d, CIV 2–5 d.

Shelter's Role in Disease Surveillance

  • Sentinel node — shelters are One Health surveillance nodes at the animal-human-environment interface; passive surveillance under-reports (iceberg phenomenon)+
  • Passive (law-driven, cheap, under-reports) vs active (systematic case-seeking, complete, costly) vs syndromic (near-real-time, non-specific)
  • Line-list data (by unit / intake ZIP / cohort) + sample biobanking enable retrospective outbreak detection; fed CIV H3N2 (2015) & HPAI cat (2024) detection
Trap Cluster scenario answer is always all four: treat affected → infection control → report to public health → preserve docs / samples.
