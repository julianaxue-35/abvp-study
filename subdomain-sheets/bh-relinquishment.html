Canine Behaviour & Relinquishment

  • Leading reason — behaviour is one of the most-cited reasons for surrender (cited in up to ~48% of relinquishments).+
  • Denominator problem — a cited behaviour is not proven risk unless you know its prevalence in retained homes (~86% of kept dogs show ≥1 troublesome behaviour).+
  • Gift ≠ risk — being received as a gift is not a risk factor (2.9% of surrenders cited it vs 4.5% of owners received one).
  • Human reasons dominate — personal/moving cited up to 69.1%, outweighing behaviour; the mix shifts by geography, SES, and era.
  • Owner report ≠ prediction — a surrendering owner's behaviour account poorly predicts how the dog behaves in a new home.
  • Adopter draw — morphology decides who gets a look; play (fetch/tug) + staying near when invited → ↑ adoption; eye-contact / front-of-pen doesn't.
Exam trap Raw odds ratios / percentages mislead. Multivariate control (training, vet visits, sterilisation) roughly halved the OR for inappropriate elimination and made other behaviours non-significant — always ask which confounders were adjusted.

Four Windows for Behavioural Prevention

  • Four windows — before intake · at intake · at adoption · after adoption; owner-support / behaviour helplines reduce surrender, and the highest-yield moment is when an owner or adopter first weighs rehoming.+
  • Before intake — highest yield but least feasible: labour-intensive, hard to reach the at-risk owners.
  • Intake / adoption — temp foster cuts stress & improves kennel presentation; at adoption you can't reliably predict adopters' deal-breakers.
  • After adoption — support prevents returns; returned dogs were re-adopted faster than the general population.
  • Fearful dogs — home-like room + 15 min/day quiet contact over 2–3 days sharply ↑ passing the adoptability assessment.

Behavioural Hygiene in Kennels

  • Outing = the reinforcer — ignoring jumping fails because leaving the kennel (the outing), not handler attention, reinforces it.+
  • Leash pulling self-reinforces — pulling works whenever it moves the dog toward a goal; use a harness + let it move scent-to-scent to deplete the motivation.
  • Substrate memory — dogs associate elimination with prior substrate; outdoor space needs dirt + concrete and must clearly differ from the indoor area.
  • Worst behaviour = most urgent foster — strong, persistent problems are the top foster priority (interventions need time/space unavailable in-kennel).

Three Husbandry-Induced Problems

  • Barrier barking/lunging — negatively reinforced as the passing dog walks away → visual barriers + playgroups, not "ignore it".+
  • Indoor elimination — confinement habituates the dog, erasing house-training (loses aversion to soiling near food/bed); fix = protected outdoor space + walks after meals + lavish reward outside.
  • Jumping/mouthing — excited greeting reinforced by the outing itself; teach an alternative greeting — walker leaves 10–15 s, feet-off earns the leash; ignoring alone fails.

Feline Community Impact & Field Dynamics

  • Opportunistic predators — urban stalk–pounce–bite; ~73% of hunting dusk→dawn; least successful on birds (~17%).
  • Home-range gradient — near houses = small range / high density / crepuscular (often owned cats out by day); feral, unmanaged = largest range / nocturnal / lowest density; fed or TNR = moderate.
  • Feeder present — cats eat faster (~4 min vs ~12 s) → less pest attraction; use a fixed schedule + stay present.
  • Sterilisation — spraying stops/drops sharply, dominant cats less aggressive, colony hierarchy unchanged; "invasive" ≠ proven harm (doesn't justify lethal control).
Verify figures Notes' "62% killed, ~6 kills/24 h, 44% success" don't match the cited Loyd 2013 collar-cam study (~30% killed, ~2.1 kills/wk) — likely blended sources; treat as unconfirmed. Camera weight must be <2% of body weight.

Consequences of Lethal Control

  • Mesopredator release — remove cats → rats/mice/rabbits boom → can paradoxically worsen native-bird survival.
  • Vacuum effect — removal vacates territory while food/shelter remain → surrounding cats migrate in (compensatory migration).
  • Pulsed control fails — short-term/uncoordinated culls are ineffective; only sustained, long-term effort has benefited native species.
  • Cat removal ≠ more birds — bird survival didn't consistently improve; habitat (pasture vs semi-desert) modifies the effect.

Community Cats in the Shelter

  • FSA (Feline Spectrum Assessment) — places a cat on the socialisation spectrum; thriving community cats may not belong in a shelter.+
  • Keep thriving cats out — cats doing well, not endangered, low wildlife impact may not belong in a shelter; first step = review ordinances (many antiquated).
  • Reduce stress before FSA — even socialised cats arrive stressed/afraid; lower stress first, then assess.
  • Placement rules — offensively aggressive cats are not adoption candidates; route less-socialised cats to working/barn homes.
  • Stress tools — group housing, foster/office housing, hiding spots (a carrier suffices), foster-to-adopt.

The Tiny Spirit Kitten Programme

  • Adopt promptly — undersocialised kittens placed early (still a little hissy) rather than tying up foster homes; ~85% adopted successfully.+
  • Target — undersocialised kittens ~2 lb (0.9 kg), ~2 mo, defensively aggressive (hiss/spit); thrive in foster but regress in-shelter.
  • Housing — individual kennel, off-ground at eye level, in the busiest public area (stressful short-term but speeds habituation); hiding box ~2 in (5.1 cm) + toys.
  • First 48 h — doors locked except cleaning; toothbrush on tongue depressor 1 min ×10/day + burrito-wrap; after 48 h open to many handlers.
  • Decision rule — no progress at 48 h → re-evaluate; adopt within 2 wks OR sterilise + return; tell adopters "not for everyone," first human bond is theirs.

Behavioural Welfare in Shelters

  • ASV: behaviour = required care — not optional; provide social contact, mental stimulation, normal behaviours, and some environmental control.
  • Five Domains — nutrition · environment · health · behavioural interaction → feed the 5th (mental/affective state); promote positives (choice/agency/enrichment), not just avoid harm.
  • Enrichment cuts LOS — predictable routine, feline hiding spots, minimising FAS (fear/anxiety/stress) reduce stress & barrier behaviours that lengthen stay and lower adoptability.
