Legislation · Animals & Public Policy (7%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet — US federal + state/local animal law for the ABVP exam. Assumes shelter-vet baseline; clarified points marked +.

Animal Welfare Act (AWA)

  • Animal Welfare Act 1966 — primary US federal animal statute; enforced by USDA APHIS (US Dept of Agriculture, Animal & Plant Health Inspection Service); Class A breeder licence needs 4+ breeding females; does NOT cover shelters+
  • Covers: Class A dealers (breeders, 4+ females), Class B dealers (brokers, any count), research facilities, exhibitors (zoos/circuses), carriers
  • Excludes: pet owners, farm/food animals; rats, mice & birds bred for research (2002 amdt — ~95% of research animals); cold-blooded species
  • Sets minimum standards (space, sanitation, vet care), dog exercise, primate psychological well-being; APHIS unannounced annual inspections + 1-yr record-keeping
  • Violations → fines, licence revocation, or criminal charges; amended repeatedly (1970–2014)
Trap Enforcer is USDA APHIS — NOT AVMA, ASPCA or HSUS (advocacy orgs, no enforcement power).

PACT Act (2019)

  • PACT Act (Preventing Animal Cruelty and Torture) — first federal statute to directly criminalize companion-animal cruelty; max 7 years imprisonment; interstate-commerce trigger+
  • Prohibits crushing/burning/drowning/suffocating/impaling of mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians + sexual exploitation + crush videos
  • Precursor = 2010 Animal Crush Video Prohibition Act (banned depictions only, not the acts); PACT closed that gap
  • Supplements — does not preempt — state cruelty law; both can apply. Exempt: vet/husbandry, hunting/trapping, pest control, research, agriculture
  • Commerce-Clause grounded: posting cruelty online or moving animals across state lines establishes federal jurisdiction; shelter vets document injuries + may testify
Trap Max is 7 years, not life; routine vet procedures (e.g. spay/neuter) are always exempt.

Federal Coverage Gap: Shelters, Adoption & Stray Management

  • No federal law governs shelter intake, stray hold periods, euthanasia, or adoption — all set at state/local level+
  • Also none for: live-release rules, foster standards, spay/neuter mandates, shelter oversight — all state/local
  • Standards vary wildly (e.g. 72-hr hold in one county vs 10 days next state vs none) — structural fact of US animal law
  • Why NACA/HSUS model ordinances + state-level advocacy carry the load for raising shelter standards

Breed-Specific Legislation (BSL)

  • Breed-specific legislation is discouraged / ineffective — AVMA (American Veterinary Medical Assoc.) & ASV (Assoc. of Shelter Veterinarians) oppose; breed ID unreliable; behaviour-based laws preferred+
  • Usual targets: pit bull–type dogs, Rottweilers, German Shepherds, Dobermans. Types: full ban (grandfather/surrender/euthanize) · restriction (muzzle, insurance, sterilize, register) · rental housing bans
  • Anti-BSL rationale: no proven bite reduction, unreliable breed ID, behaviour history is the better predictor, behaviour-based dangerous-dog laws more enforceable
  • 20+ states preempt local BSL (TX, FL, IL, PA); where no preemption, cities may still enact it
Trap Breed ID is unreliable even by trained pros vs DNA — strongest argument against BSL: the target population can't be defined.

Retail Pet Sale Bans

  • California (2019) first statewide retail pet-sale ban; part of the broader mandatory-spay/neuter debate over reducing shelter intake+
  • Stores may only source dogs/cats/rabbits from shelters or rescues — cuts the commercial mass-breeding pipeline
  • 400+ cities/counties have local ordinances; IL, MD, NY have since gone statewide
Trap California (AB 485) was first state (2019) — many cities banned earlier; don't confuse "first city" with "first state."

TNR and Community Cat Ordinances

  • TNR (trap-neuter-return) legal status varies by jurisdiction: formal municipal program · default impound (no ordinance) · explicit prohibition (feeding/return banned) · permissive/informal
  • No federal law; AVMA & ASV support TNR as one part of community-cat management; some states require rabies vaccination on return
  • Formal programs register colony managers + shelter support; without an ordinance, free-roamers are impounded and held as strays
  • Wildlife/bird-predation concerns create tension in some jurisdictions
Trap Returning cats to field where TNR is not authorized may legally count as abandonment.

Anti-Tethering Laws

  • No federal standard — state/local variation; restrict chaining/tying-out of dogs
  • Common provisions: min tether length (often 10–15 ft (3–4.6 m) or 3× body length); no heavy/logging chains; no tethering in severe weather; shade/water/shelter required
  • Some states ban continuous tethering as the primary means of confinement

State vs Local Preemption

  • Anti-cruelty statutes are state law; preemption cuts both ways — can block local BSL or block stronger local protections+
  • State preempts local BSL → cities can't ban breeds (protects owners from patchwork bans)
  • State preempts stronger local rules → cities can't exceed the state baseline (blocks local innovation)
  • No preemption → locals may enact anything stricter/different not barred by state law
Trap Direction matters — preemption can protect animals (stopping BSL) OR block stronger local protections; context decides which way it cuts.

Model Ordinances

  • Advisory templates only — not binding law; jurisdictions adopt or adapt
  • NACA (Natl Animal Care & Control Assoc.) — animal control, dangerous-animal declarations, shelter operations
  • HSUS (Humane Society of the US) — retail-sale bans, anti-tethering, cat management, TNR
  • Common elements: licensing/vaccination, bite reporting, dangerous-animal procedures, stray holds, shelter standards
  • Fill the vacuum left by the federal gap — main vehicle for standardizing hold periods + shelter ops locally
