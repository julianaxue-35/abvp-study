Body Language

Body Language · Behavioral Health (16%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet for the Body Language subdomain — canine & feline developmental windows, signalling channels, and emotional-state clusters. Added / clarified points marked +.

Canine Development

  • Socialisation window — 3 to 14 wk (peak 6 to 8 wk); first fear period 8 to 10 wk; a pup not socialised in-window is socially handicapped for life and can become neophobic+
  • Neonatal (0–2 wk) — 3-min daily handling with mild stressors → lifelong HPA-axis resilience & easier learning
  • Learned ONLY in the window — bite inhibition, care-soliciting, primary social attachment; LGD pup raised with sheep bonds to sheep for life
  • Predatory motor sequence (eye → stalk → chase → bite) ~7 mo, as early as 8 wk in Border Collies; socially mature ~18 mo, fully ~2 yr
  • AVSAB rule — socialising before 12 wk OUTWEIGHS finishing the vaccine series; well-run puppy classes advised pre-full-vaccination

Canine Communication and Body Language

  • Whale eye — sclera visible = discomfort / nervousness; hard eye (direct, prolonged stare + dilated pupils) = threat / arousal, activates the SNS (sympathetic nervous system)+
  • Calming / appeasement signals — lip licks, yawns, head turn, submissive grin (squinty eyes, smooth forehead, ears back) = de-escalation, NOT aggression+
  • Tail lateralisation — wags to dog's RIGHT = positive valence (left hemisphere); LEFT = negative / withdrawal (right hemisphere)
  • Piloerection / hackles = AROUSAL only, not a fixed emotion — occurs in play, conflict OR fear
  • Commissure — long-lip pulled back = fear / appeasement; short-lip pushed forward (C-mouth, wrinkled muzzle) = aggression; read the ear BASE, not the flap
Trap Submissive grin = teeth + SQUINTY eyes, smooth forehead, ears back. Offensive pucker = teeth + HARD eyes, wrinkled muzzle, C-mouth. Eyes + muzzle settle it.

Emotional State Clusters

  • Ladder of aggression — escalation runs appeasement / calming → avoidance → freeze / stiffen → growl / snap → bite; punishing the early rungs removes the warning. Offensive (lean forward, hard eye, raised tail) vs defensive (pulled back, ears back, tail tucked)+
  • Read CLUSTERS, never single signs — a wag alone is not "friendly"
  • Arousal cluster — piloerection + tail high & fast (often left-biased) + tight mouth + panting with locked hard eyes
  • Alert — ears forward, tail down but NOT tucked, prolonged focus; fear — low quick-wag tail, long-lip, whale eye, ears back, cowering

Feline Development

  • Socialisation window closes at ~7 wk — feline window is 2 to 7 wk (earlier than the dog); handling by 7 wk is critical for a cat to enjoy human interaction later+
  • SIRE'S personality = strong independent predictor — friendly toms sire bolder, more affiliative kittens (paternal, not just the queen)
  • Prenatal protein / calorie restriction → emotionally reactive kittens, poor social interaction, exploration & learning
  • Hand-reared from 2 wk (off queen & littermates) → fearful / aggressive, poor social & parenting skills, poorer learning
  • Handled >5 min/day (shelter kittens) → approach novel toys & unfamiliar people; full motor coordination by 7 wk

Feline Communication and Body Language

  • Purr ≠ only contentment — care-soliciting at the nipple, in friendly cats AND in mildly anxious / ill cats (dual meaning matters clinically)
  • Cats vocalise far MORE to humans than to cats — chirr / trill = closed-mouth friendly greeting; allorubbing / allogrooming / preferred associates = affiliative
  • Agonistic sounds are OPEN-mouth — hiss, spit, growl, snarl, yowl, shriek
Trap Marking = VERTICAL surface, standing, quivering tail, little / no scratching. Normal voiding = HORIZONTAL squat with substrate scratching afterwards.

Visual Signals: Tail Positions

  • Tail vertical up = amicable / approach; wrapped around a sitting body = "polite decline" (not aggressive, not keen); stiff / lashing = aggression; tucked = fear+
  • Distress face — dilated pupils, frequent blink, ears lowered / flattened, whiskers pushed FORWARD, often hissing
  • Anxious / hypervigilant = sitting sternal, tail wrapped tight; relaxed = lying lateral, neutral eyes / pupils / ears
  • Piloerection + arched / upright body = strong arousal regardless of tail position

Acoustic & Olfactory Signalling

  • Canine bark pitch — low / harsh / low-amplitude = aggression (low freq = distance-increasing); high-pitched = fear / desperation
  • Barks are short-range & context-dependent (also growl, whine, yelp, howl)
  • Vomeronasal organ (VNO) — below the nasal cavity; detects pheromones in urine / faeces / saliva, sampled via head or anogenital → olfactory bulb

Modified-Signal Breeds

  • Brachycephalic (Bulldog, Pug, Boxer, Frenchie) — muzzle / lip compressed → grin vs pucker unreliable; read eyes, ear base, weight distribution
  • Docked tail (Doberman, Boxer, Rottweiler) — tail height / lateralisation / wag lost; docked dogs receive MORE aggressive approaches (loss is real & bidirectional); read posture, ears, piloerection
  • Cropped ears (Doberman, Cane Corso) — fixed erect, looks alert / threatening regardless of true state; read forehead, eyes, posture
  • Scenario Qs — privilege intact channels (eyes, weight, posture) over the absent / reduced one

Pain-Related Behavioural Indicators

  • Pain mimics behaviour problems — new-onset aggression / snapping → pain workup BEFORE a behavioural diagnosis; look for orbital tightening / squint, ear & whisker changes on the grimace scales (Feline / Canine Grimace Scale)+
  • Cat pain EAR = rotated OUTWARD & slightly lowered — distinct from the fully flattened FEAR ear
  • Cat pain WHISKERS = pulled flat / rigid back toward the cheeks (Feline Grimace Scale)
  • Guarded / hunched posture (abdominal / spinal pain) + reluctance to move; touching the guarded area → withdrawal or snap; self-licking one site = local pain
