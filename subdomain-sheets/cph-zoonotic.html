Zoonotic Disease

Zoonotic Disease · Community & Public Health (11%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet — key shelter zoonoses (bacterial · parasitic · fungal), immunocompromised-owner precautions, bite-wound rules & the big exam traps. Added / clarified points marked +.

Overview & Classification Framework

  • Definitions & transmission categories — zoonosis · anthroponosis · reservoir / amplifying / dead-end host; routes: direct & bite/scratch, fecal-oral, aerosol, vector-borne, environmental
  • High-risk / immunocompromised owners — HIV/AIDS, transplant, chemo, pregnancy, <5 yr, >65 yr at disproportionate risk; counsel on Toxoplasma, Cryptosporidium, Bartonella, Salmonella, ringworm +
  • Classify by transmission ROUTE (direct/bite · fecal-oral · aerosol · vector · environmental) — route sets the PPE & protocol, not agent type
  • ~60% of human infections are zoonotic; ~75% of emerging human pathogens are animal-origin
  • Reservoir ≠ human-exposure source; dead-end host (humans for most arboviruses) doesn't onward-transmit

Bacterial Zoonoses in the Shelter

  • Salmonellosis · Campylobacteriosis (fecal-oral; raw diets, puppies/kittens)
  • Cat scratch disease (CSD; Bartonella henselae) — cat flea is the essential vector → flea control is the single most important prevention; not spread by saliva/bite alone; in IC hosts → disseminated bacillary angiomatosis (life-threatening) +
  • Pasteurellosis & Capnocytophaga (bite wounds; asplenic risk)
  • Q fever (Coxiella burnetii) — reservoir ruminants + cats, shed in birth fluids/milk/urine/feces; spore-like form extremely stable (survives dust mo–yrs, resistant to heat/drying/most disinfectants); ~60% asymptomatic; symptomatic = fever, headache, myalgia, pneumonia, hepatitis; chronic = endocarditis +
  • Psittacosis (Chlamydophila psittaci) — reservoir psittacines/pigeons/raptors (can look healthy) → inhaled feather dust/dried faeces (±bite); atypical pneumonia in humans; doxycycline treats BOTH bird & human; nationally notifiable +
  • Tularemia (Francisella tularensis, "rabbit fever") — reservoir rabbits/hares/rodents, ticks = vector; highly infectious (tiny inoculum); cat hunts rodent → bite/scratch transmits to handler; bioterrorism agent; same policy as plague — do NOT treat/adopt, REPORT +
  • Salmonella / Campy — raw diets, puppies/kittens, reptiles (reptiles ~74k US cases/yr) → high-risk persons avoid reptiles, chicks, ducklings
  • Capnocytophaga — asplenic/alcoholic → fulminant sepsis/DIC (~30% fatal); even a lick on broken skin
  • Q fever (Coxiella) — AEROSOL from parturient queen's birth fluids (N95, no bite needed)
  • Plague / tularemia — via rodent-hunting cats (cat pneumonic plague = aerosol) → do NOT treat/adopt, REPORT
Trap Bite wounds: NOT first-gen cephalosporins (cephalexin/cefazolin) — poor Pasteurella cover; use amoxicillin-clavulanate.

Leptospirosis

  • Spirochete shed in urine — indirect transmission via urine-contaminated water/soil; wear gloves when handling urine + hand hygiene; zoonotic & reportable in many jurisdictions; doxycycline +
  • Reservoir — rodents (Norway rat) maintenance host; unvaccinated outdoor dogs shed; flood/hoarding intakes high-risk
  • Weil's disease — jaundice + acute renal failure + pulmonary haemorrhage; 5–15% CFR in severe cases
  • Dog 4-serovar leptospiral bacterin recommended in endemic areas
  • Dx — MAT (microscopic agglutination test) is the reference test; single titre can't separate infection from vaccination → need a 4-fold rise on paired acute/convalescent titres. PCR useful early (blood, leptospiraemic phase) before urinary shedding starts. Incubation ~5–14d human / ~7d dog +
  • Environmental survival — weeks in warm, moist, neutral–alkaline conditions (mud, stagnant water); killed by desiccation, UV/sunlight, standard disinfectants (bleach, quats) +
Trap Cats are relatively RESISTANT — not a meaningful occupational lepto source; risk is dogs + rodents.

Canine Brucellosis — Brucella canis

  • Transmission — reproductive fluids / aborted material; intact & breeding dogs, commercial-breeder intake
  • Human disease & shelter policy
  • Import risk (Mexico, C/S America, E Europe, Asia); test late-term abortion, male repro failure, or discospondylitis
  • Confirmed case — euthanasia often recommended: intracellular, not cleared by tx, lifelong intermittent carrier; spay/neuter cuts but doesn't stop shedding
  • NOT airborne — needs contact with repro secretions; notify lab before culture (biohazard)
  • Zoonotic (milder than B. melitensis/abortus); exposed staff → occ-health serology at 4–6 wk
Trap RSAT = high false-positive screen; confirm with AGID/PCR before any euthanasia decision — never cull on RSAT alone.

Parasitic Zoonoses in the Shelter

  • Toxoplasmosis — risk to pregnant women is from PRIMARY infection during pregnancy, not reactivation; oocysts need ~1–5 days to sporulate → daily litter change + gloves; also spread via undercooked meat (commonest route) & organ transplant/transfusion, not just cat faeces; congenital infection → chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus, intracranial calcifications, developmental delay; IC reactivation = toxoplasmic encephalitis, #1 cause of brain abscess in HIV/AIDS (CD4<100) +
  • Toxocariasis (visceral / ocular larva migrans, VLM / OLM)
  • Hookworm (cutaneous larva migrans, CLM)
  • Cryptosporidiosis · Giardiasis
  • Toxocara — eggs need 2–4 wk to embryonate, survive soil for years; seroprevalence ~14–30%; VLM mainly 2–7yo pica kids (fever, cough, hepatomegaly, eosinophilia) / OLM (blindness)
  • Hookworm CLM — L3 skin penetration (barefoot on sand); serpiginous creeping eruption; self-limiting
  • Crypto — oocysts immediately infectious & chlorine-RESISTANT → use AHP/steam; soap-water not sanitiser
  • Giardia — dog/cat assemblages (C/D/F) mostly NOT zoonotic; human risk mainly water & assemblage A
Trap Cat is the ONLY definitive host (dogs shed NO oocysts); fetal risk only from PRIMARY maternal infection in pregnancy — seropositive-before = protected.

Fungal & Other Zoonoses

  • Dermatophytosis (ringworm) — Microsporum canis; direct contact + fomite/spore; children & immunocompromised at risk; high shelter-outbreak potential +
  • Sporotrichosis
  • Ringworm = #1 zoonosis BY VOLUME in shelters; M. canis spores persist 12–18 mo; up to 20–30% cats asymptomatic carriers
  • Dx: DTM culture is gold standard; tx = lime-sulfur dip + systemic itraconazole/terbinafine (combo > either alone)
  • Sporotrichosis — Sporothrix schenckii, dimorphic (mold in environment, yeast in tissue); traumatic inoculation (thorns) OR cat scratch/bite (Brazil epidemic); infected cats → ulcerative nodular skin lesions ± nasal involvement; human = lymphocutaneous form (nodule + ascending lymphangitic chain); itraconazole first-line both species +
Trap Wood's lamp catches only ~50% of M. canis — a negative does NOT rule out ringworm; culture required.

Viral Zoonoses & Anthroponoses

  • H5N1 (2024–25) — raw milk from infected dairy cows → fatal neuro disease in cats; never feed raw dairy; N95 + report; cat→human transmission RARE
  • Monkeypox (Mpox) — prairie dogs & small exotic rodents implicated in 2003 US outbreak (via infected Gambian pouched rats at a pet distributor) → shelters taking exotic rodents should be aware +
  • Anthroponoses (reverse zoonoses, human→animal) — MRSA (bidirectional, fomites/direct contact — screening NOT routine, reserve for outbreak/cluster investigation); M. tuberculosis (owner→dog, classic teaching example); C. difficile (survives dry surfaces days–wks, fomite spread); Group A Strep/S. pneumoniae (human→dog/cat); SARS-CoV-2 (→cats, dogs, mink) → staff with active infection limit close animal contact +

Risk Reduction & Occupational Health

  • Hierarchy of controls (elimination → substitution → engineering → administrative → PPE)
  • Written zoonosis risk program (ASV = Association of Shelter Veterinarians Standards of Care)
  • Bite / scratch first aid — immediate copious washing + wound care; assess rabies exposure (see Rabies subdomain); amoxicillin-clavulanate first-line, NOT first-gen cephalosporins +
  • Adoption counseling · PPE guide (PPE = personal protective equipment)
  • Release/adoption decision — 3-factor test: (1) risk of transmission animal→caregiver, (2) risk disease actually develops if exposed, (3) risk of a fatal outcome if it does → low on all three = release with disclosure, not automatic exclusion +
  • Hand hygiene (soap + water) = single most impactful control; sanitiser weak vs oocysts/non-enveloped agents
  • Bite first aid — pressure-irrigate punctures with 20-mL syringe + 18-G needle; >8 h delay = usually already infected; 2–30% get infected
  • High-risk adopters AVOID — <6 mo, active diarrhoea, fleas/ticks, long nails, aggressive → pick a different pet, don't deny adoption
  • Reporting — unusual/high-consequence zoonoses → state public health vet (animal side) + NNDSS (CDC's National Notifiable Disease Surveillance System, human side, fed by state/local health depts) +
  • Necropsy = high-risk procedure — non-essential personnel excluded +
  • Needlestick/sharps — never recap by hand (one-handed scoop method if unavoidable); sharps container mandatory; Needlestick Safety & Prevention Act (2000) cut hospital percutaneous injuries 38% in yr 1 +
Trap Two testable bite specifics: 20-mL syringe + 18-G needle for punctures, and the 8-hour rule for established infection.
