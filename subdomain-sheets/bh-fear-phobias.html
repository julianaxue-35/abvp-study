Fear, Phobias & Anxiety

Fear, Phobias & Anxiety · Behavioral Health (16%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet for Fear, Phobias & Anxiety — the anxiety–fear–phobia continuum, fear neuroanatomy, learning principles, the behaviour-modification toolkit, and supporting pharmacology. Clarified / added points marked +.

Definitions: Anxiety, Fear & Phobia

  • Fear = response to a PRESENT, identifiable stimulus · Anxiety = apprehension of an ANTICIPATED / future threat (may have no identifiable stimulus) · Phobia = excessive, out-of-proportion fear that persists or INTENSIFIES with exposure+
  • Adaptive value: fear can be NORMAL (judged by magnitude + context); anxiety maladaptive when chronic; phobia ALWAYS maladaptive.
  • Phobia components: panic attacks, hysteria, catatonia — profound & out of proportion to any real threat.
  • Freeze / behavioural shutdown (rigid, panting, dilated pupils, tucked tail) is a FEAR state — often misread as "calm" or "stubborn".
Trap · anticipated vs present Scanning in a quiet home with no trigger = anxious · cowering when a truck appears = fearful · hysteria at a rustling bag = phobic.

Neuroanatomy of the Fear Response

  • Amygdala = primary fear-processing site (via thalamus; fires before the cortex checks whether the threat is real). Chronic cortisol damages the hippocampus, removing its brake on the HPA (hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal) axis → vicious cycle+
  • Amygdala = central fear-CONDITIONING site; signals hypothalamic PVN → CRF → HPA axis + NE release from the locus ceruleus.
  • Locus ceruleus (pons) = highest NE concentration in brain; dysregulation → panic. Raphe nuclei supply ~all forebrain serotonin; intact forebrain needed to UNLEARN fear (extinction).
  • Tx targets: serotonin (raphe) · noradrenaline (locus ceruleus) · GABA → SSRI · TCA · alpha-2 · benzodiazepine.

Determinants of Fear & Learning Principles

  • One-event learning — a single intensely aversive event can create lasting fear. Kindling — repeated sub-threshold exposure lowers the threshold, so unmanaged exposure makes the next event WORSE, not better+
  • Determinants: genetic (species predator triggers, temperament) + environmental (poor socialisation, trauma) + learned (fear display "works" = negative reinforcement).
  • Discrimination = fear stays specific vs generalisation = fear of one child → all children.
  • High arousal → responses become AUTOMATIC, no conscious choice → only train in a calm state BELOW threshold.
  • Prognosis — good: short duration, socialised, fear WITHOUT aggression · poor: strong genetics, early onset, multiple concurrent phobias.

Behavioural Modification

  • Never punish a fearful display — fear is an emotional state; punishment adds to it, worsens the underlying problem, and raises defensive-aggression risk+
  • Two principles: (1) AVOID uncontrolled triggers outside sessions (each exposure kindles); (2) controlled SUBTHRESHOLD exposure paired with a positive outcome.
  • Owner's own frustration or upset emotional state alone aggravates the fear — stay neutral / calm.
  • Comforting a panicking dog does NOT reinforce the fear — fear is an involuntary emotion, not an operant behaviour.

The Six Core Techniques

  • Desensitisation + counterconditioning (DS = graded sub-threshold exposure; CC = pair the stimulus with a high-value reward) are the paired mainstay — keep the pet SUBTHRESHOLD throughout+
  • The six: controlled exposure · habituation · systematic desensitisation · counterconditioning · response substitution · shaping.
  • Response substitution = train a calm behaviour physically INCOMPATIBLE with fear (mat, sit-stay, watch).
  • Threshold rule: any fear sign / refused reward → drop to previous level, allow full recovery, resume lower. Grade by distance · volume · familiarity · location.

Controlled Flooding: Narrow Indication

  • Flooding is contraindicated for most fear / phobia — forced over-threshold exposure SENSITISES and worsens fear; only rarely appropriate (stimulus not a true threat, owner stays calm, pet restrained then rewarded until it habituates)+
  • Rare OK only if: stimulus is not a true threat + owner stays calm + pet restrained then REWARDED once it habituates.
  • If fear INCREASES it becomes a welfare problem and further sensitises → abort.
Trap · flooding ≠ desensitisation Flooding = forced OVER-threshold exposure — the OPPOSITE of desensitisation (stays subthreshold). Not for a true phobia.

Noise & Storm Phobia Protocol

  • 90 / 75 asymmetry — ~90% of STORM-phobic dogs also have noise phobia, but only ~75% of NOISE-phobic dogs have storm phobia. Noise phobia affects 40–50% of dogs+
  • Storm = multifactorial (barometric pressure, ionisation, lightning, rain, noise) → harder to treat, not owner-controlled.
  • Protective: exposure to loud voices before 6 mo · Risk: SOCIAL TRANSMISSION (copied from other fearful dogs).
  • Management layers: safe retreat space (never forced) · sight/sound barriers (mask with beat-heavy music) · "storm party" distraction · NEVER punish.
  • DS/CC protocol: teach a SETTLE cue first (can't train a panicking dog) → subthreshold noise recording + reserved high-value reward → raise volume only if relaxed.

Pharmacology of Fear, Phobia & Anxiety

  • Two tempos: daily MAINTENANCE (SSRI / TCA / MAOI) onset 2–4 wk, max effect up to 8 wk · EVENT drugs given 1–2 h PRE-trigger.
  • Maintenance: SSRI (fluoxetine) first-line chronic / generalised anxiety · clomipramine = most SSRI-like TCA, best for compulsive / stereotypy.
  • Event: benzodiazepine (acute panic) — but DISINHIBITION may unmask aggression + inhibits learning · also clonidine, trazodone, propranolol.
  • Acepromazine CONTRAINDICATED as monotherapy — immobilises without ANXIOLYSIS (terrified but frozen) + heightens noise sensitivity.
  • If effective, continue ≥6 mo (often lifelong); taper GRADUALLY over months — abrupt stop → relapse. (full doses live in the Behaviour Medications subdomain)

Safe Combination Rules

  • TCA + fluoxetine → cut TCA dose 10–25% · TCA + sertraline → cut TCA 50% (P450 inhibition raises TCA serum levels).
  • MAOI (selegiline) + SSRI or TCA = CONTRAINDICATED — uncontrolled serotonin accumulation, can be fatal.
  • Fluoxetine → MAOI switch needs a 5–6 wk washout (long-lived active metabolite norfluoxetine).
Trap · serotonin syndrome Tremors + HYPERTHERMIA + muscle rigidity + mental-status change + DIARRHOEA (neuromuscular + autonomic + cognitive).
