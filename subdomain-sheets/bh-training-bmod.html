Training, BMOD & Playgroups

Training, BMOD & Playgroups · Behavioral Health (16%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield summary of the Training, Behaviour Modification (BMOD) & Playgroups subdomain — exam numbers, decision rules & traps; pre-filled points marked +.

Training & Adoptability

  • Sit-at-front-of-kennel — ~1.4× more likely to be adopted, but no length-of-stay (LOS) effect.+
  • Target behaviours: name response, eye contact, hand targeting, four-on-floor, go-to-mat, loose-leash, leave/drop it.
  • Adopters favour dogs that come to front, sit, interact & don't bark.
  • Tiered volunteers: low = socialisation, sit/down; high = agility, nosework, cooperative care, challenging dogs.
  • Sessions 3–5 min, 2–3×/day; consistency (same cue + schedule across all staff) = top implementation variable. Pre + post-adoption counseling → 27.5% more adoptions.
  • Standardised curriculum's 6 priority behaviours are chosen for evidence on adoption, returns & kennel welfare; never push a jumping dog down (inadvertently reinforces).

Reward-Based Training & Reinforcers

  • Reward-based only — positive reinforcement; punishment / aversives prohibited. Food out-performs praise / petting (an ineffective reinforcer in shelters).+
  • Aversives → kennel avoidance + ↑ reactivity → ↓ adoption; positive reinforcement only in standardised curricula.
  • Human interaction (socialisation, play, obedience) clearly beneficial; focus effort on older adolescents & adults, not young pups.
Trap Praise & petting are an ineffective primary reinforcer for shelter dogs — food rewards out-perform them.

Behaviour Modification (BMOD)

  • Return rate — ~1% for BMOD (behaviour modification) dogs vs ~16% general return rate.+
  • Bite-history rehab: DSCC to triggers + safety/warning cues + alternative behaviours + bite inhibition; 300 dogs, 87% success (adopted, no re-bite), LOS 1–3 yrs.
  • Intraspecific aggression: 20–49% of dogs (43 Canadian shelters); BMOD = calm leash-walking + handler eye contact near other dogs.
  • Excessive arousal ↑ LOS, ↓ adoption: hyper-responsive, ↓ inhibition; kennel pacing/spinning/barking, out-of-kennel jumping/mouthing/grabbing.
  • Adopter concerns peak in the first weeks, rarely persist past 3 months; few regret adopting behaviourally-rehabilitated dogs. Screen adopters + offer post-adoption support.

Fear in Shelter Dogs

  • DSCC (desensitisation & counter-conditioning) — gradual exposure below threshold paired with something positive; flooding risks learned helplessness.+
  • Social + non-social fear: handling, people, dogs, isolation, novelty (neophobia); signs = tremble, freeze, hide, escape, defensive aggression.
  • ASPCA BRC (Behavioral Rehabilitation Center): specialised kennels, designated low-stress handlers, daily enrichment, standardised BMOD, fluoxetine + gabapentin.
  • BRC: 66 fearful dogs → 36% met adoptability, 44% improved but needed more work. 4 targets: people, handling, leashing/walking, novelty.

Playgroup Models: DPFL vs SPA

  • Purpose — playgroups for socialisation, enrichment & real-time behavioural assessment. Two models: DPFL (Dogs Playing for Life) & SPA (Shelter Playgroup Alliance).+
  • DPFL: large groups (6+), hands-off; aversive tools (airhorns, break sticks, spray bottles, shake cans). Efficient but higher conflict/flooding risk.
  • SPA: LIMA (least intrusive, minimally aversive) — safety/choice/control/consent; low-risk 2–3 dogs @2:1, high-risk 4–6 @3:1; treats, muzzles, longlines. High staff demand.
  • ASV: limit group size to personnel skill/training, play-yard size, individual dog behaviour & shelter resources.
Trap: cortisol & aversives Post-playgroup salivary cortisol rose only in small groups using aversives; both large-group-with-aversives and small-group-with-treats/praise showed decreased cortisol.

Candidate Selection & Vet Clearance

  • Vet clearance required for all candidates — play is species-typical but not universally safe or reinforcing.
  • Consent / choice / preference tests confirm willingness; ~50% of shelter dogs participate (higher in low-intake).
  • Helper dogs OK but rotate to avoid fatigue, stress & injury; puppies >20 wks (fully vaccinated) may join, puppy-only pairs supervised.
  • Neuter status, age, weight, size & sex also inform group composition.
Trap On-leash reactivity & barrier reactivity do NOT predict off-leash aggression or sociability.

Play & Conflict Signs

  • Healthy play = simultaneous, reciprocal affiliation with ≥1 of: self-handicapping, inhibited biting, play wrestling, play chasing, exaggerated low-intensity predatory.
  • Reliable play signal = play bow with a distinct pause. Self-handicapping = stronger dog voluntarily handicaps to sustain play.
  • True conflict: tucked/raised stiff tail, full-body hackles + stiff body, fixed stare, low growls, lunging, bite attempts.
  • Play wrestling = highest risk of escalating to conflict. Risk ↑ with >2 dogs, but no correlation between avg group size (~8) and fights/injuries. Mounting = displacement (frustration), not necessarily sexual.
  • One dog shouldn't play at another's expense — watch cowering, hiding, trembling or repeated displacing; redirect both dogs simultaneously.
Trap: misread signs Piloerection, showing teeth & standing-over can be play; barking, bouncing, ears-forward & tail-wag can be arousal/conflict.

Contraindications & Biosecurity

  • Exclude / suspend — dogs with contagious illness, injury or pain; all candidates need veterinary clearance; suspend or modify playgroups during outbreaks.+
  • Playgroup disease prevalence: CPV <1%, distemper 0%, CIV 1%, CIRDC 19%.
  • Psychotropics, steroids & analgesics alter behaviour → factor into group composition.
  • Gear: flat washable quick-release collars (no buckles), basket muzzles, 6-ft leash / biothane longlines; toys & treats may trigger resource guarding.
  • Yard: high secure fencing, connected zones with separate entry & exit pens + two-way gates, elevated platforms, rest/avoidance areas, regular inspection.
Trap Gentle Leader headcollars are NOT recommended for playgroups.

Documentation & Evaluation (SPIDER)

  • SPIDER = Setting goals, Planning, Implementation, Documentation, Evaluation, Readjustment.
  • Conflict/fight management plan must be prepared & articulated to all staff before playgroups start.
  • Monitor goals, behavioural dynamics & incidents each session; surveillance cameras replay fights to ID antecedents/consequences, voice-to-text apps aid real-time notes.
  • Handlers: praise + petting aid play, but bowing/lunging/clapping/chasing evokes human-dog play → stay neutral when assessing conspecific sociability.
