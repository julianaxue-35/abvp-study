Regulatory

Regulatory · Shelter Management (18%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield summary of the Regulatory (Shelter Management) subdomain — practice acts, stray hold law, DEA controlled substances, zoonotic reporting. Source: Shelter Medicine for Veterinarians and Staff, 2nd Ed., Ch. 6. Clarified points marked +.

Practice Acts and Supervision

  • State practice act — sole legal authority for delegating veterinary tasks; internal SOPs and board policies cannot expand it+
  • State-specific — legal delegation in one state may be a criminal violation in another
  • Shelter exemptions — some acts let out-of-state licensed vets practise in registered shelters (written into law to attract staff)
  • Delegable where act allows — microchipping, core SQ vaccines by trained non-licensed staff under defined supervision

Supervision Level Definitions

  • Rabies — must be given by a licensed veterinarian; core vaccines (DHPP, FVRCP) may be delegable under indirect supervision where a shelter exemption exists+
  • Direct — vet physically on premises; indirect — vet off-site with prior instructions, immediately reachable by phone
  • Strays — direct supervision is the best-practice standard (ownership unsettled), even where indirect would be legal for an owned animal
  • Rabies never delegable — regardless of any other shelter exemption that applies

Internal SOPs Cannot Supersede State Law

  • Hierarchy — statutes and vet board regs always override internal SOPs and board-approved protocols
  • Not a defense — "our SOP permitted it" is not a valid legal defense at prosecution
  • Void — an SOP that expands authority beyond the practice act is legally void however carefully written
Trap A non-licensed assistant performing surgery under an internal SOP has no defense — the practice act governs, not the SOP.

Stray Hold Laws and Ownership

  • ID present — microchip or rabies tag triggers a minimum 7-day hold; shelter is bailee (custodian, not owner) — no elective surgery during hold+
  • Standard hold — 3–7 days per local ordinance; ID overrides to min 7 days even in a 3-day jurisdiction
  • During hold — stray is the unknown owner's property; shelter has no authority to permanently alter it
  • Title transfer — hold expiry = automatic statutory transfer of title (no paperwork or court action)
  • Private-shelter rule — handling strays where there is no municipal hold procedure = own legal risk; route strays through a government shelter or contract to settle ownership
Trap The 7-day hold is triggered by ID markers (chip/rabies tag), NOT the municipal hold period. Intake-record errors (wrong chip #, markings) → wrongful adoption + property-rights liability.

Permissible & Prohibited Treatments During Hold

  • Permitted vs prohibited — OK: core vaccines, deworming, parasite tx, pain control, emergency/life-saving surgery. Not OK: elective spay/neuter or irreversible alteration+
  • Emergency test — needed NOW to prevent death or severe suffering? Yes → proceed; No → wait for hold expiry
  • Retained authority — triage, stabilising surgery, or humane euthanasia allowed during hold when immediately required
Trap A non-painful necrotic tail tip on a stable stray is NOT an emergency → palliate (pain control, wound care), delay amputation until hold expires.

DEA Regulations and Controlled Substances

  • DEA registration (Drug Enforcement Administration) — individual and location-specific, non-transferable; all use ceases at that location the day the registered vet departs (no grace period)+
  • Cannot loan number — vet cannot sign over the DEA number; final responsibility for CS disposition stays with the vet
  • No staff vet — DEA may issue a direct facility procurement permit (facility cert + certified euthanasia techs + defined methods)
  • Scope — DEA is sole federal authority over pentobarbital and chemical immobilisation agents

Off-Site Shipping: Federal Violation

  • The violation — an off-site vet ordering euthanasia solution shipped to the shelter's address for the shelter's own use
  • Ship-to rule — all CS must ship to and be stored at the exact address on the vet's DEA license
  • No transfer — an off-site vet cannot hand euthanasia solution to a facility for its independent use

Storage, Quality Control & Permit Suspension

  • Controlled-drug storage — securely locked, substantially constructed cabinet or safe, permanently anchored to the building; monitor everyone with access+
  • Not acceptable — unlocked cabinet, personal vehicle, or open shelving
  • QC scope — covers everyone with access, including board members, vets, and staff outside the usual hierarchy
  • Permit suspension — a CS investigation lets the agency temporarily suspend permits; those operations must cease pending outcome

Mandatory Zoonotic Disease Reporting

  • Report to public health — state/local public health department, not the vet board or DEA; rabies-suspect plus human exposure = immediate / within-24-hour report+
  • State-level — each state sets its own reportable list; attending vet is primary reporter (director/ACO co-report in some states)
  • Timelines — rabies immediate + PEP coordination; Brucella canis needle-stick exposure → immediate; other conditions ~3–7 days
  • Investigated source — for Salmonella/Campylobacter, human lab cases trigger; the shelter is the investigated source, not the reporter
  • Ringworm — individual cases usually NOT reportable; only cluster outbreaks with confirmed human transmission trigger local notification
Trap Public health (not the vet board) can inspect, restrict movement, or suspend operations; cooperation is mandatory — obstruction is criminal, not merely a regulatory violation.
