HQHVSN — HPAI Biosecurity Framework

HPAI (highly pathogenic avian influenza) mitigation for HQHVSN clinics & free-roaming cat colonies — a 6-domain framework:

  • Facility layout & traffic flow — designated intake areas for feral cats / shelter animals / community pets with signage to prevent crossover; isolation & quarantine space away from high-traffic zones; controlled, limited-personnel access to feral holding.
  • Personnel hygiene & PPE (personal protective equipment) — mandatory handwashing + alcohol-based sanitiser; gloves, gowns, N95 masks during outbreaks; clinic-only scrubs & footwear to protect staff's own pets.
  • Cleaning & disinfection — written SOPs (standard operating procedures) for daily & between-patient cleaning; dedicated equipment per animal group to minimise fomite spread.
  • Patient management — grouped scheduling separating high-risk feral surgeries from routine; immediate isolation of cats with respiratory / neurological signs; plan ante- & post-mortem testing.
  • Public & staff education — train volunteers / caretakers on biosecurity; guide colony caregivers on safe feeding & monitoring for illness.
  • Monitoring & wildlife control — surveillance of colony cats + reporting of unexplained illness; rodent control & wildlife deterrents near colonies and clinics.
⚠ HPAI: isolate any feral/colony cat with respiratory or neurological signs and pursue testing — cats can acquire H5N1 from raw poultry / wild birds.

📄 Journal Articles

  • Integrated HPAI-mitigation biosecurity framework for HQHVSN clinics & cat colonies: a 6-domain protocol — facility layout/traffic flow, personnel hygiene & PPE, cleaning & disinfection SOPs, patient management (grouped scheduling + isolation), education, and monitoring/wildlife control — intended to reduce HPAI transmission risk to staff and patients in HQHVSN settings.
  • Gabapentin in shelter cats (OVH): pre-op gabapentin did NOT significantly reduce stress or pain or improve ease of handling perioperatively — limited evidence for its perioperative use in this setting.
  • HQHVSN anaesthetic/analgesic protocols (2017 US survey, 106 facilities): commonest canine premed = acepromazine + opioid (ace/hydromorphone 26%, ace/butorphanol 23%); canine induction = ketamine/midazolam (39%); commonest feline = total-IM DKT (dexmedetomidine/ketamine/butorphanol) or TTDex; most gave an injectable NSAID; only 40% used local anaesthetics; 86% satisfied with their protocol.
  • Local block in HQHVSN feline castration (RCT): an intratesticular lidocaine (± bupivacaine) block lowered the intraop heart-rate response and improved anaesthetic depth vs placebo, without more incisional complications — supports local blocks in HQHVSN multimodal protocols (~$1 per cat).
  • Perioperative hypothermia in HQHVSN & ambient temperature: perioperative inadvertent hypothermia (PIH) is common in spay-neuter populations despite short surgical times, and is linked to prolonged anaesthetic recovery (cats) and more postoperative pain (cats and dogs). Raising the ambient temperature in surgery and recovery areas may reduce it. PIH prolonged time to extubation by ~3.8 min, and each 1 °C (1.8 °F) drop in rectal temperature at extubation raised the pain score by ~0.51 units.
