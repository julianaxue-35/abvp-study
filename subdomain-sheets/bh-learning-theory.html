Learning Theory

Learning Theory · Behavioral Health (16%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield learning-theory points for the shelter exam — habituation vs sensitisation, classical phases, the four operant quadrants, and LIMA/humane training. Added or clarified points are marked +.

How Animals Learn

  • Learning — experience → long-lasting behaviour change; innate behaviours need no learning history and are intrinsically reinforcing (but still modifiable).
  • 5 aspects — attention, contiguity (events close in time), reinforcement, repetition/association, stimulation.
  • Working vs long-term memory — working = frontal lobes, temporary; long-term needs CREB protein synthesis + consolidation during sleep (REM assists).
Trap: teach only one new behaviour at a time — working memory is easily disrupted during transition to long-term memory.

Non-Associative Learning

  • Habituation ↓ vs sensitisation ↑ — repeated exposure to a single stimulus, no pairing; habituation lowers the response, sensitisation raises it+
  • Habituation use — reduces fear to harmless stimuli; the goal of shelter exposure/socialisation.
  • Imprinting — irreversible attachment; forms only in a short sensitive period early in life; classic in birds and neonatal foals.
Trap: habituation is passive (mere repeated exposure, no pairing); desensitisation/counter-conditioning is active (sub-threshold + paired reward).

Classical (Respondent) Conditioning

  • Classical vs operant — classical (respondent) = involuntary reflex to a predictive cue; operant = voluntary behaviour shaped by its consequence+
  • Secondary reinforcer — a CS that predicts something wanted (clicker) can reinforce operant behaviour.
  • CER — conditioned emotional response: pairing an object/place/person with an emotion (fear, joy, anxiety).
  • One-trial learning — a single (often life-threatening) event → lasting learning; generalises to similar stimuli.
  • State-dependent memory — memory learned in one state may not retrieve in another → train cues in varied contexts.
Trap — cue poisoning: punishment delivered alongside a cue/context poisons it, producing later fear or aggression to that cue.

The three phases and the core terms

  • Before — unconditioned stimulus (US) → unlearned reflex (unconditioned response, UR).
  • During — neutral stimulus (NS) paired with US; NS must immediately precede the US.
  • After — NS becomes the conditioned stimulus (CS) → elicits the conditioned response (CR) alone.
  • Aids — CS novelty/salience, US salience, tight CS–US contiguity, biological preparedness.
Trap — timing: backward (NS after US), simultaneous, or long-delayed pairings condition poorly; forward with short delay is best.

Operant Conditioning and Humane Training

  • Positive reinforcement preferred — follow LIMA (Least Intrusive, Minimally Aversive) and the Humane Hierarchy; punishment-based methods raise fear, anxiety, and aggression (ASV)+
  • Operant = consequences — voluntary, goal-directed behaviour that operates on the environment; strengthened/weakened by what follows.
  • Three-term contingency — Antecedent → Behaviour → Consequence (ABC).
  • Neg. punishment OK — removing something wanted (e.g. withhold attention) is force-free; positive punishment (adding an aversive) is what to avoid.
Trap: positive punishment doesn't fix the cause/emotion, may worsen or resurface the behaviour, raises fear, poisons cues, risks safety, and reduces learning.

The four quadrants

  • Reinforcement ↑ vs punishment ↓ — positive = add a stimulus, negative = remove one; reinforcement increases behaviour, punishment decreases it+
  • Extinction burst — when the reinforcer stops, the behaviour briefly intensifies before it fades+
  • ↑ behaviour — +R add pleasant (treat for a sit); −R remove aversive (release leash pressure).
  • ↓ behaviour — +P add aversive (avoided in humane training); −P remove something wanted (withhold attention).
Trap: negative reinforcement is not punishment — "negative" = remove; reinforcement always increases behaviour, punishment always decreases it.

Flooding and the humane alternative

  • Flooding → learned helplessness — high-intensity exposure with no escape; the humane alternative is desensitisation and counter-conditioning (DS/CC) kept below the fear threshold+
  • DS/CC mechanism — gradual sub-threshold exposure paired with something positive; changes the underlying emotion, not just the visible behaviour.
  • Learned helplessness — the shut-down animal looks calm but is not fixed; the fear is suppressed, not resolved.
Trap: flooding vs systematic desensitisation — same trigger, opposite method; flooding is above threshold with no escape, DS is sub-threshold and gradual.

Putting It Into Practice

  • Delay intake behaviour evals — assessments in the first 24–48 h mislead; situational stress and arousal distort behaviour, so let the animal settle first+
  • Prevent problems — socialise during the sensitive period: early gentle handling, maternal rearing with varied stimuli (→habituation), avoid negative experiences.
  • Set up to succeed — reward-based training, supervision, meet needs (chew/scratch/climb/exercise/social), prevention & confinement.
  • ASV cat housing — ideal individual floor ≥11 sq ft (1 sq m); cohousing ≥18 sq ft (1.7 sq m)/cat; separate functions via multi-compartment housing.
