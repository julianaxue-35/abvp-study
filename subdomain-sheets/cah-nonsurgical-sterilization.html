Non-Surgical Sterilization

Non-Surgical Sterilization · Companion Animal Homelessness (12%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet — HPG-axis interference levels, product regulatory status, four contraceptive modalities, population thresholds, ID marking. Added / clarified points marked +.

The HPG Axis: Three-Level Architecture

  • Pulsatile GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) from hypothalamus → pituitary FSH / LH (follicle-stimulating / luteinising hormone) → gonadal steroids; continuous, non-pulsatile stimulation desensitises receptors+
  • Negative feedback — gonadal androgens / estrogens / progesterone suppress hypothalamus & pituitary
  • Axis is highly conserved across mammals → GnRH-targeting agents get broad cross-species efficacy
  • Gonadal targets = zona pellucida (on ovulated egg, for sperm recognition) + thecal support cells of ovary / testis

Three Levels of HPG Axis Interference

  • Level 1 — hypothalamus / ant. pituitary: GnRH agonists (deslorelin, gonazon) + progestins / androgens block GnRH signalling & gonadotropin release
  • Level 2 — gonads: chemical castrants (zinc gluconate, CaCl₂) destroy testicular tissue directly; GnRH vaccine binds GnRH post-hypothalamus, pre-BBB → halts ovulation + spermatogenesis
  • Level 3 — downstream / oocyte: PZP vaccine hits zona pellucida → blocks sperm recognition; spares HPG axis → fewer hormonal / behavioural effects

Population Modeling: The 40 to 50% Rule

  • 40–50% treated per 6-month interval AND a near-closed population (limited immigration / emigration) — both conditions required+
  • Evidence base = feral cat colonies (modelling of TNR-style non-surgical contraception)
  • Ideal-contraceptive criteria differ by setting: feral = permanent / lifetime, field-deliverable by a non-vet; owned = shorter-term, reversibility a feature not a flaw
Trap 40–50% threshold ALONE fails in an open population — treated animals are continuously replaced by intact immigrants, negating any population effect.

Approved & Available Products

  • Zinc gluconate = Neutersol / Zeuterin / Esterilsol — intratesticular injection, dogs (Neutersol FDA-approved 2003, off US market 2005)+
  • Esterilsol / Zeuterine = identical zinc-gluconate formulation, sold Mexico / Central / South America (not US)
  • Infertile = zinc gluconate + DMSO (vehicle) + arginine (neutraliser) — Brazil, launched 2009; same testicular-sclerosis mechanism as Neutersol+
  • Suprelorin (deslorelin) — GnRH superagonist, reversible; first available Australia 2004
  • Gonazon — EU-approved 2006 (1-yr reversible, female dogs) but never commercially released ("approved" ≠ "available")
  • ALL approved sterilants labelled dogs only — cat use is off-label

Chemical Contraceptives

  • SC misdirection (subcutaneous) of zinc gluconate → necrotising reaction / scrotal ulcers; chemical restraint advised+
  • Zinc gluconate intratesticular → testicular sclerosis → permanent azoospermia / oligospermia + partial ↓testosterone; ~99% effective; testes stay in situ → ID mark required
  • Calcium chloride — must be in a lipid vehicle (alcohol / lidocaine) for deep necrosis without extreme pain & scrotal sloughing
  • Cats worse than dogs — higher rate of severe inflammation, scrotal necrosis, self-mutilation (reluctance = surgery faster + worse reactions, both)

Immunocontraceptives

  • PZP vaccine (porcine zona pellucida) — anti-zona antibody, females only, downstream (spares HPG); completely ineffective in queens (abnormal estrus + injection reactions); poor cross-species; expensive
  • GnRH vaccine — anti-GnRH antibody pre-BBB → shuts down ovulation + spermatogenesis; both sexes; works in horses / deer / elephants with annual booster (sterile abscess risk)
  • Canine GnRH-vaccine failure — neither safe nor effective; severe injection reactions, no drop in sperm density / morphology

Hormonal Contraceptives

  • GnRH agonists e.g. deslorelin (Suprelorin) — reversible; "flare" 1–3 wks (transient fertile estrus / ↑libido) before suppression+
  • Mechanism: continuous non-pulsatile GnRH stimulation → downregulates & desensitises pituitary receptors → ↓LH / FSH; broad cross-species
  • Historical (1970s): progestin megestrol acetate + androgen mibolerone — frequent oral dosing; AEs pyometra, mammary hyperplasia / carcinoma, DM, adrenal suppression → abandoned
  • Feralstat = low-dose megestrol in cat food for ferals; not recommended (no control over non-target consumption)

Identification Marking

  • Ear-tipping CONTRAINDICATED — permanent mark for a temporary contraceptive; use microchip / tattoo / metal ear band instead+
  • Acceptable marks: microchip; tattoo on caudolateral ventral abdomen or inner pinna; small metal ear band
  • Ideal feral marker: minimally invasive, field-applicable, visible at distance, lasts ~ contraceptive duration OR encodes type + date
  • Why ear-tip fails: signals permanent sterility; on a temporary implant the animal regains fertility but stays flagged → field teams skip re-treatment → wrecks population data

Board-Critical Gaps & Exam Traps

  • No approved permanent non-surgical option for female dogs or cats yet+
  • Deslorelin does NOT suppress immediately — flare = 1–3 wk transient ↑LH / FSH / steroids first; female fertile estrus, male ↑libido / aggression; keep from intact males in window
  • Experimental, no canine data: IUD (dogspiral) — cervix anatomy concerns; gene delivery — AAV anti-GnRH wanes <1 mo, MIS lasts only months in cats
Trap An option claiming deslorelin causes "immediate, profound downregulation within hours" is wrong — fertility is transiently enhanced for 1–3 wks before suppression.

📄 Journal Articles

  • Addressing Flare-Up Effect in an Aggressive Tomcat Following a 4.7 mg Deslorelin Implant: A Case Report: A 3-year-old British Shorthair tomcat with severe owner-directed aggression and urine marking was managed with a 4.7 mg deslorelin implant plus gabapentin (10 mg/kg PO q12h). A temporary resurgence of aggression and marking occurred 10 days after implantation, resolving spontaneously within 4 days. Improvements included cessation of urine marking, increased affiliative behaviours, and stable cohabitation with a younger tomcat also implanted after puberty.
  • Advancing and Historical Reversible Contraceptive Solutions for Feline Reproductive Control: Progestins have significant adverse effects in both sexes, including increased risk of uterine infections, mammary tumours, and metabolic complications. GnRH agonists, which can reversibly suppress reproductive function, have emerged as a safer and more flexible alternative to surgical sterilisation; melatonin has shown promising results regulating estrus cycles in female cats; and immunocontraceptive vaccines under development offer hope for scalable, humane population-control measures.
  • Chemical Castration of Male Dogs: Owner-Reported Effects on Behavior and Health, Reasons for Chemical Castration, and Similarities with Subsequent Surgical Castration: The most common reason for chemical castration was testing the influence of surgical castration on behaviour beforehand; the most common reason for leaving a dog intact afterward was negative behavioural effects of the chemical castration. Owner-reported effects of chemical and surgical castration were completely or largely the same in 81.7% of cases.
  • Comprehensive Evaluation and Future Perspectives of Non-Surgical Contraceptive Methods in Female Cats and Dogs: Current options include immunocontraception via vaccination, hormonal analogs blocking the reproductive axis, kisspeptin analogs, the NSAID firocoxib, and cytotoxin delivery to the pituitary — all with non-conclusive efficacy so far. None is currently authorised by the FDA or European Medicines Agency for contraception or sterilisation of cats or dogs.
  • Gene Therapy Delivery of Anti-Müllerian Hormone in Prepubertal Female Domestic Cats Induces Long-Term Sterilization: A single administration of an AAV vector delivering an anti-Müllerian hormone transgene to prepubertal cats fully prevented pregnancy once females reached adulthood, with treated kittens monitored for up to 21 months. The IM injection was well tolerated and did not impact physical growth or male spermatogenesis; in females it induced sterility by preventing breeding-induced ovulation and the luteal-phase progesterone rise, giving safe, potentially lifetime sterilization.
  • Gene Therapy with Feline Anti-Müllerian Hormone Analogs Disrupts Folliculogenesis and Induces Pregnancy Loss in Female Domestic Cats: A single IM injection of an AAV-delivered feline AMH analog produced a greater than 1000-fold increase in AMH levels, sustained for 9 months, associated with abnormal estrus cyclicity and declining follicle numbers.
  • 2024 AAFP Indoor/Outdoor Lifestyle Position Statement: Cats need to be microchipped and, when appropriate, wear a visible ID tag. Significant effort must be made to meet all environmental needs of the indoor-only cat — food, water, comfortable/safe resting places, stimulated hunting and foraging, play, safe territory with vertical space, and clean toileting areas — with some safe, controlled outdoor access considered where possible. Young cats and male cats are more likely to engage in activities that increase injury risk and should be monitored more closely.
  • Impact of a Local Government Funded Free Cat Sterilization Program for Owned and Semi-Owned Cats: A free cat sterilization, microchipping, and registration program run by an Australian council (2013–2014) sterilized an average of 4.1 cats/1000 residents per year over 8 years. City-wide cat intake fell 66%, euthanasia fell 82%, and owner reclaims rose from 6% to 16% of intake; cat-related calls fell 51% in target areas and 36% city-wide. The council realised net savings of AU$440,660 against a sterilisation outlay of AU$77,490.
  • Outcomes of a Community Cat Program Based on Sterilization of Owned, Semi-Owned and Unowned Cats in a Small Rural Town: A small-town community cat program desexed 308 cats — 94 cats per 1000 residents — over 3.4 years. By the third year this was associated with a 60% decrease in cat intake, an 85% reduction in euthanasia numbers, and 39% fewer cat-related calls to the local council.
  • Prepubertal Use of Long-Term GnRH Agonists in Dogs: Current Knowledge and Recommendations: Short- and long-term effects of deslorelin implants applied at different points in the prepubertal period are similar to those seen in adult dogs. The prepubertal bitch's age and the dosage used appear to be the main determinants of response, along with individual metabolism. Deslorelin-mediated long-term delay of puberty does not carry negative effects on subsequent ovarian function, steroid hormone concentrations, uterine health, or fertility, though more molecular study is needed on hormone concentration and peripheral receptor expression by application timing.
