Stress

Stress · Behavioral Health (16%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield stress cheatsheet — SAM/HPA cascades, cortisol traps, FAS, first-week rule, feline stress, long-stay deterioration. Pre-filled points marked +.

Stress Physiology

  • SAM vs HPA cascades — SAM (sympathetic-adrenal-medullary) fires in seconds → epinephrine + norepinephrine from adrenal medulla; HPA (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal) over minutes → CRH (corticotropin-releasing hormone) + AVP (arginine vasopressin) → ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) → cortisol from adrenal cortex+
  • Cortisol kinetics — peaks 20–30 min post-stressor, stays elevated ≥90 min.
  • SAM output (seconds) — epi + norepi → pupil dilation, ↑HR, ↑RR; acute stress enhances memory/sensory function.

Chronic HPA Dysregulation

  • Low cortisol ≠ low stress — chronic stress → adrenal hypertrophy + ↑ glucocorticoids but ACTH unchanged; a normal/low resting cortisol in a long-stay resident can signal a dysregulated HPA axis, not a relaxed animal+
  • ACTH / LHRH stim tests (luteinising-hormone-releasing-hormone) — ↑HPA responsiveness + ↓pituitary sensitivity = chronic-stress HPA dysfunction.
  • Cortisol caveats — signals only stress activation, not type (good/tolerable/toxic); confounded by collection time, neuter status, sampling method.
  • Still reactive — dysregulated long-stay dogs remain responsive to acute stressors (restraint, venipuncture) despite blunted resting cortisol.

Systemic Effects of Stress

  • Behaviour & cognition — ↑maladaptive behaviour + aggression, impairs social behaviour/memory/learning; chronic → structural brain change.
  • HPA systemic effects — ↑energy + O₂, ↓flow to non-critical tissue; inhibits digestion, growth, immunity, reproduction, pain perception.
  • Net cost — ↑disease susceptibility, slowed healing, shortened lifespan.

Stress Taxonomy & Dynamics

  • Acute vs chronic — low–moderate acute stress is beneficial (aids coping); extreme acute or chronic stress harms physical + emotional health+
  • Categories — good (copes, no harm) · tolerable (distressing but copes) · toxic (exceeds coping → lasting harm).
  • Types — physical (pain, hunger, temp, disease) vs psychological (novelty, isolation, unpredictability, chronic frustration).
  • Trigger stacking — stressors accumulate with no return to baseline → amplified response/explosion; context learning locks the reaction to that setting.
  • Maternal / early-life — gestational maternal stress alters pup behaviour → learning impairment + easier conditioning of fear (esp. auditory).
Trap Most potent psychological stressors = novelty, withholding of an expected reward, and anticipation of punishment.

The First-Week Rule

  • Stress highest wk 1 — defer non-essential stressful procedures to allow adaptation + relationship-building.
  • Relinquished vs stray — owner-relinquished = intense psychological stress, no adaptation; strays/returns = lower baseline; prior kennelling ↓ activated response.
  • Petting >15 min/day — no change in relinquished dogs, but ↓ stress in strays.
  • Translocation stress — 4 routes: capture · captivity · transport · release.

Measuring & Identifying Stress

  • Cortisol kinetics + leukogram — cortisol peaks 20–30 min, elevated 90+ min; stress leukogram = lymphopenia + neutrophilia+
  • FAS scale (Fear, Anxiety, and Stress) — rapid clinical scoring to identify and grade stress at the cage-side+
  • Sampling matrix — blood/saliva = momentary (confounded by handling/venipuncture); urine/faeces/hair = long-term, non-invasive, for welfare + housing audits.
Trap To audit housing over months use faecal/hair cortisol metabolites; blood/saliva cortisol is a diagnostic error (acute collection stress).

Feline Stress

  • Feigned sleep · FURI · sickness behaviour — feigned sleep is a coping mechanism, not relaxation; high stress score links to high FURI (feline upper respiratory infection) rates; watch for sickness behaviour (inappetence, hiding)+
  • Five pillars of feline welfare — safe place · separated resources · play/predation · consistent human contact · respect for smell
  • ~4% stay high-stress — most cats settle within 2 wks; ~4% remain highly stressed → may be unsuited to shelter housing.
  • Feral floor space — same minimum as friendly cats: 11 sq ft (1 sq m) short-term (may only use hides at night).
Trap Feigned sleep = coping, NOT relaxation ("cat sleeping/relaxed" is an anthropomorphic error). A trapped tame cat is indistinguishable from feral — let it calm before assessing personality.

Adaptation, Deterioration & Management

  • Three states to distinguish — sensitisation (↑ activity), habituation (↑ or ↓ activity), and depression-like affective state (wakeful inactivity, loss of reward responsiveness)+
  • Adaptation timeline — cortisol falls days 1–3 (regaining homeostasis); by day 6 dogs spend more time at back of kennel/less active (may = comfort).
  • Deterioration signs — barrier aggression, ↑arousal, worsening reactivity, ↑fear, abnormal behaviour; escape behaviours (door-scratch, floor-dig, whine) rise with LOS.
  • When stress found — stop & reassess: cause? options? procedure truly necessary now? Weigh need vs want.

Long-Term Stays

  • Keep LOS short (length of stay) — stays >a few days require daily enrichment; chronic stress ↓coping, ↑fear/anxiety/frustration, jeopardises placement.
  • Options — enrichment, added space/choice, foster; spay-neuter long-stays (reproductive stress → spraying, marking, fighting).
  • Group housing — for pro-social dogs (behaviour stabilises in 1–2 days); very fearful dogs rarely acclimate → high deterioration risk.
