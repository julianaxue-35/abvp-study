Behaviour Medications

Behaviour Medications · Behavioral Health (16%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield behavioural pharmacology for the shelter vet: when to treat, disorder-to-drug matches, neurotransmitter targets, the core classes, and serotonin syndrome. Pre-filled clarifications marked +.

Principles & When to Treat

  • Never used alone — medication is always paired with behaviour modification (BMOD), enrichment, and environmental change+
  • Alters the emotional state, not the behaviour itself — drug makes BMOD easier, never replaces it.
  • Treat when enrichment has failed: still hiding/trembling >2–3 days, not eating/eliminating/grooming, or overactive/compulsive.
  • 3 P's gauge abnormality — Provocation, Proportion, Persistence (normal dog recovers from mild–mod stress in a couple of min).
  • High arousal (piloerection, mydriasis, hard panting, salivation, poor recovery) → favour clonidine over trazodone.
  • US approval status (dogs) — only clomipramine, fluoxetine & selegiline hold FDA approval; every other behaviour drug used in dogs/cats is off-label+
  • Before starting meds, ask — (1) what's the trigger? (2) what's the intensity of the reaction? (3) what's the ability to recover? (same idea as the 3 P's); then frame the case as Behaviour–Trigger–Target–Motivation+
  • Risk vs outcome, for the Tx decision — risk to other animals/people/community · emotional risk to the adopter long-term · QOL for animal + adopter · deterioration risk without intervention · shelter resources · is the community equipped to manage it?+

Disorders & First-Line Treatment

  • First-line — dog anxiety: fluoxetine or paroxetine (SID = once daily); fearful cat still hiding: alprazolam 0.125 mg/cat+
  • Compulsive disorder — clomipramine or fluoxetine + BMOD; continue ≥3 wk after improvement, then wean over 3–4 wk.
  • Separation problems (SRP) — graded short-departure program + fluoxetine or clomipramine.
  • Urine marking — fluoxetine (dog + cat); buspirone gives ~50% reduction in cats.
  • Cat anxiety — enrichment / alt housing / foster first; benzodiazepine (alprazolam) only if still hiding despite hides & perches.

Neurotransmitters

  • Serotonin (5-HT) — ↓ = depression/anxiety/aggression; SSRIs, SARI (trazodone) & TCAs all raise it.
  • GABA — main inhibitory transmitter; benzodiazepines ↑ it (flumazenil reverses).
  • Noradrenaline — alpha-2 agonists (clonidine) ↓ NE release → sedation/anxiolysis; beta-blockers mask the physical fear signs.
  • Glutamate / dopamine / ACh — gabapentin ↓ glutamate; excess dopamine → stereotypy; TCA anticholinergic → dry mouth, urine retention, mydriasis.
  • State → NT pattern — anxiety/compulsion: ↓5-HT with ↑NE, dopamine, glutamate; shut-down animal: all ↓ except ↑GABA.
  • Glutamate — primary excitatory transmitter; NMDA is one receptor subtype, blocked by ketamine and memantine+

Core Drug Classes

  • Baseline vs situational — SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors) & TCAs (tricyclic antidepressants) = daily maintenance, weeks to effect; benzodiazepines, alpha-2 agonists, trazodone, gabapentin = situational / PRN (as needed)+
  • SSRIs — block 5-HT reuptake, full effect 4–8 wk; fewer cardiac effects, may ↓ thyroid; fluoxetine dog 1–2 mg/kg SID, can alter blood glucose (caution in diabetics), wean if used >4 wk.
  • TCAs — block NE + 5-HT reuptake, anticholinergic; LOWER seizure threshold → contraindicated in seizure disorders.
  • Clomipramine = strongest serotonergic TCA, licensed canine separation anxiety; ineffective for owner-directed aggression.
  • Alpha-2 (clonidine) — give 1.5–2 h pre-event; anxiolysis below the sedation dose; Sileo (dexmedetomidine gel) for canine noise aversion.
Trap: MAOI washout Fluoxetine ½-life ≥5 days → 5-wk washout before an MAOI (selegiline); clomipramine needs 2 wk. Skipping it stacks serotonergics → serotonin syndrome.

Benzodiazepines

  • Cat caution — avoid oral diazepam in cats (rare fatal acute hepatic necrosis); always wean 10–25% per week+
  • GABA-A agonist — onset 1–2 h; ~67% effective for anxiety; for panic, storms, noise phobia, feline spraying.
  • Agent picks — alprazolam for acute fear/panic; clonazepam/clorazepate for chronic generalised anxiety.
  • Disinhibition can ↑ aggression — basket-muzzle or crate-train animals with an aggression history; watch paradoxical excitation.
Trap: cats + oral diazepam Rare fatal acute hepatic necrosis — if used, check liver function at baseline and recheck day 3–5.

Trazodone SARI

  • Situational bridge — SARI (serotonin antagonist & reuptake inhibitor); fast-acting, bridges while a baseline SSRI/TCA takes effect. Dose-dependent: targeted anxiolysis at the low end, sedation climbs higher+
  • Mechanism — 5-HT2A antagonist + reuptake inhibitor; also blocks histamine and alpha-1.
  • Dose — dog 2–5 mg/kg PRN or q12h; cat onset ~3 h vs 1–2 h in dogs → trial the dose first.
  • Cautions — avoid before a neuro exam; caution with impaired haemostasis (↓ platelet aggregation).
  • Intake dose — low-dose in the first 48 h → less respiratory illness, shorter length of stay, more adoptions.

Other Agents & Serotonin Syndrome

  • Avoid ACP alone — acepromazine (ACP) sedates without anxiolysis in fearful animals (a "chemical straitjacket")+
  • Serotonin syndrome — from stacked serotonergic drugs; antidote cyproheptadine 1.1 mg/kg q1–4h, halve or stop the drug+
  • Serotonin-syndrome triggers — SSRI + TCA + MAOI, or tramadol, amitraz, St John's wort, buspirone; signs resolve <48 h once halved/stopped.
  • Buspirone (5-HT1A partial agonist) — mild anxiolytic, no motor/cognitive impairment; NOT for panic, phobia, compulsive, or aggression.
  • Gabapentin — alpha-2-delta ligand, ↓ glutamate/NE; NOT a GABA-receptor drug; good feline pre-visit; use xylitol-free liquid in dogs.
  • Mirtazapine — appetite stimulant / antiemetic; max 30 mg TOTAL per day (not per kg). Cat dose 1.88–3.75 mg/cat q72h+

Adjuncts non-pharm

  • Pheromones — via vomeronasal organ; cat F3/F4, dog-appeasing; spray lasts ~6 h, diffuser 30 days; weak–moderate evidence.
  • Alpha-casozepine (Zylkene) — milk-protein, GABA-like; useful mainly in cats (large dogs would need too much).
  • L-theanine — green-tea derivative; ↓ glutamate, ↑ GABA/dopamine; for chronic anxiety.
  • Probiotics — gut–brain axis; improve anxiety behaviours and cortisol; costly.
