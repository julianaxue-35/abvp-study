Population Management · Shelter Management (18%) · Shelter Medicine

Highest-yield exam points for the Population Management subdomain; my added / clarified points are marked +.

Asilomar Accords

  • 4 categories — Healthy (must be age ≥8 wks), Treatable–Rehabilitable, Treatable–Manageable, Unhealthy & Untreatable; pegged to the community standard, not the shelter's own resources+
  • 2004, Pacific Grove CA, ~20 shelter leaders; voluntary framework, not a legal mandate.
  • Reported to Shelter Animals Count (SAC); track %live vs %euth within each category over time.
  • Healthy/Treatable euthanasia = unmet goal; Unhealthy & Untreatable euthanasia = expected/appropriate.
TrapCommunity-referenced, NOT shelter-referenced — a limited budget can't reclassify a Treatable animal as Untreatable; two shelters in one city should categorise the same animal identically.

Capacity for Care (C4C)

  • Count housing units, not cages — capacity for care (C4C) is set by 5 factors (funds, facility, personnel, housing, live outcomes); only welfare-compliant humane housing units (HHUs) count+
  • Operating beyond C4C = defined "unacceptable practice", never an emergency option; within → ↓LOS, ↓disease, ↓euth, ↑live release.
  • Multiple ceilings — can bust the staffing ceiling with empty cages, or the housing ceiling with spare staff; assess all 5 at once.
  • NACA min 15 min/animal/day (feeding + cleaning only) = a floor, not a welfare target.
  • Chronic strain → structured strategic planning; never drop care standards to fit more animals.

Track System & Flow-Through

  • Fast track = pups/kittens/friendly dogs (high adoptability); reassign to slow track if not adopted in 2–3 wks.
  • Slow track = medical/behavioural/geriatric; larger, enriched, quieter housing.
  • Open selection: adopters start paperwork during the stray hold (told reclaim may still occur) — converts hold time into live-release prep.
  • LOS cutters: kill unproductive holds, field reunification (chip scan), foster, RTF, transport; drop discriminatory barriers (income checks, home visits, breed bans).
TrapSlow track full → NO new slow-track animals; fix by accelerating exits, not double-housing. RTF cats ARE admitted & counted in SAC BasicDataMatrix; TNR cats are not (Community Services Matrix only).

Population Formulas

  • Master relationship — average daily population = average daily admissions × average length of stay (LOS); halving LOS halves the population+
  • RHC = MDA intake × legal hold period → stray-hold units (15/day × 4d = 60).
  • ADC = target ALOS × MDA adoptions → adoption-floor spaces (5d × 4/day = 20).
  • SCDC = total daily staff-care minutes ÷ min-per-animal → census ceiling (2400 ÷ 15 = 160).
Trap15 min/animal/day = feeding + cleaning only; a staffing floor, NOT "adequate care" — medical/behavioural animals need far more.

Rounds & Welfare

  • Population rounds = daily, all departments — ask 3 questions per animal: how are you doing? / pathway & updates? / next steps? (vs. medical rounds = vet team delivering care)+
  • Needs found in rounds acted on promptly — deferral to next day is not acceptable.
  • Vulnerable: pediatric (foster ASAP, protect socialisation window), geriatric, nursing mums/neonates (neonates can't thermoregulate → foster preferred).
  • Geriatric intake minimum database: PCV/TP, USG, urine dipstick.
TrapSAC pediatric cutoff = strictly under 5 months (notes saying "4–5 months" are wrong).

Isolation vs. Quarantine

  • Quarantine ≠ isolation — quarantine = apparently healthy but exposed (hold one incubation period); isolation = currently sick/infectious+
  • Isolation: separate air handling, dedicated PPE, staff visit last; segregate by species/pathogen.
  • Quarantine: no mixing with general population; monitor twice daily for the full incubation period.
TrapParvo-exposed but well dog → quarantine; dog with vomiting + haemorrhagic diarrhoea → isolation; unknown healthy stray → standard intake hold.

Outcomes & Planning

  • Reduce intake to cut euthanasia — Marsh (2010): intake correlates with euthanasia, adoption does not; live release rate (LRR) has 3 calculation methods, so it is not comparable across shelters unless the method is known+
  • 3 LRR methods: standard (live out ÷ live intake), outcomes-based, net-intake.
  • Community intake planning figure: ~5–20/1000 people urban, ~20–40/1000 rural.
  • OIE (owner-intended euthanasia) = its own SAC intake category, NOT bundled under owner surrender.
TrapSingle most effective lever to lower euthanasia = reduce intake (front-door management), not pushing adoptions harder (Marsh 2010).

Foster Care

  • Accumulated infectious risk — risk = every animal ever in the home (not the current census); it does not reset when animals leave+
  • Homes needed: winter = monthly kitten intake ÷ 2; peak season = ÷ 3 — always recruit above calculated need.
  • Fospice: shelter retains final euthanasia decision authority (state it in the agreement).
  • Households with own pets <20 wks can't foster (incomplete antibody protection); resident cats must be FeLV/FIV negative.
  • Staff fostering the employer's animals → state labour/overtime law risk (has drawn fines).
TrapThe divisor = kittens absorbed per home (≈2 winter, ≈3 peak). ÷3 gives fewer homes per kitten, but peak intake is far higher so total homes still rise (90÷3 = 30 vs 20÷2 = 10).

📄 Journal Articles

  • Community factors & animal-shelter outcomes: areas with greater economic stress and lower educational attainment are more likely to have a municipal shelter, which increases stray intake and ultimately euthanasia; community economic stress is also directly associated with lower adoption and higher euthanasia.
  • Underage-kitten mortality (foster-based rearing model): vs baseline, mortality risk was ~12× for the lightest-weight group, ~15× with anorexia, ~6× with weight loss, ~5× with trauma, ~3× with diarrhoea. The same clinical signs act as prognostic indicators in both shelter/rescue and foster settings — use them to target monitoring, intervention, and euthanasia decisions.
  • Capacity for Care (C4C) — worker perceptions: an owned-cat waitlist may decrease owned-cat admissions by advising owners about alternatives to relinquishment.
  • Free-roaming dog dynamics (2 European regions): populations under ongoing catch-neuter-release + sheltering; avg monthly apparent survival ~0.93 at both sites; density ranged ~7–40 dogs/km²; detection lower on weekdays and higher on market days; apparent survival lower in males.
  • Shelter response to COVID-19 (14 NE-US shelters): dog & cat intake fell significantly vs the same months of 2019; adoption and euthanasia rates did not differ, but absolute numbers adopted and euthanised fell with the lower intake. Several shelters expanded foster networks (operational changes + public appeals), yet the proportion housed in foster care did not differ (Mar–Jun 2019 vs 2020).
  • Intake-category trends (Colorado, 2008–2018): total dog intake rose over the period but showed no change once adjusted for human population; cat intake fell on both raw and per-capita measures. For dogs, stray % fell while interstate-transfer % rose; for cats, stray % fell while owner-surrender % rose. Rescue organisations showed the largest increase in dog interstate transfers. State-level category monitoring guides resource-allocation and policy decisions.
