Dog Behaviour Assessment and Information Sources

  • Mixed, longitudinal model — formal evaluation tests predict future behaviour (esp. aggression) poorly, so combine objective history + observation across many contexts; foster & returns are the most accurate reflection of home behaviour.+

Behaviour Evaluation Science

  • Validity depends on prevalence — Patronek & Bradley: at ~16% aggression prevalence, even 85% sensitivity/specificity leaves ≈48% of positive flags false ("no better than a coin flip"). SAFER (Safety Assessment for Evaluating Rehoming) sens 60% / spec 50%, misses MODERATE aggression. For aggression favour high NPV (negative predictive value).+

Risk, Behaviour Modification and Outcomes

  • LIMA (Least Intrusive, Minimally Aversive) + Humane Hierarchy; positive reinforcement over punishment. Respond promptly to significant safety risk — a credible report of severe human- or dog-directed aggression → euthanise as soon as legally possible (no benefit to holding).+

Bite history: documentation, placement, and legal considerations

  • Dunbar Bite Scale — levels 1–6 (1 = air snap … 4 = deep puncture … 6 = fatal); Level 3+ narrows placement (no infants/toddlers). Most US states impose a legal duty to disclose known bite history to adopters — document disclosure and adopter sign-off.+

Welfare and Ethical Decision Making

  • Three viewpoints of welfare — biological functioning · affective/emotional state · natural living; no single gold-standard measure. Frameworks: Five Freedoms, Welfare Quality (4 principles), Five Domains. QBA (Qualitative Behaviour Assessment) shows high inter-rater agreement.+

Daily rounds

  • Six rounds questions — Who are you? How are you? Are you where you should be? What can we provide today? Where are you going? Are you meeting the Five Freedoms/Domains? Run rounds before cleaning & feeding; reduces LOS (length of stay) and prevents crowding.+
