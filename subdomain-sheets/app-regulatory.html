Regulatory (Policy) · Animals & Public Policy (7%) · Shelter Medicine

Rapid-review of the Regulatory subdomain (Animals & Public Policy) — non-profit governance, stray holds, practice-act supervision, DEA rules, shelter liability. Clarified/added points marked +.

501(c)(3) Structure

  • IRS 501(c)(3) (Internal Revenue Service) = federal tax-exempt charity; donations deductible; NO political-campaign activity; the facility is licensed by the state agriculture dept, not the vet board.+
  • Private inurement barred — no net earnings to any individual; excessive exec pay triggers it.
  • Lobbying limited-OK; candidate campaigning = zero.
  • Board = strategic oversight (mission, policy, budget, hire/fire the exec director); duties of care, loyalty, obedience; conflicts → disclose + recuse. Board does not run day-to-day ops.

IRS Form 990

  • Form 990 is a public document — anyone may request it; 3 consecutive years unfiled → automatic loss of tax-exempt status.+
  • Annual info return — revenue, expenses, assets, exec compensation, governance.
  • Must hand over the 3 most recent on request; Candid/GuideStar post them free online.
  • Municipal shelters do not file a 990 (government entity, not an IRS charity).

Municipal vs Non-Profit Shelters

(government entity vs private 501(c)(3): funding, oversight, tax status, hold obligation)

  • Municipal = govt dept/agency; tax-appropriation funded; council/CAO oversight; income-tax-exempt as govt but not 501(c)(3) — no 990, no private board, no inurement/political rules.
  • Non-profit = private tax-exempt corp; donations/grants; Board → exec director; files 990; state AG oversight.
  • Stray hold: municipal is the designated impounding authority — compelled, no choice; an independent private shelter takes strays at its own legal risk unless contracted.
  • A non-profit on a govt animal-control contract can gain government-actor status (§1983 risk) yet still owes all 501(c)(3) duties.

Statutory Hold Periods

  • With owner ID (tag / microchip) — held longer, often up to 7 days (Illinois Animal Control Act model); no-ID strays often 3–5 days.+
  • Hold = min days the animal stays available for owner reclaim before disposal (adopt/euth/transfer).
  • ID = collar/tag/microchip/rabies tag → triggers the longer hold.
  • Some states count business days only (exclude weekends/holidays); evidence animals held per court order, overriding the normal hold.

No Elective Surgery During the Hold

  • Elective spay/neuter before the hold expires = unauthorised alteration of another person's property (conversion); emergency care only.+
  • Animal stays the owner's legal property until the hold lapses unreclaimed → ownership then passes to the shelter by operation of law.
  • Emergency/life-saving care is allowed and required; costs recoverable from owner on reclaim.
  • Hold off microchip/tattoo too during the hold — it marks the animal as shelter property.
Trap Elective spay/neuter before the hold expires is a violation even if reclaim is unlikely or a written S/N policy exists — it's conversion of the owner's property.

Supervision Levels: Direct vs Indirect

  • Direct supervision (vet physically on premises) required for surgery, diagnosis, prescribing, and rabies vaccination.+
  • Direct = vet on-site, immediately available; indirect = vet authorised + phone-reachable, off-site.
  • Indirect OK: non-rabies intake vax, exams/BCS, dewormer/flea, blood draws by techs under standing orders.
  • Prescribing needs a valid VCPR (usually a physical exam) — a new condition needs a new exam; a phone description won't do. Rabies vax = direct only (legal cert status).

DEA Controlled Substance Regulations

  • DEA (Drug Enforcement Administration — federal) regulates Schedule II–V; pentobarbital = C-II; address rule — order/receive only at the exact registered address.+
  • Anyone dispensing/administering Sched II–V needs a DEA registration (usually the attending vet).
  • Sched II: DEA Form 222 to order + perpetual inventory log; biennial (2-yr) count; III–V records kept 2 yrs.
  • Sched I = no accepted medical use (heroin); II = pentobarbital, fentanyl, morphine. Disposal via reverse distributor/take-back — never down the drain.
Trap A relief vet whose DEA address is home/another clinic can't have pentobarbital shipped to the shelter — order/receive only at the exact registered address; update it on a move.

Public Accountability Laws

(FOIA — Freedom of Information Act; open-meetings & public-records law; government audit — municipal shelters only)

  • Municipal shelters fall under FOIA/state public-records — intake logs, euth records, contracts, emails all requestable within statutory timeframes.
  • Open-meetings laws: budget/policy sessions public; closed session only for personnel/litigation/security.
  • Government audit + competitive-bid procurement above thresholds — can't just pick a preferred vendor.
  • Private non-profits are not under FOIA — accountability runs through the public Form 990 + state AG.

Liability & Legal Exposure

(§1983 — 42 U.S.C. federal civil-rights claims — apply only to government actors; sovereign immunity; indemnification)

  • §1983 needs "state action" — only government actors (municipal, or a non-profit on a govt contract); an independent private shelter is not exposed.
  • Most states swapped the old "one-bite" rule for strict-liability bite statutes — owner liable regardless of prior knowledge if the victim was lawfully present.
  • Sovereign immunity partly shields municipal shelters (varies by state, statutory caps); private shelters carry full tort liability.
  • Govt indemnifies employees acting in scope; a private-shelter employee may face personal exposure.
  • Disclose all known bite/aggression history at adoption — concealment = liability for post-adoption injury; shelter keeps ownership + primary liability during foster/trial adoption unless ownership is transferred.
