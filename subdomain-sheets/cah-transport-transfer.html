Animal Transport & Transfer

Animal Transport & Transfer · Companion Animal Homelessness (12%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet — Animal Transport & Transfer (ASV Guidelines 2nd ed, ch. 11); pre-filled points marked +.

Relocation Framework, Goals and Regulations

  • UNACCEPTABLE to transport an animal when transport itself is likely harmful; every design decision must decrease LOS (length of stay) and respect Capacity for Care.+
  • Source = community with excess pets; destination = unmet adopter demand — don't hold for relocation when live outcomes exist locally.
  • Weigh benefits vs risks for ALL affected animals — those transported, those already at the destination, and those left at the source — plus human safety, not just the traveller's welfare.+
  • Distance tiers — local <4 h · regional · interstate/international; risk, paperwork and welfare planning all scale up with each tier.+
  • Follow federal + state/local regs at BOTH ends; Ag/Health depts require CVIs, set vaccines, age/health limits.
  • Collect non-urgent health/outcome data within 14 d; quarterly aggregate review; track total LOS = source + destination.
Trap Do NOT return animals to source even if unexpected medical/behaviour issues surface at destination — fix selection criteria, don't re-ship. 14-day (individual) vs quarterly (aggregate) are distinct intervals.

Source and Destination Responsibilities

  • Source vaccinates the whole population at/before intake, treats parasites, heartworm-tests dogs ≥6 mo; Destination does a brief intake health check & houses without displacing residents (S/N (spay/neuter) performed at destination).+
  • Vaccinate the whole source population, not just travellers; don't hold animals back for a vaccine/booster response.
  • S/N at destination — travel does NOT raise the 2% post-op complication rate (within 2 wk); source S/N only adds LOS.
  • Destination quarantine driven by health status + disease risk, not routine for all; multi-source loads → separate compartments.
Trap "Vaccinate only the transport pool" is the wrong answer — population-wide vaccination at/before intake is the standard.

Animal selection criteria

  • Must be easily handled by varied people, tolerate prolonged confinement, and show no overt aggression to any co-travelling species.
  • Known behaviour concerns → NOT eligible unless animal + human safety ensured AND best welfare chance is at the destination.
  • Importing animals <8 wk may be prohibited in some states; international transports need a written policy statement.
  • Behaviour medication may be used under vet direction where it genuinely helps — but the animal must not be sedated for the journey.+
  • Destination obligations — brief health check at intake, adequate housing, and fulfil spay/neuter + any quarantine requirement; quarantine is usually not mandated and is set by health status, not routine.+

Records, certificates and identification

  • A valid CVI (Certificate of Veterinary Inspection) + proof of rabies vaccination accompanies each animal; microchip before transport is ideal for permanent ID.+
  • Share health + behaviour records with destination; CVI/rabies rules vary by state.
  • Departments of Health/Agriculture also require emergency contact information, a named contact person at each end, and written agreements between all parties — not just the CVI.+
  • Visual ID always (collar/tag; inner-ear mark or painted claw on a neonate); mark each enclosure with the animal's unique ID.
  • Manifest (every animal aboard) kept separate from the vehicle so it survives an accident; a cloud copy satisfies this.

The fit-for-transport examination

  • Examined by trained staff within 24 h prior to travel; a veterinarian must clear any animal with medical concerns or recovering from surgery.+
  • Trained staff may clear apparently-healthy animals; vet-only for medical/post-surgical — not the driver, inspector or foster coordinator.
  • Screens for infectious disease + ability to tolerate confinement, handling and animal exposure.
Trap UNACCEPTABLE to sedate to the point an animal can't swallow, walk or thermoregulate — risks choking, aspiration, hypothermia, arrest. Meds only under vet direction, never to mask poor practice.

Vehicles, Climate Control and Safety

  • Ground band 7.2–29.5°C (45–85°F); comfort 18–26.6°C (64–80°F); humidity 30–70%.
  • AVOID factory cargo climate units (built for static loads like flowers) — can't clear metabolic heat; use roof-mounted unit + insulation + insulated floor mat.
  • Sensors at animal level; battery CO detector in animal compartment; dual-control remote alarms; no scented sprays/essential oils.
Trap Air ≠ ground limits: cargo hold -7 to 27°C (19–81°F); loading BANNED if aircraft interior >29°C (84°F).

The ASV confinement grid

  • 28 h max total confinement (excl. overnight rest) · 12 h max driving/day · 10 h min overnight rest · visual obs Q4h.+
  • Loading/unloading ≤1 h each (counted inside the 28 h); 2 drivers for trips >4 h.
  • Air: max loading 45 min; ≤30 min on a loaded plane without take-off if ground >23.9°C (75°F).
  • Overnight: house animals off the vehicle when feasible (foster/shelter/hotel/hub); never leave unattended without adequate monitoring.
  • At the overnight stop dogs must be walked and cats must have litter trays; juveniles need extra provision if ambient >29°C (84°F).+
Trap Adults — water ≥Q4h at stops but food only ≥Q24h; juveniles eat small amounts Q4h (small meal ≤4 h pre-departure). Not the same clock.

Enclosure Standards and Species-Specific Protocols

  • Sized to stand/sit erect, turn around, lie naturally without lying on another animal; vents on ≥3 sides, ≥1 in (2 cm) unobstructed.
  • Kennels must be removable, not built into the vehicle — durable for repeated use, won't collapse or cause injury, and easy to clean and disinfect.+
  • Enclosures secured against movement with doors secured; use a disinfectable visual barrier to reduce visual stimulation.+
  • Neonates <8 wk travel with the mother (enclosure big enough to nurse) and with siblings where possible; community cats single layer, NEVER stacked (feral den + guillotine doors).
  • Cats need a hiding place in the enclosure, and litter trays for long-distance travel.+
  • Cats ideally on a separate vehicle from dogs; if shared, physical + visual + noise barriers.
  • Puppies, kittens, geriatric and brachycephalic animals need different environmental parameters — or an alternative transport mode altogether.+
  • Fearful / feral — martingale collar + leash and a slip lead during loading/unloading; GPS tracker on flight-risk dogs.+
Trap Unfamiliar animals MUST NOT share a primary enclosure — no exceptions for size, age, temperament or trip length.

Behavioural Stress, Sedation Evidence and Outcomes

  • Moving animals across regions spreads region-specific disease — named zoonoses plague, rabies, leptospirosis; heartworm (Dirofilaria immitis) is vector-borne, not a classic zoonosis.+
  • Heart rate peaks during loading/unloading, not flight (dogs mostly inactive in the air).
  • Recovery scales with journey length — beagles transported 22 h took 2 weeks for serum cortisol and total bilirubin to normalise; a 9.5 h journey normalised overnight.+
  • In flight, dogs lay down ~50% of the time and were inactive ~75%; 44% of owned dogs showed signs of fear, anxiety and stress plus motion sickness.+
  • Clomipramine BID starting 3 d pre-transport ↓cortisol, ↓N:L ratio, ↓HR, ↓drooling vs placebo — not acepromazine at loading.
  • Post-transport disease: dogs CIRDC 17% at 30 d (highest); cats FURI 25.8% (↑ with younger age, longer transport, longer destination LOS).
