Mental Health & Resiliency

Mental Health & Resiliency · Shelter Management (18%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield cheatsheet — occupational hazard control, waste anesthetic gas, HQHVSN ergonomics, and the burnout / ProQOL frameworks. Pre-filled points marked +.

Workplace Safety & Hazard Control

  • Hierarchy of risk control — Elimination → Substitution → Engineering → Administrative → PPE (personal protective equipment — LAST resort)+
  • What each tier means — remove entirely · replace with a safer alternative · modify the environment · change work practice / procedure · last resort, never the preferred method+
  • 3 hazard categories — Chemical (WAG, disinfectants, formalin) · Biologic (zoonoses: ringworm, Q fever, Bartonella; allergens; sharps) · Physical (MSK strain, lifting)
  • Participatory ergonomics — delegate problem-solving to the workers doing the task; not merely "ask staff for feedback"
  • Why it works — staff-designed fixes are more usable & cut resistance vs top-down mandates
Trap "Most effective" control = Elimination, never better gloves/PPE — PPE is the bottom tier.

Waste Anesthetic Gas (WAG) Safety

  • PPE does NOT protect against WAG — masks / HEPA filters fail; use engineering controls first (active scavenging > ventilation) then administrative (low flow, daily leak test, monitoring badges)+
  • Chronic exposure — miscarriage / pre-term (primary repro risk), balance & proprioception deficits, haematologic changes, genotoxicity
  • Global warming potential of iso / sevo >1,000× CO2 (specific figure — testable; HEPA does nothing for gas)
  • Charcoal canisters adsorb gas (≠ charcoal masks); weigh daily for exhaustion; least-effective, never primary
  • Room ventilation must be non-recirculating — recirculating air returns the gas to the same staff; pair with drip-free bottle adapters on the keyed filling system+
  • Filling — min staff present; pregnant / trying-to-conceive staff never in room; keyed adapter, end of day
  • Large spill — ventilate → cat-litter absorb → sweep to sealed container (or OR suction); small spill just evaporates
Trap Daily leak test & lowest flow = administrative; scavenging = engineering. Leak test daily, badges periodic.

Surgical Suite Design & Visual Environment

  • Lighting CRI >90 (not 80 = commercial standard, not "≥90"); must carry red spectrum >600 nm for tissue / vessel / blood ID
  • Presbyopia hits most vets by ~40; verify new glasses don't force a head/neck posture change (cervical MSK load)
  • Mask fogging fix — tape over nose bridge (NOT remove mask / anti-fog lens / change mask)
Trap CRI must be >90 and carry red >600 nm — a high-CRI room with no red spectrum still fails for surgery.

Physical Ergonomics for HQHVSN Surgeons

  • Table height — 5–10 cm below elbow (not at/above); small patients to the near side, not centred
  • Most common MSK sites — lower back, neck, shoulders, right thumb, right wrist
  • Micropauses — 15–30 seconds (not minutes), several/hr + movement; saddle chair, anti-fatigue mat, alternate sit/stand
  • Thicker suture = more force = more hand/wrist injury — smallest effective suture, pedicle ties, alternate grips & knot styles
  • 3 MSK risk-factor categories+
    • Biomechanical — static / awkward postures, high repetition, application of significant force+
    • Psychosocial — psychological stressors (high workload, low decision latitude); independently drive MSK injury — ergonomic, not just mental+
    • Individual — physical fitness, personal resilience, coping skill+
  • Also — cushioned footwear alongside the saddle chair / anti-fatigue mat+
Trap Table below elbow; micropauses = seconds not minutes. MSK risk is not purely biomechanical — psychosocial and individual factors are named categories.

The Job Strain Model

  • Job Demand-Control model — high demand + low control / low decision latitude = major strain; high demand + high control = challenge & growth, not distress+
  • High demand alone ≠ max strain — the control variable is the key modifier
  • HQHVSN driver — extreme time pressure + emotional weight + low autonomy over scheduling / protocols / outcomes
  • Perioperative-death debrief barrier = scheduling / operational tempo, not a legal ban or software limit; text mandates candid error discussion anyway

At-Risk Demographics

  • Highest risk — young, female, solo practitioners (social isolation is an independent risk factor)+
  • Shelter vets — elevated distress vs other cohorts (e.g. multi-doctor GP)
  • Recommended interventions — mindfulness, psychotherapy, compassion-fatigue training (NOT more surgical volume / isolation / admin rotation)

The Four Pillars of Coping

  • 4 pillars — technical learning · finding perspective · seeking support · emotional learning+
  • Technical learning = analyse the error for skill / team fixes (not just do more surgeries)
  • Finding perspective = context without minimising the event; seeking support directly counters isolation risk
  • Emotional learning = build personal capacity to recover from professional trauma + self-awareness of responses

Burnout Spectrum ProQOL

  • Distinguish the constructs — compassion fatigue (cumulative cost of caring) vs burnout (chronic work stress) vs moral distress (blocked from ethical action, e.g. euthanizing an adoptable animal) vs secondary traumatic stress (STS — indirect trauma, can arise from a single case)+
  • Compassion fatigue = umbrella — includes burnout + STS; driver = high euthanasia volume / repeated exposure
  • Moral distress fix = policy clarity / ethics consult, not workload reduction; STS can hit from a single case
  • Burnout vs STS — burnout = cumulative chronic work stress; STS = sudden indirect trauma from cruelty / seizure cases
  • Protective factors — collegial relationships + a sense of professional autonomy / competence (mirror-image of the low-decision-latitude driver in the Job Strain Model)+
Trap Moral-distress pain comes from the ethical conflict, not care volume — needs a different institutional response than compassion fatigue.

Compassion Satisfaction ProQOL

  • Positive dimension — protective buffer against fatigue / burnout; must be actively cultivated by leadership, not automatic+
  • ProQOL is bidirectional — measures satisfaction (positive) + fatigue / STS / burnout (negative)
  • Reducing fatigue alone plateaus — low fatigue + low satisfaction still = attrition / disengagement risk
  • Cultivate via — recognise outcomes, celebrate live-release milestones, share positive stories
  • Leadership's job — ensure staff can see their work is meaningful at the population level, not only case-by-case+
