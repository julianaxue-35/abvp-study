High-yield cheatsheet — Animals & Public Safety. Terse board points; clarified / added items marked +.

Community Cat Definitions

  • Free-roaming (umbrella) = owned-outdoor + stray + feral cats
  • Stray — was socialized to humans; now lost, abandoned, or escaped
  • Feral — not socialized; defined by socialization level, not birthplace
  • Lit note — stray/feral split used inconsistently; ~15% of cats presented for OHE are feral
  • Ranging — females & human-fed cats have smaller home ranges; human abandonment is the main driver of growth
Trap Feral ≠ "born outdoors" — an outdoor kitten can be socialized; an abandoned adult stays socialized. This drives the TNR-vs-socialize-for-adoption call.

Population Dynamics

  • TNR threshold (trap-neuter-return) — need ≥70% sterilization of intact females to stabilize a colony+
  • Fecundity — seasonally polyestrous; 1.4 litters/yr, ~3 live births/litter, 50–100 kittens/lifetime
  • Growth — up to 150%/yr despite 50–75% kitten mortality (<6 mo)
  • Pre-trap — food in unset traps 2–7 d + withhold other food (acclimatize, not starve); post-return death ~6%
  • Sterilization benefit — cuts pyometra, mammary tumours & fight-borne FIV
Trap The 70% applies to intact females only, not total colony size. Below it the colony keeps growing despite active TNR.

Management Strategies

  • TNR = gold-standard first-line: trap-neuter-vaccinate-ear-tip-return; highest public acceptance
  • Lethal / TRE — only viable in truly closed (island) populations; rebound otherwise
  • RTF/SNR & relocation — adjuncts only; don't cut population alone; relocated cats try to return
  • Mesopredator release — cat removal can worsen wildlife (rodent boom); fed colonies hunt <1% of time
  • Why manage — complaint drivers are nuisance (yowling/spraying), disease (Toxoplasma — cat = only definitive host, ringworm/M. canis, fleas/tapeworm), and wildlife predation (bird-conservation concern); neutering removes the top complaints
Trap Vacuum effect — removing cats without blocking immigration/breeding lets surrounding cats repopulate. TNR-held sterile residents defend territory and suppress it.

TNR vs TVHR

  • TVHR = vasectomy (M) + hysterectomy (F); infertile but hormones preserved
  • Hormones retained → yowling, fighting, spraying + FIV/FeLV risk + mammary tumours all persist
  • Stump pyometra still possible (ovaries left in)
  • Evidence — only one modelling study favours TVHR, unreplicated; costlier & harder → not recommended over TNR
  • Caveat — any colony shrinkage under TVHR may reflect shorter lifespan, not strategic efficacy
Trap "TVHR is more humane" is FALSE — it preserves nuisance behaviours and disease risk; the only thing it shares with TNR is infertility.

Welfare, Legal & Public Perception

  • Dangerous-dog laws — vary by jurisdiction; know local dangerous-dog / feeding-ban statutes & caretaker liability+
  • Welfare scale — 4 params: BCS, coat, ocular/nasal discharge + ear crusting, injuries
  • Public opinion — favours TNR; euthanasia is consistently the LEAST acceptable option; overwhelming majority prefer leaving cats outdoors over capture/euthanasia even knowing traumatic-injury risk
  • Colony caretaker attachment — studies consistently show high attachment between caretakers and colony cats, even without physical contact; significant polarisation vs. bird-conservation professionals +
  • Zoonosis — disease/parasite rates ≈ owned cats; rabies = most-reported +ve domestic species in US (NASPHV: 3-yr vaccine, re-trap 1 yr then q3yr; single dose still protective)
  • BSL — ineffective; AVMA & ASV oppose; targets pit bull-type dogs, Rottweilers, GSD regardless of temperament → legal euth by breed/appearance, not behaviour; individual behaviour-based dangerous-dog laws + owner education + bite-prevention programs + enforcement of existing laws preferred +
  • Survival bias — sterilized adults: more obese, fewer skin lesions, but more permanent disability than intact

Dog Bite Epidemiology

  • US burden — ~4.5 million bites/yr; ~800,000 need medical care (~half children); 30–50 fatalities/yr+
  • Highest risk — serious injury/fatality in children <5 and the elderly
  • Who bites — most bites involve a familiar dog, not a stranger attack
  • Wound care — wash (virucidal, e.g. povidone-iodine) + pressure-irrigate 20 mL / 18 G saline; cover & seek care
  • Pathogens — dog: Staph/Strep/Pasteurella; cat bites = deeper punctures → higher infection (Pasteurella)

Dog Bite Risk Factors

  • Animal — intact males bite more than neutered; most bites are from familiar dogs, not strangers+
  • Animal (more) — resource guarding, fear-/pain-based aggression, predatory (esp. small kids), prior bite hx
  • Human — children <5 (can't read signals, face-level), elderly, unfamiliar dog in unfamiliar context
  • Triggers — approaching a dog that's eating/sleeping/with pups; running or sudden movements
  • Prevention — never leave young children unsupervised with dogs; teach kids not to approach eating/sleeping/guarding dogs

Shelter Obligations — Documentation & Disclosure

  • Disclosure — written bite history must be disclosed to adopters, fosters & transfer partners+
  • Record — document ALL known bites (written/electronic); verbal disclosure insufficient
  • Access — restrict + clearly mark high-bite-risk housing; risk assessment before any live outcome for mild-moderate aggression
  • Dunbar — L3 vs L4 = does any puncture exceed half the canine-tooth depth (top-tested threshold, see Dunbar card); disclosure applies at EVERY level
  • Staff — body-language/handling training reduces but does not eliminate bite risk

Dunbar Bite Severity Scale (L1–L6)

  • L1 — aggressive display (growl/snap/lunge), no skin contact +
  • L2 — skin contact, no puncture (bruising/redness/abrasion only) +
  • L3 — 1–4 punctures, single bite, no puncture >½ canine-tooth length +
  • L4 — as L3 but ≥1 puncture >½ canine-tooth length ± deep bruising/bidirectional (shake) laceration; dog failed to self-inhibit +
  • L5 — multiple L4 bites in one incident, OR ≥2 separate L4 incidents +
  • L6 — victim killed +
Trap L3→L4 is the board-tested cutoff — sustained bite pressure with failed self-inhibition, not just "more punctures." Most shelters restrict public adoption from L4 up without specialist eval.

The 10-Day Rabies Observation Rule

  • Quarantine — any dog/cat/ferret that bites a human → observe 10 days regardless of vaccination status; if it dies, test (head cooled, not frozen)+
  • Intake — ASK whether the animal has bitten anyone in the past 10 days
  • Dies in window — death for any reason → mandatory rabies testing; aggression itself can be a rabies sign
  • Staff — handlers should get pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP); call public health for other species
