Facility & Environment (Behaviour)

Facility & Environment (Behaviour) · Behavioral Health (16%) · Shelter Medicine

High-yield behavioural facility & environment recall — ASV written-policy, species separation, feline spatial minimums, daily routine/predictability, acoustics & lighting. Physical plant + its operation only (enrichment & feline behaviour-mod live elsewhere). Pre-existing points marked +.

Environmental Management & Mental Health

  • Written policy required — engineer the environment to minimise stimuli that induce fear, stress & frustration; support mental health (ASV — Association of Shelter Veterinarians directive; not an informal aspiration)+
  • Novel environment = worst stressor for shy, undersocialised & geriatric animals → foster placement is the best option for them
  • Social housing lowers stress — shelter dogs with conspecifics showed lower faecal cortisol & displacement behaviours than housed alone (even vs an enriched solo cage; Corsetti 2023)
Exam trap Feral animals must NOT be housed in the shelter — the only exception is a brief stay directly to deliver veterinary care. Reject distractors proposing long-term housing, colony-room placement, or in-facility behavioural rehab of a truly feral animal.

Housing, Species Separation & Space

  • Double-compartment housing — separates rest from elimination; aids house-training and lowers stress (forcing a dog to live in its elimination area harms both)+
  • Enclosure must allow — stand, lie down, turn around, walk several steps, stretch fully & retreat from aversive stimuli
  • Separate zones for eating, drinking, eliminating & exercising
  • Cramped = abnormal behaviour — frustrates locomotion/exploration → inactivity, pacing, stereotypies
  • ≥ 24 h = long-term housing standard — "short-term/temporary" should mean < 24 h (one circadian cycle); any longer stay must meet full husbandry best-practice, not minimal holding (Warwick 2023)

Species Separation

  • Absolute separation rules — prey away from predators; cats away from dogs (out of sight & sound where possible); feral animals not housed except a brief stay for veterinary-care delivery+
  • Mandatory & continuous — not a "when convenient" preference; prey kept from predatory species at all times
  • Cats vs dogs — separate by both SIGHT and SOUND where the building allows

Spatial Requirements

  • Feline group-housing minimums — ≥ 20 in (51 cm) between functional areas (feed, water, rest, litter); ~ 3 ft (0.9 m) personal space per cat; hiding boxes & elevated perches allow retreat and reduce stress (capacity set by resource geometry, not raw floor area)+
  • Capacity = resource geometry, not floor-area math — must fit feed/water/litter/perch stations ≥ 20 in (51 cm) apart while keeping ~ 3 ft (0.9 m)/cat (a 9×12 ft / 2.7×3.7 m room holds fewer cats than area alone implies)
  • Too small → aggression + reduced activity in group-housed cats

Routine, Predictability & Acoustic Environment

  • ASV — consistency — provide a structured environment; minimise reassignment of enclosures, caregivers & schedules
  • Unpredictability = no daily-care routine + frequent enclosure-setup disruption + irregular or continuous light/dark
  • Anticipation is protective — animals learn to expect positive events (esp. feeding) from a set routine

Daily Routine & Predictability

  • Predictability is a welfare intervention — a scheduled 1–2 h daily quiet period lowers ward noise; consistent schedules and anticipation of feeding are protective+
  • Quiet period specifics — 1–2 h each afternoon with public & staff access restricted to the ward significantly lowers kennel noise
  • Trade-off — blanket visitor-access restriction cuts noise BUT may ↑ length of stay & ↓ adoption → prefer the scheduled quiet period over full lockout
  • Efficient, consistently scheduled care reduces barking

The Acoustic Environment

  • Noise-reduction hierarchy — source isolation (machinery away from wards) + structural acoustic panels before behavioural targeting; barking is socially facilitated+
  • Partial visual barriers — reduce triggers, but do NOT fully block visual access (full blocking can worsen barking in some dogs); shelters can exceed 100 dB (decibels)+
  • The 100 dB number — OSHA advises human hearing protection above 100 dB for 2 h/day; dogs hear more acutely & are exposed far longer
  • Barking drivers — over- OR under-stimulation; socially facilitated so target the dogs that repeatedly initiate
  • Placement & handling — reactive dogs away from doors/high-traffic; staff work quietly, no cage/door slamming (esp. during cleaning)
Exam trap To cut excessive ward noise the strongest answers prioritise source isolation (noisy machinery far from wards) + structural acoustic panels BEFORE behavioural measures alone.

Lighting (Physical Plant)

  • Natural light preferred — or artificial light approximating natural in BOTH duration and intensity
  • Track the sun's path so individual enclosures aren't hit by excess sun/overheating through windows or skylights
  • Irregular / continuous light or dark = a form of unpredictability that disrupts circadian rhythm
  • Composition matters, not just brightness — in shelter cats dim blue-depleted light ↓ cortisol by day 5, while plain dim light ↑ hiding (Yaw 2025)
