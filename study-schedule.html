Weekend · 5–8 hrs total

  • Deep diveGo hard on the week's hardest / highest-yield topics — protocols, dosing, decision trees, numbers.

  • Journal8–10 journal abstracts + their MCQs (focal domain) — drill the appraisal moves: design, sample, key stat, "what changed". Rotate in a few biostats / earlier-domain journal Qs to keep them warm.
  • Self-checkRe-test last week's logged errors. Did they stick? Rest the other weekend day if you're fried.

Principles that make this work

Active recall > re-reading

Cover the page and say it out loud, or self-quiz. Reading passively feels productive but barely sticks.

The error log is the engine

Every wrong MCQ goes in a running list with why you missed it. Re-testing those is the single highest-yield hour you'll spend.

Journal questions are a skill

They test critical appraisal — design, bias, the key stat — not memory. Train them like a skill: for every abstract, name the design and the number. That's your top diagnostic gap, so they're in the plan every week.

Spaced review beats cramming

The "spaced review" column drags old domains forward so they're still warm in November — that's why P-Health (done in July) keeps reappearing.

Protect the rest nights

2 rest nights/week is in the plan on purpose. Burnout in September costs you more than the hours you'd save.

Mocks build stamina, not just knowledge

The two full single-sitting sims (31 Oct / 1 Nov) train pacing and focus for a long exam day — treat them like the real thing.

Taper, don't cram

No new material in the last 48 hrs. Skim summaries, sleep well. Cramming the night before trades recall for anxiety.

Falling behind a week? Don't try to claw it all back — shrink the next domain's reading, keep the MCQs. Questions expose gaps faster than re-reading fills them. Week 17 has built-in flex for exactly this.
