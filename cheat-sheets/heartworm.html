Heartworm Disease

Heartworm — Dirofilaria immitis · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Dirofilaria immitis — clinically silent, slow, costly to treat; shelters balance testing, prevention, and selective treatment, with strict exercise restriction the key to preventing fatal pulmonary thromboembolism (PTE).

Life Cycle & Biology

  • Mosquito (intermediate host) ↔ dog (definitive host); cats/ferrets are dead-end, not reservoirs.
  • Prepatent period ~6 months → do not screen before ~7 months of age.
  • mf cannot mature without passing through a mosquito.
  • Wolbachia — essential endosymbiont, passed vertically to mf; the doxycycline target.

Diagnosis: Antigen vs Microfilaria run both

  • Ag test = primary screen; detects adult-FEMALE worm Ag only.
  • mf detected separately by concentration — Modified Knott's.
  • Run BOTH (AHS) — some dogs are Ag−/mf+.
  • Preventives → false-negative Ag from immune complexes; heat-treat serum to unmask.
  • "No Ag" ≠ uninfected; confirm every +Ag before any adulticide.
⚠ Trap: the Ag test detects adult-female worm antigen, NOT mf — mf are found by Knott's. That's why AHS runs both.

Discordant Tests & Look-alike

  • Ag+ / mf+ (patent) — active transmission reservoir; treat, and make mf non-infective before relocation.
  • Ag+ / mf− (prepatent or occult) — still infected, still treat; give an ML or 28-day doxycycline before relocation so a prepatent dog doesn't turn mf+ in transit.
  • Ag− / mf+ (exam trap) — confirm mf are truly D. immitis on Knott's; Ag usually blocked by immune complexes → heat-treat + repeat. Treat as positive.
⚠ Trap — look-alike: non-pathogenic Acanthocheilonema (Dipetalonema) reconditum also circulates mf; distinguish from D. immitis by morphology on Knott's.

Pre-Transport / Safe Relocation AHS · ASV

  • Screen every dog >6 months for both Ag and mf, within 30 days of transport.
  • Make mf non-infective FIRST — single topical moxidectin, or ML + permethrin/dinotefuran/pyriproxyfen spot-on; doxycycline further blocks transmission.
  • Recheck Knott's day 7 — two negatives 7 days apart, then relocate.
  • After melarsomine, do NOT transport for ≥4 weeks — stress/exertion raise fatal PTE risk.
  • Never relocate a positive dog silently — disclose status + plan to the receiving org.

Radiographic Staging

  • Thoracic rads = most objective severity measure, basis for staging.
  • Near-pathognomonic: enlarged, tortuous, truncated pulmonary-artery branches ± parenchymal disease.
  • Severe: right-heart enlargement → classic "reverse D" silhouette.
  • Echo confirms if worms seen, but a poor screening test (light burdens).

AHS Treatment Protocol 3-dose

  • Melarsomine — only FDA-approved adulticide; kills worms ≥120 days old; deep IM into epaxial lumbar (L3–L5).
  • 3-dose split (~99% vs 90.8%): Day 0 ML + doxycycline 10 mg/kg PO BID × 28 d; melarsomine 2.5 mg/kg dose 1 (~Day 60); doses 2 & 3 24 h apart (~Days 90/91).
  • Prednisone taper after each injection — 0.5 mg/kg q12h (wk 1) → q24h (wk 2) → every other day (wks 3–4).
  • Strict cage rest / leash-only throughout AND ≥6–8 weeks after the last injection — top factor preventing fatal PTE.
  • Slow-kill (ML-only) NOT recommended except select cases (prior melarsomine anaphylaxis; grave non-HW prognosis); avoid in non-restrictable dogs / suspected ML resistance.
  • Caval syndrome — heavy burden obstructs tricuspid valve; acute, fatal ~2 days unless worms surgically extracted.
  • 30–120-day gap — MLs kill only worms ≤30 d, melarsomine only ≥120 d; modern view: no gap if you start at diagnosis (delay doesn't improve efficacy).
  • Shelter "moxi-doxy" alt — when melarsomine unavailable: topical moxidectin monthly + doxycycline 10 mg/kg PO q12–24h × 28 d, repeat until Ag−. Slower but far better than none; exercise restriction still applies.

Post-Adulticide Follow-up & Retreatment

  • Two tests, two questions — Knott's = "still a reservoir?"; Ag = "adults gone?" They are decoupled.
  • Retest mf ~4 weeks after last injection (~Day 120); if +, give a microfilaricide and recheck.
  • Microfilaricide is gradual — moxidectin (incl. ProHeart depot) drops counts by ~day 30 but may not clear until ~5 months; persistent low mf = slow clearance, judge by trend. Don't stack a 2nd ML on an active ProHeart depot.
  • Retreatment trigger = 9-month Ag test — AHS moved 6→9 months (2018) because residual Ag from dying worms lingers ~3 months.
  • Ag+ at 9 mo (confirm; heat-treat can unmask): retreat with 30 d doxycycline + 2 doses melarsomine. Ag− at 9 mo = cured, even if mf cleared slowly.
⚠ Trap: retreatment is decided by the 9-month Ag test, NOT mf status — the two are decoupled.

Feline & Ferret Heartworm

  • Cat — low worm burden; no transmission risk. Melarsomine NOT used (fatal pulmonary reaction); most self-cure ~18–48 months. Supportive: prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg/day, tapered, for HARD (heartworm-associated respiratory disease) + monthly prevention. Even one worm's death can cause sudden death — counsel adopters.
  • Feline diagnostics — no single test sensitive; combine antibody + antigen + imaging. Antibody = both sexes from ~2 mo (exposure, not persistence); antigen = adult females only, ~5.5–8 mo; 30–50% of infected cats are Ag−.
  • Screening — NOT indicated in asymptomatic shelter cats; test symptomatic, counsel adopters.
  • Ferret — highly susceptible; a single worm can obstruct the tiny heart. mf transient, Ag often false-negative. Bilirubinuria = most consistent finding.

Prevention ASV PS

  • ASV endorses AHS — year-round prevention for dogs, cats, and ferrets; provide prevention even without screening; treat infected dogs promptly.
  • HW management shouldn't divert resources; a shelter without capacity may transfer infected dogs to a capable partner or release to an informed adopter with follow-up.
  • High-prevalence: screen mf first (cheaper); low-prevalence: Ag first. Cured = two Ag tests 6 months apart negative.
  • MLs (ivermectin, selamectin, milbemycin, moxidectin) reliable only vs worms ≤30 days; DEET unsafe in dogs, cats, ferrets.
  • Permethrin spot-ons block mosquito transmission on relocated dogs.
⚠ Trap: permethrin is NEVER used on cats — severe, potentially fatal toxicity.

📄 Journal Articles

  • Non-arsenical adulticide (moxidectin + doxycycline) — systematic review/meta-analysis: the "moxi-doxy" protocol in 358 dogs (mostly stage 1–2 disease) achieved antigen-negative conversion by ~18 months, with ~99% negative by 12 months and rapid decline in microfilaria counts. Well tolerated — a practical alternative when melarsomine is unavailable/contraindicated (still a slow-kill approach requiring strict exercise restriction).
  • "Do not let your guard down!" — Dirofilaria immitis & D. repens in shelter dogs, northern Italy: heartworm prevalence along the Po river valley rose from ~31% (mid-1990s) to ~98%. Of 510 dogs, 33.9% had circulating microfilariae — 15.7% D. immitis, 6.9% D. repens, 11.4% mixed. Unowned/shelter dogs are an important reservoir that lets D. immitis persist in an area and raises exposure risk for all dogs.
  • Macrocyclic-lactone resistance, Mississippi Delta (southeast Missouri): genomic analysis of D. immitis isolates found most had genotypes consistent with macrocyclic-lactone (ivermectin-class) resistance — of 96 samples, 94.8% resistant, 4.2% mixed, only 1% susceptible. A large resistant population; prevention/treatment protocols in this region should be reconsidered.
  • Prevention & long-term outcomes in primary-care dogs: even lapsed preventive lowered heartworm-positivity risk versus no preventive at all; dogs with lapsed injectable preventive had lower positivity than both no-preventive dogs and those with lapsed monthly products — injectable preventive is more forgiving of gaps.
