Surgery & Anaesthesia

Surgery & Anaesthesia · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Peri-operative safety in the shelter theatre — pre-op risk stratification, breed/drug adjustments, anaesthetic mortality patterns, emergency response, and structured recovery monitoring (recovery is the highest-risk window).

Pre-op Assessment

  • Thorough assessment — assign an ASA (American Society of Anesthesiologists) physical-status category.
  • Pre-anaesthetic bloodwork rarely changes management in healthy young animals — studies suggest bloods were "unnecessary" in the large majority; only ~0.1–0.2% of cases led to a change in the anaesthetic protocol. Risk Factors breed / genetic

  • MDR1 / ABCB1 mutation (multidrug-resistance 1 gene) → drug sensitivity; reduce doses of acepromazine (ACP), butorphanol, and be cautious with ivermectin-class drugs. + check: exact drug list — photo clearly shows reduced ACP + butorphanol; ivermectin-class caution to confirm.
  • Sighthounds (e.g. Greyhound) — hyperlactataemia, stress, and prolonged recovery after barbiturates (thiopental); plan for their low body fat.

Meds to Adjust

  • Antihypertensives — discontinue / withhold, esp. ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors (e.g. benazepril), on the morning of anaesthesia.
  • Insulin — give half the dose 2–4 h before, with feeding.

Anaesthetic Mortality

  • ~<0.05% in dogs, ~0.11% in cats (healthy patients).
  • Deaths cluster ~3 h post-op; 47–60% of anaesthetic deaths occur post-operatively, most within the first 3 h.
⚠ Trap: Recovery is the highest-risk period — monitor closely.

Induction / Intubation

  • Chamber & mask induction NOT recommended — should apply to only ~10–15% of animals (reserve for unhandleable / feral).
  • Intubate for — unhandleable dogs, unknown fasting status, respiratory / pharyngeal disease, and brachycephalic dogs.

Hypoventilation / Apnoea

  • Triggers to act — end-tidal CO₂ (ETCO₂; carbon dioxide) >55 mmHg; respiratory rate (RR) <8/min; apnoea >30 s.
  • Start manual IPPV (intermittent positive-pressure ventilation) — tidal volume 10–15 mL/kg at 8–12 breaths/min.
  • Reversal agents — naloxone (opioids), atipamezole (alpha-2 agonists); doxapram (respiratory stimulant).

CPR cardiopulmonary resuscitation

  • Follow ABC (Airway, Breathing, Circulation).
  • Epinephrine (adrenaline) every 3–5 min.
  • Atropine once for PEA (pulseless electrical activity) / asystole.
  • Ventricular fibrillation (VF) — lidocaine / amiodarone.
  • Reverse any reversible anaesthetic agents.

Pain Scoring

  • CMPS-SF (Glasgow Composite Measure Pain Scale – Short Form), dogs — intervene at ≥6/24. + check: denominator — notes appear to read 6/24 for dogs; confirm the intervention threshold denominator.
  • UNESP-Botucatu scale, cats — intervene at the validated cut-off (~8). + check: cat cut-off — notes appear to read ~8 (possibly 8/24); confirm the validated UNESP-Botucatu threshold.
  • QOL (quality-of-life) scales for dogs & cats.

Post-op Monitoring

  • Check the patient hourly for the first 4 h, then every 2 h.
  • Recovery criteria — BAR (bright, alert, responsive); core temp >37°C (98.6°F); stable heart rate (HR) & respiratory rate; pain score below the intervention threshold; orientated.
  • Ensure the patient can take water without retching before discharge. (Rotate staff / take breaks.)
