Internal Parasites

Internal Parasites · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Shelter endoparasite control protects the population, the public, and each animal's adoptability. High-yield levers: which stage is immediately infective (Giardia, Cryptosporidium) vs needs environmental development; match the drug to the spectrum (fenbendazole misses Dipylidium & Echinococcus → praziquantel); never forget the zoonoses (larva migrans, Cryptosporidium). Heartworm has its own sheet.

Control & Transmission

  • Three shelter concerns — protect population (limit environmental contamination); protect staff/adopters from zoonoses; untreated worms cut adoptability & burden new owners.
  • ASV intake mandate — treat all dogs & cats for roundworms + hookworms at intake, from 2 wk of age; every animal leaves covered vs hook + round. Whipworm/tapeworm = targeted (cost).
  • Broad-spectrum heartworm/flea spot-ons do not replace a primary dewormer.
  • Direct (single host) vs indirect (vector/intermediate host: Dipylidium via flea, Taenia via prey) — indirect can't establish facility-wide without the vector → control the vector.
  • Immediately infective when shed → Giardia, Cryptosporidium, Tritrichomonas. Need environmental development → Toxocara, Ancylostoma, Strongyloides, Trichuris, coccidia, Toxoplasma.
  • Persistence — ascarid eggs nearly indestructible in soil; hookworm eggs die by desiccation; whipworm eggs hardy (bipolar plugs).
⚠ Trap: immediately-infective agents (Giardia/Cryptosporidium) need isolation-level daily sanitation; ascarid/hook/whip/coccidia are shed non-infective → no isolation, but post-treatment feces are heavily contaminating.

Deworming & Formulary

  • Pediatric schedule — puppies from 2 wk, kittens from 3 wk, q2wk to 12 wk, then monthly to 6 mo; treat pregnant/nursing dams.
  • Pyrantel pamoate — hooks + rounds; cheapest; retreat 2 wk then monthly. Safe mainstay.
  • Fenbendazole — hooks, rounds, whips, Giardia, most tapeworms; 50 mg/kg PO q24h × 3–5 d. Misses Dipylidium & Echinococcus.
  • Praziquantel — all tapeworms incl. Dipylidium; 5–7.5 mg/kg.
  • Milbemycin oxime — hook/round/whip plus heartworm prevention.
  • Combos — Drontal Plus (pyrantel + febantel + praziquantel); Profender (emodepside + praziquantel, feline).
  • Reduce T. canis in pups — treat pregnant bitch ivermectin/doramectin day 40–50 gestation (reduces, not eliminates, shedding).

Intestinal Endoparasites nematodes

  • Hookworm (Ancylostoma caninum) — percutaneous/oral/paratenic/transmammary (A. caninum only); prepatent ~2 wk → nursing pups get regenerative anaemia/melena by ~14 d. A. braziliense = most cutaneous larva migrans (US).
  • Roundworm (Toxocara canis/cati, Toxascaris leonina) — ingestion, transplacental (T. canis), transmammary; T. canis prepatent ~5 wk. Leading cause of visceral + ocular larva migrans.
  • Baylisascaris procyonis — raccoon ascarid; fatal neural larva migrans (deadliest).
  • Whipworm (Trichuris vulpis) — caecum/colon; prepatent ~3 mo; barrel egg, bipolar plugs.
  • Strongyloides stercoralis — zoonotic threadworm; percutaneous + transmammary; passes L1 larvae, not eggs → Baermann.
  • Ollulanus tricuspis — feline "vomit worm"; via vomitus not feces → diagnose on vomit.
  • Diagnosis — zinc-sulfate centrifugation (test of choice); intermittent shedding + long prepatent → single negative ≠ clear.
⚠ Trap: larva migrans by agent — Toxocara = visceral/ocular; A. braziliense = cutaneous; Baylisascaris = neural (fatal).

Tapeworms cestodes

  • Dipylidium caninum — flea = intermediate host → flea control controls it; visible rice-grain segments mean review flea control. Not covered by fenbendazole → praziquantel.
  • Taenia — prey intermediate host → unlikely to cycle in a shelter.
  • Echinococcus — significant zoonosis (hydatid disease); not covered by fenbendazole → praziquantel.
⚠ Trap: fenbendazole is broad but misses Dipylidium & Echinococcus — those need praziquantel.

GI Protozoa coccidia

  • Coccidia (Cystoisospora) — host-specific; disease of naive pups/kittens. Ponazuril 20–50 mg/kg PO q24h × 1–3 d is coccidiocidal = drug of choice; sulfadimethoxine only labelled but coccidiostatic. Signs precede oocyst shedding → sick animal, negative float.
  • Cryptosporidium — C. canis/C. felis, host-adapted; oocysts immediately infective. Resistant to bleach/chlorine → 6% hydrogen peroxide, steam >60 °C (140°F), desiccation. Dx modified Ziehl-Neelsen (acid-fast); no reliable drug — hygiene.
  • Toxoplasma gondii — cat = ONLY definitive host; sheds oocysts only ~2 wk acute; oocysts need >24 h to sporulate → daily litter removal clears them; a seropositive cat sheds less; treat clindamycin.
  • Neospora caninum — dog = definitive host (raw beef/venison); NOT zoonotic; congenital pups get ascending flaccid hind-limb paralysis ~3 wk; treat littermates.

Giardia & Tritrichomonas

  • Giardia duodenalis — flagellate; trophozoite (falling-leaf motility, "owl-eyes", ventral disc) + hardy cyst. Cysts immediately infective → rapid ward/fomite spread; reinfection from cysts in the coat.
  • Assemblages — dogs C/D, cats F (host-adapted); A & B potentially zoonotic (low risk).
  • Signs — small-bowel diarrhea (soft, pale, fatty, mucoid); many asymptomatic shedders.
  • Diagnosis — saline wet-mount (trophozoites) + zinc-sulfate flotation (cysts) + antigen ELISA/SNAP. Confirm cure by flotation, NOT antigen (stays antigen-positive after shedding stops).
  • Treat — fenbendazole 50 mg/kg PO q24h × 5 d + environment; treat disease, not shedding; bathe day 1 & end; if persistent repeat the fenbendazole cycle — don't switch to metronidazole (dysbiosis/chronicity). Cysts resist QACs → dry, steam/heat >60–70 °C (140–158°F), or accelerated hydrogen peroxide.
  • Tritrichomonas foetus (cats) — chronic large-bowel diarrhea (fresh blood, mucus, tenesmus) in young congregate cats that look well between bouts. Dx fresh smear (forward rolling, single axostyle), InPouch TF, or PCR; ~22–54% Giardia co-infection. Treat ronidazole 30 mg/kg PO q24h × 14 d (reversible neurotoxicity).
⚠ Trap: metronidazole treats Giardia but NOT Tritrichomonas (use ronidazole) — and it isn't even first-line for Giardia (fenbendazole is).
