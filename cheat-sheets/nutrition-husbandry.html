Nutrition & Husbandry

Nutrition & Husbandry · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Feed to energy needs, score every animal by palpation, and respect the feline metabolic quirks (obligate carnivore, hepatic lipidosis from fasting) that turn routine husbandry into an emergency.

Complete & Balanced Diets (AAFCO)

  • One diet: complete, balanced, life-stage-appropriate; water always; consistency cuts inappetence & GI upset.
  • AAFCO statement: must be current; "all life stages" covers growth + adult + pregnant/nursing.
  • Dog vs cat: dog = practical omnivore (meat-only ≠ balanced); cat = obligate carnivore needing pre-formed taurine, arginine, vitamin A & arachidonic acid.
  • Recalls: pull immediately, never feed.
⚠ Trap: taurine-deficient feline diet → dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) + central retinal degeneration.

Life-Stage & Energy Needs (RER/MER)

  • RER = 70 × kg0.75 kcal/day (linear approx 30×kg + 70 for 2–45 kg).
  • MER = RER × factor: neutered adult 1.6, intact 1.8, growth 2–3×, gestation 1.8–2, lactation 2–6, weight-loss ~1.0.
  • Calorie density: ~350–500 kcal/cup dry; ~120–190 kcal/5.5 oz (156 g) can.
  • Treats: ≤10% of daily calories.

Feeding Management & Schedules

  • Adult dogs: portion-feed ≥1×, ideally 2×/day; large deep-chested breeds ≥2×/day (↓ GDV/bloat).
  • Puppies <6 mo & high-demand (pregnant/nursing/underweight/debilitated): more often or free-choice dry.
  • Adult cats: ≥2 meals/day (prefer small frequent); meal-feed to monitor intake.
  • Group-housed cats: free-choice dry practical (canned spoils) but non-eaters missed for days.
  • Cohoused: feed separately or monitor for guarding/bullying.
  • Turnover: discard uneaten food ≥q24h; contaminated/pest-infested immediately.

Body Condition Score (1–9)

  • Scale: 9-point — 1–3 under, 4–5 ideal (target 5/9), 6–9 over-to-obese.
  • 5/9: ribs palpable not visible; waist + abdominal tuck.
  • Palpate, don't eyeball: coat hides ribs; in cats palpate the caudal-abdomen fat pad.
  • Surgical gate: BCS ≤3/9 → vet clearance before GA (low reserves, hypothermia); stabilise 1–2 first.
  • Monitor: weigh & score at intake, weekly if stay >7 days; flag if drops ≥1 point.
  • Obesity: BCS 6–7 → cut calories 20–25%; 8–9 → program at RER of target weight, recheck q2wk. Safe loss 1–2% BW/week.

Anorexia, Refeeding & Hepatic Lipidosis

  • Thresholds: adult cat off food 3–5 days → hepatic lipidosis risk; kitten <4 mo off food >24 h → hypoglycaemia, death.
  • Food aversion: nauseated cats form aversion — offer periodically, don't leave in cage.
  • Refeeding syndrome: refeed a starved animal too fast → phosphate/potassium/magnesium shifts → weakness, seizures, death; reintroduce slowly + monitor electrolytes.
  • Obese-cat trap: never fast aggressively — rapid fat mobilisation outpaces the liver → lipidosis; aim 1–2%/week. Signs: jaundice, vomiting, lethargy, ↑ ALT/ALP + bilirubin.
⚠ Trap: BCS 8/9 cat eating little for 5 days — wrong answer "continue caloric restriction"; correct = vet exam + nutritional support.

Neonatal & Orphan Feeding

  • Rewarm first: never feed a hypothermic neonate (core <94°F/34.4°C) — cold gut ferments milk → endotoxaemia; rewarm first.
  • Ambient: wk 1 ~85–95°F (29–35°C), −5°F (2.8°C)/wk to 70–75°F (21–24°C) by wk 4.
  • Cadence: KMR (never cow's milk) q2–3 h first days, q4–5 h to 3 wk, 4×/day to 6 wk; wean 3–4 wk on warm slurry.
  • Volume: stomach capacity ≈ 40–50 mL/kg per feed — never exceed (→ fatal aspiration).
  • Orphans: weigh daily wk 1 + stimulate elimination; birth ~100 g, gain 10–15 g/day (<7 inadequate); screen dam for FeLV/FIV.
  • Fading kitten: hypothermia → ileus → endotoxaemia → hypoglycaemia (<60 mg/dL) + dehydration → collapse; rule out panleukopenia.

Reproduction & Kitten Age Estimation

  • Feline basics: queens seasonal, induced ovulators; gestation ~65 days, 3–5 kittens; puberty queens 5–9 mo, toms 8–13 mo.
  • Pregnancy dx: palpation d14–17; ultrasound d10; relaxin ↑ d25; radiographs after skull calcification ~d43.
  • Pound-a-month: ~1 lb (0.5 kg)/month → 3 lb (1.4 kg) kitten ≈ 3 mo; eyes open 7–10 days, walk/play 3–4 wk.
  • Dentition (heavily tested): deciduous erupt ~3–4 wk (I1→I2→I3); adult canines ~5.5–6 mo; permanent canines + small testes ≈ 6–9 mo; mature testes ≈ 12–14 mo.
  • Housing limit: kittens <6–8 wk not housed in shelter (infection risk) → foster.

Food & Water Safety / Storage

  • Water: cats ~50 mL/kg/day, dogs ~50–80; dry-only cats need ~2× (wet food = 70–80% water) → concentrated urine, urinary-disease risk.
  • Dehydration: ≥5% = skin tent >1–2 s, tacky MM, sunken eyes; ≥10% = depression, weak pulse, prolonged CRT.
  • Dry storage: sealed pest-proof bins, date opened, use within maker's window.
  • Wet/canned: refrigerate opened cans; discard portions after 2 h at room temp (4–6 h max, feral protocol).
  • No raw diets: ASV advises against — Salmonella/Campylobacter shedding, staff/immunocompromised risk, rarely balanced.
  • Bowl hygiene: individual bowls; scrub daily; wash-disinfect-rinse between occupants.

Housing & Husbandry Basics

  • Cat units: each needs ≥1 hide box + elevated perch; hiding ↓cortisol (a solution — don't remove it); settle in 2–5 wk.
  • Feral/fractious cats: squeeze panel + hide box, service from safe side; disposition = TNRM, don't hold long-term.
  • Geriatric & grooming: soft warm bedding for orthopaedic disease; regular grooming limits faecal-oral spread (giardia, parvo).
  • Rabbits & exotics: see their own cheatsheets — this sheet is dogs & cats.

Shelter Toxicology

  • Feline basis: glucuronidation deficiency (UGT1A6 pseudogene) slows phenol, aspirin/salicylate & acetaminophen clearance; drives permethrin sensitivity.
  • Permethrin/pyrethroids: dog spot-on on cats → tremors, fasciculations, hyperthermia, seizures.
  • Analgesics: acetaminophen → methaemoglobinaemia + Heinz-body anaemia; aspirin & ibuprofen also unsafe.
  • Household/plant: onions (incl. onion-powder baby food) → haemolytic anaemia; lilies → nephrotoxic to cats.
  • Disinfectant residue: QAC, phenols & pine oils groomed off fur → oral ulceration, hypersalivation, anorexia; avoid rodenticides (relay toxicity).
⚠ Trap: dog permethrin spot-on on a cat — mechanism answer is the feline glucuronidation deficiency, not receptor density or haemoglobin fragility.
