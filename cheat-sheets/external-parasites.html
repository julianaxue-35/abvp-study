External Parasites

External Parasites · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Ectoparasites drive shelter dermatology, anaemia, and zoonotic + vector-borne disease. High-yield levers: know the host-specific few (lice, Demodex) vs opportunistic feeders; treat the environment (flea pupa, brown dog tick indoors); remove ticks promptly; and flag the zoonoses — Sarcoptes, Notoedres, Cheyletiella, flea-borne Bartonella/plague/typhus.

Why PPE for only the first 3–5 days (Sarcoptes): a modern acaricide kills the mites within a few days, so the treated animal stops shedding infective mites by ~day 3–5 (no longer contagious to handlers/other animals); the mite survives only a few days off the host; and the human infestation self-resolves — so isolation/PPE can safely be relaxed after ~3–5 days of effective treatment.
⚠ Trap: Demodex canis is not contagious between adult dogs (dam-to-pup only) and non-zoonotic, whereas Sarcoptes is highly contagious + zoonotic — and D. gatoi IS contagious between cats. Commonly reversed on the exam.
⚠ Trap: isoxazolines (afoxolaner / fluralaner / sarolaner / lotilaner) are the modern one-drug answer — a single oral class covers fleas, ticks, Sarcoptes, Demodex, Otodectes & Cheyletiella. If a stem offers a single agent for "mange + fleas + ticks," it's an isoxazoline.

Ticks & Tick-Borne

  • Life cycle / vector role — egg→larva→nymph→adult; most are 3-host ticks (feed once per stage, moult in the environment). Vector of Babesia, Ehrlichia, Anaplasma, Cytauxzoon, Borrelia (Lyme), Hepatozoon.
  • Brown dog tick (Rhipicephalus sanguineus) — the shelter exception: all stages feed on dogs and it completes its whole cycle indoors → often needs professional pest control. Other ticks (Ixodes, Dermacentor, Amblyomma) need rodent/bird larval hosts → won't persist indoors.
  • Removal & emerging — fine-tipped forceps (never bare hands), grasp close to skin, steady traction; many pathogens (e.g. Lyme) need feeding time → early removal prevents infection. Emerging: Asian longhorned tick (Haemaphysalis longicornis), US since 2017.

Fleas & Flea Control

  • Ctenocephalides felis — cat flea; commonest flea on dogs too.
  • Life cycle — egg→larva→pupa→adult; only the adult lives on the host. ~95% of the population (eggs/larvae/pupae) is OFF the host in the environment; pupa is the hardiest stage (persists months, ecloses on heat/CO₂/vibration).
  • Transmission — acquired from the environment, not host-to-host jumping.
  • Disease / age — flea-bite anaemia (young/old/debilitated); FAD (flea-allergy dermatitis) → lumbosacral/tail-base alopecia.
  • Vector role — intermediate host of Dipylidium caninum; transmits Bartonella (cat-scratch — zoonotic), Rickettsia felis/typhi (typhus), haemotropic Mycoplasma, Yersinia pestis (plague), Francisella tularensis.
  • Treatment — adulticide + insect-growth regulator + environmental control; nitenpyram for instant adult kill at intake; isoxazolines / imidacloprid / selamectin.
⚠ Trap: the flea is the intermediate host of Dipylidium caninum — treat fleas and deworm. Because fleas come from the environment (pupa hardiest), always pair adulticide with environmental control.

Lice host-specific

  • Host specificity — strictly host-specific (only lice & Demodex are); survive only days off-host. Cats carry one louse (Felicola subrostratus).
  • Life cycle — entire cycle ON the host (egg/nit→nymph→adult); nits glued to hair are NOT killed by insecticides.
  • Transmission — direct contact + fomites (shared grooming tools, bedding).
  • Chewing (Mallophaga — Trichodectes canis, also a Dipylidium vector) vs sucking (Anoplura — Linognathus setosus, anaemia in heavy burdens).
  • Treatment — isoxazolines / topical (fipronil, selamectin); mechanically remove nits; treat ≥2× a week apart + separate infested animals.

Vector-Borne & Zoonotic

  • Vector map — ticks: Anaplasma, Lyme, Ehrlichia, RMSF, Cytauxzoon, Babesia, Hepatozoon. Fleas: Bartonella, typhus (Rickettsia felis/typhi), plague (Yersinia pestis). Sandfly: Leishmania. Kissing bug: Trypanosoma cruzi.
  • Chagas (Trypanosoma cruzi) — southern US; myocarditis, right-sided cardiomegaly; stercorarian (fecal) transmission from the kissing bug or by ingestion.
  • Leishmania infantum — sandfly vector; also vertical/blood-borne in US Foxhounds; zoonotic reservoir.
  • Personnel safety — gloves + covering clothing for the first 3–5 d of mange treatment; clean shared gear; remove standing water, garbage, decaying matter & long grass to limit vector breeding.
⚠ Trap: only lice & Demodex are strictly host-specific — every other arthropod here is an opportunistic feeder, so environmental & vector control matter as much as treating the animal.
