Rabbits

Rabbits · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Rabbits are highly stress-sensitive prey animals for whom shelter life is fundamentally hard — the ASV (Association of Shelter Veterinarians) 2026 Guidelines for Humane Rabbit Housing set the exam-tested standards for space, light, noise, co-housing and RHDV sanitation, while their unique hindgut-fermenting GI tract dictates diet and drug choices.

Prey-Species Stress & Shelter Fit

  • Domestic rabbit = Oryctolagus cuniculus, a burrowing, social prey species — shelter housing is hard to reconcile with these instincts.
  • Highly stress-sensitive — sight, sound & smell of predators (dogs, cats, ferrets) impose major physiological stress. ~100 million olfactory cells (vs ~30 million in humans) → predator scent alone triggers fear.
  • Housing must fulfil the Five Freedoms & Five Domains; shelter housing is a last resort — try owner self-rehoming, foster, rescue/boarding, or community spay/neuter-return first.
  • Fewer units → larger enclosures + more attention → faster outcomes. Housing MORE rabbits does not increase adoptions — it raises average length of stay (ALOS) & cost.
  • It is inhumane to keep a rabbit in constant fear/anxiety/stress → act immediately: transfer, return-to-field, offsite housing, or humane euthanasia.
⚠ Trap: Do NOT cycle air from dog/cat areas into rabbit housing — predator scent in recycled air causes stress even with no visual contact.

Housing & Space ASV 2026

  • Min RECOMMENDED (stays >2 wk, exercise built in) — single 33 ft²/3 m² (length 9–10 ft/3 m, width ≥3 ft/0.9 m); pair 45 ft²/4 m² (width ≥5 ft/1.5 m). Unobstructed run 3 m/9.8 ft; open-top wall ≥1.25 m/4.1 ft.
  • Min ACCEPTABLE (<2 wk ONLY, no built-in exercise) — single 8–11 ft²/0.8–1 m²; caregivers must give separate daily exercise.
  • 4 enclosure components — darkened hide (rest fully outstretched) · litter box ≥1 per rabbit with hay access · eat/drink area (open dish, away from litter) · exercise (stand on hind legs + 3 unimpeded hops).
  • Materials non-toxic, predator-proof, non-porous, quiet latches; wood not recommended.
⚠ Trap: Slatted/grid/WIRE floors must NOT be used → pododermatitis (sore hocks); cover slippery floors with rubber/foam/grass mats.
⚠ Trap: "Minimum acceptable" is NOT the standard of care — temporary short-stay only + daily supplemental exercise; longer stays need the recommended 33 ft²/3 m².

Bacterial Diseases

  • Pasteurella multocida ("snuffles") — most common isolate (>50%); G-neg rod; pneumonia, otitis externa/media/interna (OE/OM/OI → head tilt), abscesses, conjunctivitis, endocarditis, pyometra; triggered by stress/overcrowding/poor sanitation. Culture — don't assume Pasteurella.
  • Bordetella bronchiseptica — cross-species; rabbits are asymptomatic reservoirs that infect guinea pigs → never co-house rabbits with guinea pigs.
  • Enteropathogenic E. coli (EPEC) — outbreaks in weaning/commercial rabbits; GI signs.
  • Tyzzer's disease (Clostridium piliforme) — weanlings; acute diarrhoea + sudden death; ante-mortem dx hard; necropsy = patchy necrosis of liver + proximal colon + myocardium.
  • Lawsonia intracellularis — weanlings 2–4 mo; proliferative enteropathy / GI signs.
  • Rabbit syphilis (Treponema paraluiscuniculi) — spirochaete; host-specific, NOT zoonotic; crusting ulceration/scabs on face + anogenital region; treat ALL exposed rabbits with injectable penicillin G.
  • Tularemia (Francisella tularensis, "rabbit fever") — flea/tick-borne; ZOONOTIC + reportable; fever, neuro signs, death.
⚠ Trap: Oral (enteral) penicillin is FATAL in rabbits — disrupts the cecal microbiome → Clostridium enterotoxaemia. But injectable penicillin G IS used for Treponema syphilis. Safe antibiotics: doxycycline, trimethoprim-sulfa (TMS), fluoroquinolones.

Light · Noise · Ventilation ASV 2026

  • Lighting — min ambient 3–5 footcandles (FC) ≈ 30–50 lux; dimmable; full darkness at night with light-blocking hides. Rabbits are crepuscular — a disrupted light/dark cycle → poor stress adaptation, altered immunity, shorter lifespan.
  • Noise — prolonged 50–70 dB (decibels) is detrimental (conversation ≈50 dB, vacuum ≈70 dB; shelters often exceed 100 dB). Controls: distance from dog housing, quiet latches, music off at night.
  • UVB (ultraviolet B) — indoor rabbits risk vitamin D deficiency; sun-dried hay gives some, supplemental UVB raises blood vitamin D; always allow retreat from UV.
  • Climate — 59–72°F/15–22°C (heat stress >86°F/30°C); humidity 30–70% relative humidity; ventilation ≥10 air changes/hour. Misters NOT recommended (damp fur → dermatitis/flystrike).
⚠ Trap: Motion-activated lighting is specifically CONTRAINDICATED — night-active rabbits trigger the lights and destroy the dark phase of their circadian rhythm.

Co-housing & Handling ASV 2026

  • Rabbits are social — avoid single housing where possible (single-housed → wall-pawing, bar-gnawing, GI dysfunction). Preferred group size 2–3.
  • Never mix unfamiliar rabbits for shared exercise. Each rabbit needs its own hide, platform, water & enrichment; co-housed enclosure width ≥1.5 m/5 ft.
  • Length of stay (LOS) — bonded pairs stay longer than singles → list as singles for adoption unless already bonded.
  • Handling — ALWAYS support the hindquarters (unsupported kicking → vertebral fracture/luxation). Whiskers very sensitive; rabbits dislike having the nose touched.
⚠ Trap: All group-housed rabbits must be spayed/neutered (SN) — NEVER co-house intact adults (fighting → serious injury/death). Keep opposite-sex pairs separated ≥2 weeks after sterilization.

Ectoparasites

  • Fleas — use imidacloprid or selamectin. NEVER fipronil (toxic to rabbits).
  • Ticks — can transmit myxomatosis & tularemia; ivermectin 0.2–0.4 mg/kg SC q2wk ×2–3.
  • Lice.
  • Ear mites (Psoroptes cuniculi) & fur mites (Cheyletiella) — ivermectin / selamectin. Cheyletiella is zoonotic.
⚠ Trap: Fipronil (Frontline), steroids/dexamethasone, and dog/cat flea-tick products must NOT be used on rabbits.

Endoparasites

  • Coccidia (Eimeria spp.) — hepatic coccidiosis can be FATAL in young rabbits (<6 mo), obstructing liver function + GI signs; tx TMS, ponazuril, amprolium.
  • Cryptosporidiosis — zoonotic; self-limiting diarrhoea 4–5 d in young rabbits → PPE + handwashing.
  • Pinworms (Passalurus) — common, non-pathogenic; benzimidazole if needed.
  • Taenia — rabbit is the INTERMEDIATE host (cysticercus in SQ/muscle); praziquantel 5–10 mg/kg, repeat in 10 d.
  • Encephalitozoon cuniculi — microsporidian; neuro signs: head tilt, hindlimb paresis, uveitis, cataract, renal failure. Dwarf breed + head tilt → E. cuniculi; standard breed + head tilt → Pasteurella/otitis. Dx: IgM + IgG + CRP panel (IgM = active infection); histopath definitive. Tx fenbendazole 7.5–20 mg/kg PO ×30 d + NSAIDs. Zoonotic to immunocompromised people.

Diet & GI Health

  • Hindgut fermenters — hay-based (timothy/grass) available at all times; alfalfa only for pregnant/lactating/growing rabbits (high calcium). Pellets ~¼ cup per 5 lb/2.3 kg; water 120–150 mL/kg/day. Cecotrophs (soft feces eaten from the anus) = essential nutrition, not disease.
  • Dental — continuously growing teeth → malocclusion common; formula I 2/1 C 0/0 P 3/2 M 3/3 = 28 (small 2nd upper incisor = "peg tooth"); a thorough oral exam is essential.
  • GI (gastrointestinal) stasis — anorexia + weight loss ± diarrhoea → ALWAYS check the teeth first; often precipitated by improper antibiotics (dysbiosis).
  • Urine "sludge" — high dietary calcium (alfalfa) + vitamin-D-independent calcium absorption → bladder sand. Red/orange urine usually porphyrins (fluoresces under Wood's lamp), NOT true haematuria.
  • Uterine adenocarcinoma in 50–80% of intact does >3–4 yr → spay all non-breeding does at 6–9 months.

Viral Diseases (RHDV · Myxo)

  • Myxomatosis (Leporipoxvirus) — often fatal in domestic rabbits; fever, lethargy, swollen eyelids, ocular/nasal discharge, skin lesions/haemorrhage; spread by fleas & mosquitoes → wear PPE.
  • RHDV 1 & 2 (calicivirus, non-enveloped) — REPORTABLE; hepatic necrosis + DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation), ~40–100% mortality (see Sanitation & RHDV card).
  • Vaccination — US has no routine vaccine; Europe uses myxomatosis + RHDV vaccines. RHDV2 vaccine protective >14 d after the 2nd booster (≈35 d after the initial dose); e.g. Cunical, Eravac.
  • Quarantine — 14 d if RHD is reported locally or after outdoor exposure.

Sanitation & RHDV

  • RHDV (rabbit haemorrhagic disease virus, types 1 & 2) — REPORTABLE non-enveloped calicivirus; affects only European/domestic rabbits >2 months (neonates resistant); replicates in liver → hepatic necrosis + disseminated intravascular coagulation; mortality ~40–100%.
  • Sudden death with blood from orifices — but absence of bleeding does NOT rule it out. No US-licensed vaccine; treatment not recommended → isolate + full personal protective equipment.
  • Cleaning order — healthy kits → healthy adults → unhealthy → RHDV quarantine LAST; separate supplies for healthy vs sick. Acetic acid (vinegar, pH 2–3) breaks down alkaline mineral deposits.
  • Intake quarantine = risk-assessment based (not routine) — at-risk = outdoor/feral exposure <10 d, unvaccinated population, or no proof of vaccination → 10-day quarantine; skip if it would lengthen stay for a single rabbit.
⚠ Trap: RHDV is non-enveloped → many routine disinfectants FAIL (standard quaternary ammonium + some phenolics insufficient). Use accelerated hydrogen peroxide, potassium peroxymonosulfate, or 0.5% sodium hypochlorite.

📄 Journal Articles

  • ASV Guidelines for humane rabbit housing in shelters: establishes minimum space and environmental standards — enabling natural behaviours, hiding/enrichment, appropriate light cycles for crepuscular rabbits, and flooring that reduces pododermatitis.
  • Sheltering domestic rabbits in Canada/USA during COVID-19 & RHDV2 (2017–2022): organisations frequently used resource-intensive practices (foster care, waiting lists, transfers). Declined surrenders rose from 67.1% to 88.5% and waitlists from 59% to 80%; COVID caused decreased intakes/adoptions plus concurrent operational challenges (RHDV2) and sustained resource constraints.
  • Run access, hutch size & time-of-day (pair-housed pet rabbits, faecal corticosterone): guidelines should let rabbits exercise outside the midday period; commonly-reported ~0.75 m² hutches should NOT be recommended for rabbit pairs, even with 3 h/day midday run access.
  • "Starter-pet" perception harms rabbit welfare (Danish surveys): owners who saw rabbits as starter pets or had lower willingness to pay were more likely to use restricted housing and to withhold continuous gnawing opportunities, ad-lib hay and routine health care; a child as main caretaker was linked to inadequate housing and fewer gnawing opportunities — owner education + minimum-requirement standards are key.
  • Genotype × housing on group-housed doe aggression/injuries (smallholding): the housing × genotype interaction significantly affected aggression; lowest aggression in Mecklenburg does on ground soil, with fewer injuries in does and kits and lower kit mortality.
  • Epidemiology of behavioural problems in pet rabbits (owner survey): commonest problems were inappropriate elimination, then non-social fear, destructive behaviour and compulsive disorders (often >1 per rabbit); more common in intact females, in rabbits acquired before/around weaning, and from breeders/pet stores.
  • Most prevalent welfare issues in pet small mammals: the most impactful perceived issue was small/inadequate housing for every species except the rat, where respiratory disease was the main concern.
  • Abandonment & rehoming of rabbits/rodents, Madrid (2008–2021): of 1,009 animals in a foster network, 46.4% rabbits and 19.7% hamsters; 51.2% arrived with health issues and 41.3% were abandoned (mostly urban); 686 were adopted (mean 9.2 months) and 323 died before adoption.
  • Rabbit café visitor experience & adoption interest: rabbits were rated highly social and social individuals were preferred over shy ones; the café was an enjoyable venue for human–animal contact but produced no adoptions during the study.
  • Blindfolding & swaddling for handling stress (rabbits): neither blindfolding nor swaddling significantly changed heart or respiratory rate; large between-individual variation in baseline suggests prior handling experience is the key determinant.
