Rabbits

Rabbits · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Rabbits are highly stress-sensitive prey animals for whom shelter life is fundamentally hard — the ASV (Association of Shelter Veterinarians) 2026 Guidelines for Humane Rabbit Housing set the exam-tested standards for space, light, noise, co-housing and RHDV sanitation, while their unique hindgut-fermenting GI tract dictates diet and drug choices.

Prey-Species Stress & Shelter Fit

  • Domestic rabbit = Oryctolagus cuniculus, a burrowing, social prey species — shelter housing is hard to reconcile with these instincts.
  • Highly stress-sensitive — sight, sound & smell of predators (dogs, cats, ferrets) impose major physiological stress. ~100 million olfactory cells (vs ~30 million in humans) → predator scent alone triggers fear.
  • Housing must fulfil the Five Freedoms & Five Domains; shelter housing is a last resort — try owner self-rehoming, foster, rescue/boarding, or community spay/neuter-return first.
  • Fewer units → larger enclosures + more attention → faster outcomes. Housing MORE rabbits does not increase adoptions — it raises average length of stay (ALOS) & cost.
  • It is inhumane to keep a rabbit in constant fear/anxiety/stress → act immediately: transfer, return-to-field, offsite housing, or humane euthanasia.
⚠ Trap: Do NOT cycle air from dog/cat areas into rabbit housing — predator scent in recycled air causes stress even with no visual contact.

Housing & Space ASV 2026

  • Min RECOMMENDED (stays >2 wk, exercise built in) — single 33 ft²/3 m² (length 9–10 ft/3 m, width ≥3 ft/0.9 m); pair 45 ft²/4 m² (width ≥5 ft/1.5 m). Unobstructed run 3 m/9.8 ft; open-top wall ≥1.25 m/4.1 ft.
  • Min ACCEPTABLE (<2 wk ONLY, no built-in exercise) — single 8–11 ft²/0.8–1 m²; caregivers must give separate daily exercise.
  • 4 enclosure components — darkened hide (rest fully outstretched) · litter box ≥1 per rabbit with hay access · eat/drink area (open dish, away from litter) · exercise (stand on hind legs + 3 unimpeded hops).
  • Materials non-toxic, predator-proof, non-porous, quiet latches; wood not recommended.
⚠ Trap: Slatted/grid/WIRE floors must NOT be used → pododermatitis (sore hocks); cover slippery floors with rubber/foam/grass mats.
⚠ Trap: "Minimum acceptable" is NOT the standard of care — temporary short-stay only + daily supplemental exercise; longer stays need the recommended 33 ft²/3 m².

Light · Noise · Ventilation ASV 2026

  • Lighting — min ambient 3–5 footcandles (FC) ≈ 30–50 lux; dimmable; full darkness at night with light-blocking hides. Rabbits are crepuscular — a disrupted light/dark cycle → poor stress adaptation, altered immunity, shorter lifespan.
  • Noise — prolonged 50–70 dB (decibels) is detrimental (conversation ≈50 dB, vacuum ≈70 dB; shelters often exceed 100 dB). Controls: distance from dog housing, quiet latches, music off at night.
  • UVB (ultraviolet B) — indoor rabbits risk vitamin D deficiency; sun-dried hay gives some, supplemental UVB raises blood vitamin D; always allow retreat from UV.
  • Climate — 59–72°F/15–22°C (heat stress >86°F/30°C); humidity 30–70% relative humidity; ventilation ≥10 air changes/hour. Misters NOT recommended (damp fur → dermatitis/flystrike).
⚠ Trap: Motion-activated lighting is specifically CONTRAINDICATED — night-active rabbits trigger the lights and destroy the dark phase of their circadian rhythm.

Co-housing & Handling ASV 2026

  • Rabbits are social — avoid single housing where possible (single-housed → wall-pawing, bar-gnawing, GI dysfunction). Preferred group size 2–3.
  • Never mix unfamiliar rabbits for shared exercise. Each rabbit needs its own hide, platform, water & enrichment; co-housed enclosure width ≥1.5 m/5 ft.
  • Length of stay (LOS) — bonded pairs stay longer than singles → list as singles for adoption unless already bonded.
  • Handling — ALWAYS support the hindquarters (unsupported kicking → vertebral fracture/luxation). Whiskers very sensitive; rabbits dislike having the nose touched.
⚠ Trap: All group-housed rabbits must be spayed/neutered (SN) — NEVER co-house intact adults (fighting → serious injury/death). Keep opposite-sex pairs separated ≥2 weeks after sterilization.

Diet & GI Health

  • Hindgut fermenters — hay-based (timothy/grass) available at all times; alfalfa only for pregnant/lactating/growing rabbits (high calcium). Pellets ~¼ cup per 5 lb/2.3 kg; water 120–150 mL/kg/day. Cecotrophs (soft feces eaten from the anus) = essential nutrition, not disease.
  • Dental — continuously growing teeth → malocclusion common; formula I 2/1 C 0/0 P 3/2 M 3/3 = 28 (small 2nd upper incisor = "peg tooth"); a thorough oral exam is essential.
  • GI (gastrointestinal) stasis — anorexia + weight loss ± diarrhoea → ALWAYS check the teeth first; often precipitated by improper antibiotics (dysbiosis).
  • Urine "sludge" — high dietary calcium (alfalfa) + vitamin-D-independent calcium absorption → bladder sand. Red/orange urine usually porphyrins (fluoresces under Wood's lamp), NOT true haematuria.
  • Uterine adenocarcinoma in 50–80% of intact does >3–4 yr → spay all non-breeding does at 6–9 months.

Key Diseases & Drug Cautions

  • Pasteurella multocida — most common isolate (>50%); "snuffles," pneumonia, otitis, head tilt; emerges with stress/crowding. In respiratory disease, culture — don't assume Pasteurella.
  • Encephalitozoon cuniculi — intracellular microsporidian (a fungus); the dwarf-breed head tilt/vestibular sign (standard-breed head tilt is more likely Pasteurella/otitis). No reliable cure.
  • Bordetella — rabbits are asymptomatic reservoirs that infect guinea pigs → never co-house rabbits with guinea pigs.
⚠ Trap: Oral (enteral) penicillin is FATAL in rabbits — disrupts the cecal microbiome → Clostridium enterotoxaemia. For rabbit syphilis (Treponema) use injectable penicillin G.
⚠ Trap: Fipronil (Frontline), steroids/dexamethasone, and dog/cat flea-tick products must NOT be used on rabbits. Safe antibiotics: doxycycline, trimethoprim-sulfa (TMS), fluoroquinolones.

Sanitation & RHDV

  • RHDV (rabbit haemorrhagic disease virus, types 1 & 2) — REPORTABLE non-enveloped calicivirus; affects only European/domestic rabbits >2 months (neonates resistant); replicates in liver → hepatic necrosis + disseminated intravascular coagulation; mortality ~40–100%.
  • Sudden death with blood from orifices — but absence of bleeding does NOT rule it out. No US-licensed vaccine; treatment not recommended → isolate + full personal protective equipment.
  • Cleaning order — healthy kits → healthy adults → unhealthy → RHDV quarantine LAST; separate supplies for healthy vs sick. Acetic acid (vinegar, pH 2–3) breaks down alkaline mineral deposits.
  • Intake quarantine = risk-assessment based (not routine) — at-risk = outdoor/feral exposure <10 d, unvaccinated population, or no proof of vaccination → 10-day quarantine; skip if it would lengthen stay for a single rabbit.
⚠ Trap: RHDV is non-enveloped → many routine disinfectants FAIL (standard quaternary ammonium + some phenolics insufficient). Use accelerated hydrogen peroxide, potassium peroxymonosulfate, or 0.5% sodium hypochlorite.
