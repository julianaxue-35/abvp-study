Intestinal Worms (Helminths)

Intestinal Worms · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

For every worm the exam wants five things: life cycle (DIRECT = no intermediate host — faecal-oral / larval / percutaneous / trans-mammary; INDIRECT = requires a named intermediate host), the transmission route, the age/species most affected, the drug, and the zoonosis. High-yield: puppies & kittens get hook + round vertically (trans-mammary ± trans-placental); whipworm is an adult-dog disease; every tapeworm is indirect (flea / prey / offal) and needs praziquantel — fenbendazole misses Dipylidium & Echinococcus. Heartworm has its own sheet.

Nematodes — Roundworms soil / vertical

  • Hookworm (Ancylostoma caninum, A. braziliense; Uncinaria stenocephala): LC: DIRECT (no intermediate host). Tx: percutaneous skin penetration, oral, paratenic hosts, trans-mammary (A. caninum milk only). Age: nursing puppies/kittens → regenerative anaemia + melena by ~14 d; prepatent ~2 wk. Dx: zinc-sulfate centrifugation. Tx drug: pyrantel / fenbendazole / milbemycin. ZOONOTIC → cutaneous larva migrans (A. braziliense most). (Uncinaria = "northern hookworm", cooler climates, mainly oral, no trans-mammary, less pathogenic.)
  • Roundworm / ascarid (Toxocara canis & T. cati, Toxascaris leonina): LC: DIRECT (paratenic hosts amplify; egg needs environmental development to become infective). Tx: egg ingestion; trans-placental (T. canis); trans-mammary (T. canis in dogs, T. cati in cats). Age: puppies/kittens — T. canis in-utero pups shed by ~3 wk; prepatent ~5 wk. Tx drug: pyrantel / fenbendazole. ZOONOTIC → visceral + ocular larva migrans (T. canis leading cause). (Toxascaris leonina = ingestion only, NO vertical route.)
  • Whipworm (Trichuris vulpis): LC: DIRECT. Tx: ingest embryonated egg from soil (needs environmental development); caecum/colon. Age: ADULT dogs (not pups) — large-bowel diarrhoea, hematochezia, tenesmus; can mimic hypoadrenocorticism (pseudo-Addison). Prepatent ~3 mo (long); barrel egg with bipolar plugs, hardy in soil. Tx drug: fenbendazole or milbemycin — pyrantel does NOT cover whipworm. Zoonosis: low (human whipworm a separate species).
⚠ Trap: Toxocara vertical routes — T. canis (dogs) = trans-placental + trans-mammary; T. cati (cats) = trans-mammary ONLY (no trans-placental). Pups can be born already infected; kittens are infected through the milk.
⚠ Trap: whipworm = adult-dog disease with a 3-month prepatent period; a single negative float doesn't clear it. Pyrantel (the cheap hook/round dewormer) misses whipworm — need fenbendazole/milbemycin.

Nematodes — Larval & Lung Baermann

  • Strongyloides stercoralis (threadworm): LC: DIRECT. Tx: percutaneous larvae (mature in warm damp soiled runs) + trans-mammary from an acutely infected dam; auto-infection possible. Age: young dogs in wet/soiled pens. Dx: passes L1 larvae, not eggs → Baermann funnel. Tx drug: ivermectin 0.2 mg/kg SC (repeat 1 wk) or fenbendazole 5–14 d. ZOONOTIC (a true direct zoonosis — develops in humans too).
  • Feline lungworm (Aelurostrongylus abstrusus): LC: INDIRECT — intermediate host = slug / snail → paratenic host (rodent, bird) → cat. Tx: cat eats infected snail/paratenic host; eggs coughed up, swallowed, passed in faeces. Age: cats, worse in kittens — LRT disease (cough, dyspnoea), mimics feline asthma; possible anaesthetic death. Dx: Baermann (larvae). Tx drug: fenbendazole or topical macrocyclic lactone (moxidectin/emodepside/selamectin). Not zoonotic.
⚠ Trap: match lungworm to its faecal test — Aelurostrongylus → Baermann (larvae); Capillaria → flotation (eggs); Paragonimus → sedimentation (heavy fluke eggs). Wrong technique misses it.

Cestodes — Tapeworms all indirect

  • Dipylidium caninum (flea tapeworm): LC: INDIRECT — intermediate host = FLEA (or chewing louse). Tx: dog/cat ingests an infected flea while grooming. Age: any dog/cat with fleas — rice-grain proglottids at anus/in bedding. Tx drug: praziquantel (NOT covered by fenbendazole). Cure = flea control, not just deworming. Zoonosis: rare (child swallows a flea).
  • Taenia spp.: LC: INDIRECT — intermediate host = prey (rodents, rabbits, ruminants/offal). Tx: ingest cysticercus in intermediate-host tissue (hunting / raw meat). Age: hunting/roaming dogs & cats — unlikely to cycle inside a shelter. Tx drug: praziquantel (fenbendazole covers most Taenia but praziquantel is reliable). Zoonosis: low for the canine/feline species.
  • Echinococcus spp. (E. granulosus, E. multilocularis): LC: INDIRECT — definitive host = dog/canid; intermediate host = sheep/livestock (E. granulosus) or rodents (E. multilocularis). Tx: dog eats hydatid cysts in raw offal/viscera; adult is tiny. Age: rural dogs fed raw offal. Tx drug: praziquantel (NOT fenbendazole). ZOONOTIC → hydatid disease: humans ingest eggs from dog faeces → hydatid cysts in liver/lung (E. multilocularis = alveolar, malignant-like).
⚠ Trap: fenbendazole misses Dipylidium & Echinococcus — both need praziquantel. Visible rice-grain segments = review flea control (Dipylidium's intermediate host).
⚠ Trap: Echinococcus is the big zoonosis — canine tapeworm, but humans are the accidental intermediate host and grow hydatid cysts. Deworm at-risk dogs with praziquantel and don't feed raw offal.

Heartworm own sheet

  • Heartworm (Dirofilaria immitis) — see the separate Heartworm sheet for the mosquito-vectored life cycle, antigen vs microfilaria testing, staging, the AHS melarsomine protocol, and feline heartworm.

Control · Deworming · Formulary

  • ASV intake mandate — treat every dog & cat for roundworms + hookworms at intake, from 2 wk of age (zoonotic risk); every animal should leave covered vs hook + round. Whipworm/tapeworm = targeted (cost).
  • Pediatric schedule — puppies from 2 wk, kittens from 3 wk, q2wk to 12 wk, then monthly to 6 mo; treat pregnant & nursing dams.
  • Pyrantel pamoate — hooks + rounds; cheapest, safe mainstay; retreat 2 wk then monthly. Misses whipworm & tapeworm.
  • Fenbendazole — hooks, rounds, whips, Giardia, most tapeworms; 50 mg/kg PO q24h × 3–5 d. Misses Dipylidium & Echinococcus.
  • Praziquantel — all tapeworms incl. Dipylidium & Echinococcus; 5–7.5 mg/kg PO or injectable.
  • Milbemycin oxime — hook/round/whip plus heartworm prevention.
  • Combos — Drontal Plus (pyrantel + febantel + praziquantel); Profender (emodepside + praziquantel, feline).
  • Prepatent periods — hookworm ~2 wk; T. canis ~5 wk; whipworm ~3 mo (long → single negative float doesn't clear).
  • Environmental persistence — ascarid (round) eggs nearly indestructible in soil (heat/bleach only disrupt the coat to speed desiccation); hookworm eggs die by desiccation on dry surfaces; whipworm eggs hardy (bipolar plugs). Post-treatment faeces are heavily contaminating → prompt pick-up.
  • Reduce T. canis in pups — treat pregnant bitch with ivermectin/doramectin at day 40–50 gestation (reduces, doesn't eliminate, shedding).
