Vaccination

Vaccination · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Intake naivety drives shelter risk — match the vaccine type, timing & route to the pathogen and the maternal-antibody window; a vaccine can never beat natural infection, so store and record every dose correctly.

Intake Susceptibility

  • Dogs on intake — ~50% (±20%) naive for canine distemper virus (CDV); ~30% (±15%) naive for canine parvovirus type 2 (CPV-2).
  • ~64.5% are unprotected against CPV-2, CDV, or both.
  • Cats on intake — ~50% naive for feline panleukopenia virus (FPV); >75% already have antibody to feline calicivirus (FCV) / feline herpesvirus (FHV).
  • A vaccine can never be expected to give more effective or longer-lasting protection than natural infection.

Vaccine Types 4

  1. Modified-live (MLV) — mimics natural infection; rapid onset; full protection ~2–3 weeks after the last dose.
  2. Killed (inactivated) — needs 2 doses; NOT used in shelters except rabies; safer in pregnancy & immunosuppression. + check: the "safer in pregnancy / immunosuppression" property belongs to killed (inactivated) vaccines, not MLV.
  3. Recombinant — rapid onset; works through maternally-derived antibody (MDA); no adjuvant → lower injection-site-sarcoma risk (rabies, feline leukaemia virus (FeLV)).
  4. Mucosal / oral (intranasal) — local humoral & cellular upper-respiratory-tract (URT) immunity within 3–7 days; use for respiratory pathogens.

Vaccine Efficacy

  • Can be expressed as the preventable fraction — proportion of vaccinated animals that do NOT develop disease vs unvaccinated.
  • Causes of vaccine failure — MDA interference, immunodeficiency, concurrent disease, poor nutrition, chronic stress, very young / old age, improper handling / storage.

Maternal Antibody & Serum feline

  • Colostrum must be provided within 72 h of birth.
  • A donor cat may be used but must be FeLV / feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV)-negative and healthy; serum can be frozen at −20°C (−4°F).
  • Hyperimmune serum given before or at exposure can prevent infection in naive animals.

Canine Schedule

  • Core — DA2PP (distemper, adenovirus-2, parvovirus, parainfluenza) + intranasal Bordetella + canine parainfluenza (CPiV) + parenteral rabies.
  • Timing — start at 4 weeks, boost every 2–3 weeks until 18–20 weeks. Dogs >18–20 weeks: an initial MLV DA2PP then a booster 2–3 weeks later.
  • Bordetella + CPiV can be given from 3 weeks; intranasal gives local innate + mucosal immunity. Both intranasal and oral Bordetella are more effective than parenteral and are less interfered with by MDA.
  • CDV — almost immediate immunity after a single MLV dose. Canine adenovirus-2 (CAV-2) cross-protects canine adenovirus-1 (CAV-1); MLV CAV-2 can be given intranasally to pups as young as 3 weeks.
  • Rabies >12 weeks — single dose before release (or on entry if a long length of stay (LOS) is anticipated).

Feline Schedule

  • Core — FVRCP (feline viral rhinotracheitis / herpesvirus-1, calicivirus, panleukopenia) + rabies. FeLV is core for owned kittens <1 yr; in shelters only for group-housed / sanctuary-type cats.
  • Timing — start at 4 weeks, boost every 2–3 weeks until 16–20 weeks. Cats >16 weeks get one dose then a booster 2 weeks later.
  • FPV — a KILLED (inactivated) product is preferred in pregnant queens (avoids fetal cerebellar hypoplasia).
  • FCV — immunity in ~7 days, fuller after a 2nd dose. FHV-1 — nearly all infected cats become latently infected and can be reinfected.

Injection Sites & Sarcoma feline

  • Site mapping — FVRCP → right fore limb; rabies → right hind limb; FeLV → left hind limb. Interscapular injection is no longer recommended.
  • Injection-site sarcoma — 1-2-3 rule — biopsy a post-vaccinal mass that persists >3 months, is >2 cm, or is still enlarging 1 month after injection.
  • Adverse-event rate — ~0.5%; type I hypersensitivity, lethargy, etc.
⚠ Trap: 1-2-3 rule = persists >3 months · >2 cm · still enlarging 1 month after injection.

Rabies Detail

  • Products — canine rabies vaccine = inactivated / adjuvanted; feline = non-adjuvanted (recombinant).
  • Immunity ~2–3 weeks after injection. 1-year vs 3-year products — a 3-year product is preferred.
  • Most animals >3 months are protected by a single injection, with the legally-required booster 1 year later.
  • An antibody titre is NOT a substitute for vaccination; exemptions must be documented.

Vaccine Storage

  • Store at 2–8°C (36–46°F) in a refrigerator; never freeze; position ~2–3 inches from all walls; monitor temperature (per manufacturer).
  • Once reconstituted — use MLV within 30–60 min; use killed within 4 h; if MLV sits at room temperature ≥1 h, discard.
  • Record — batch + serial number, expiry date, injection site, and administrator's name.

📄 Journal Article

  • Risk of commercial pseudorabies (Aujeszky's) vaccines in cats: feline pseudorabies is sporadic; the attenuated pseudorabies virus (PRV) vaccine strain causes only mild signs in cats, and transmission is pig-to-cat — a reminder that some live vaccines can affect non-target species.
