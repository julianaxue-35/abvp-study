Euthanasia

Euthanasia · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Exam weight sits roughly equally on the decision (QOL + written criteria, not illness alone), the technique, the controlled-drug law, and staff wellbeing. IV pentobarbital (EBI) is the shelter gold standard.

Decision & Quality of Life

  • Basis = current + projected QOL, not illness alone; weigh treatability + resources.
  • T / M / U — only Untreatable is ethically unambiguous; resources decide for Treatable.
  • Written criteria + Capacity for Care guard against bias / arbitrary decisions.
  • Behavioural euthanasia — un-mitigable safety risk or severe pathology; documented assessment.
  • Depopulation ≠ euthanasia — whole-population destruction in an emergency; last resort, NOT a routine outbreak response.
⚠ Manageable animals are NOT euthanasia candidates — maintainable with ongoing care.

Stray Holds & Law

  • Stray hold — legal period before euthanasia; euthanise early only if a vet deems irremediable suffering, with thorough documentation (medical notes + photos).
  • ID found (tag / chip / tattoo) → contact owner + provide palliative/stabilising treatment, unless actively suffering.
  • After the hold, evaluate sick/injured by the same adoption criteria as any animal.
  • State sets technician certification + controlled-drug access (direct vs indirect).
  • EBI preferred; CO / gas increasingly banned.
⚠ Not every state requires a vet to press the plunger — trained/certified techs can perform EBI.

Controlled Drug & Disposal

  • Pentobarbital alone = Schedule II; + phenytoin (Beuthanasia-D, Euthasol) = Schedule III (easier to secure).
  • Phenytoin = cardiotoxic adjunct that speeds arrest — the schedule change is the formulation, not "denaturing".
  • Strict records + secured storage per DEA/state; vet stays responsible even with volunteer staff.
  • Disposal: relay toxicosis + environmental/food-chain contamination → crematorium/burial.
  • Rabies suspect — submit the head (whole body for bats); preserve the brain.

Agents

  • Pentobarbital label dose ~30–50% above lethal (safety cushion, not waste).
  • T61 withdrawn — differential onset risked paralysis/pain before unconsciousness.
⚠ A paralytic (neuromuscular blocker), alone or added to pentobarbital, is NEVER acceptable — paralysed yet conscious.

Pre-Euthanasia Sedation

  • ASV — sedate most cases; strongly for distressed / dangerous / fractious.
  • Acepromazine — no analgesia, ↓BP (harder venipuncture), can cause paradoxical stimulation.
  • Ketamine or tiletamine-zolazepam (Zoletil) (± ace / xylazine) IM for fractious cats; xylazine emetic + ↓BP (delays pentobarbital) → fast or combine.
  • Oral pentobarbital in food — ~3× IV dose, sedation 15–60 min; gloves + mask when mixing.

Routes & Confirming Death

  • IV = preferred — ~1 mL / 10 lb (4.5 kg); onset 45–90 s.
  • IP when IV impractical (esp <10 lb, feral cats) — ~3 mL / 10 lb (3× IV), onset 5–15 min; low-right abdomen (avoid spleen), aspirate first, then dark/quiet.
  • Unacceptable routes — IH (intrahepatic — very painful), SC, IM.
  • Confirm death: no heartbeat + loss of corneal reflex (± absent deep-pain response, later rigor mortis).
⚠ IC (intracardiac) only on an unconscious/anaesthetised animal — NEVER conscious (most-tested fact).

Carbon Monoxide & Gas

  • ASV: CO unacceptable for shelter cats/dogs; EBI preferred.
  • Why — distress before unconsciousness; slow (10–20 min at 6%); delayed uptake in <16 wk / geriatric / sick / pregnant; explosive >10%; staff hazard.
⚠ Meeting every AVMA contingency still doesn't make CO acceptable in shelters.

Special Populations

  • Dam FIRST, then neonates — exception to the no-witnessing rule.
  • Fetuses — uterus out en bloc, intact, kept closed ≥1 hour (NOT 30 min); opening it can start air-breathing.
  • Reptiles — freezing unacceptable; anaesthetise then pentobarbital IV / IC / intracoelomic; hold 24–48 h (slow metabolism).
  • Fish / amphibians — MS-222 immersion, ≥30 min after gill movement stops + a confirmatory step.
  • Birds — cervical dislocation / decapitation only conditionally acceptable.
  • Horses — 2 experienced handlers; sedate/tranquillise, then IV jugular pentobarbital.

Euthanasia Room & Procedure

  • Room — quiet, dimmable, well-ventilated, soundproofed, easy-clean; away from busy foot-traffic; near holding + body storage; secured, lockable drug storage.
  • Before injecting — double-check animal ID + scan for a microchip; ≥2 trained handlers for safe, humane restraint.

Staff Wellbeing

  • Euthanasia is a major driver of compassion fatigue (secondary traumatic stress) and burnout.
  • Prevent — rotate duties, let attached staff opt out, peer support/debrief, self-care; only staff who can deliver a fear-free death should perform it. (full detail on the Mental Health sheet)
