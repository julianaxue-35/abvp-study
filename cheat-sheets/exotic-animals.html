Exotic & Other Animals

Exotic & Other Animals · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

Non-cat/dog shelter species live or die on species-specific husbandry — right diet, thermal gradient, ultraviolet-B light, bedding & strict predator/prey separation — and each group carries its own zoonoses, so match the care to the biology and protect the staff and public.

Ferrets

  • Obligate carnivores (fat 15–20%, protein 30–35%; kitten food OK, cat food isn't). Predators — must NOT be housed near birds, rodents or rabbits. Treat every incoming ferret empirically for ear mites (Otodectes cynotis).
  • Canine distemper virus (CDV) is ~100% fatal — no treatment. Chin/lip + inguinal/perianal dermatitis is pathognomonic; humane euthanasia is the best shelter option. Vaccinate healthy ferrets for CDV + rabies.
  • Aleutian disease virus (ADV, a parvovirus) — chronic wasting; hallmark hypergammaglobulinaemia; ascending paresis. No vaccine; euthanise symptomatic shelter ferrets.
  • ~1/3 of ferrets >3 yr get one of the classic neoplasias: insulinoma (glucose <60 mg/dL, hind-limb weakness, ptyalism), lymphoma, or adrenocortical disease — symmetrical tail-base alopecia advancing rostrally + vulvar swelling in spayed jills, tied to early US spay/neuter.
⚠ Trap: Ferrets have a high rate of anaphylaxis to CDV & rabies vaccines, usually within 25 min — monitor every vaccinated ferret ~25 min; first-line diphenhydramine 2–4 mg/kg.

Guinea Pigs

  • Bordetella bronchiseptica is a major respiratory pathogen — rabbits, dogs, cats & primates are asymptomatic carriers that infect guinea pigs (shared airspace can suffice); never co-house.
  • Elodont (continuously growing) teeth → cheek-tooth overgrowth on low-fibre / muesli diets; oral exam essential. Use solid floors (wire → pododermatitis); pine & cedar bedding contraindicated.
  • Ovarian cysts — 76% by 5 yr: anorexia, abdominal distension, bilateral symmetrical truncal alopecia.
  • Sow pubic symphysis fuses at ~7–8 mo if never bred → dystocia if bred for the first time after that age.
⚠ Trap: Guinea pigs can't synthesise vitamin C (lack L-gulonolactone oxidase) → scurvy. Plain rabbit pellets don't supply enough — feed guinea-pig-specific, vitamin-C-formulated pellets + fresh veg.

Small Rodents hamster · gerbil · rat · mouse

  • Wet tail = Lawsonia intracellularis proliferative ileitis — classic in weanling hamsters (4–8 wk): haemorrhagic diarrhoea, may die within 48 h, mortality ~90%.
  • Mycoplasma pulmonis = most common respiratory pathogen of rats & mice; intracellular → can't be cleared, recrudesces; worsened by high cage ammonia and cedar/pine bedding.
  • Avoid cedar & pine shavings (aromatic compounds → respiratory/skin disease) — use shredded paper.
  • Zoonoses — rat-bite fever (Streptobacillus moniliformis), lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus (LCMV), hantavirus. Male mice fight; gerbils are territorial (house singly); hamsters have a low startle threshold (bite).

Reptiles

  • Poikilotherms — provide a thermal gradient so the animal chooses its body temperature: ambient 80–85°F (27–29°C) + a focal basking spot ~10°F warmer. Too cold → immunosuppression/GI stasis; too hot → thermal burns/death.
  • Ultraviolet-B (UVB) light drives skin vitamin-D synthesis (esp. herbivores/omnivores): wavelength 290–320 nm, bulb 12–18 in (30–46 cm) away, NOT filtered through glass/acrylic, replace every 12 mo. Deficient UVB / calcium → metabolic bone disease (MBD)+.
  • Temporary housing only — review local law first; individualise ID (never group several reptiles under one ID); don't co-house (cannibalism) or house with carnivorous mammals. Cat litter must never be used.
⚠ Trap: Phenolics & glutaraldehyde are toxic to reptiles/amphibians — never use them; disinfect with dilute bleach 1:32 or a quaternary ammonium compound (QAC), then rinse thoroughly (highly absorptive skin).

Avian (Birds)

  • Gram-negative organisms in psittacine droppings = potential pathogens (normal flora is gram-positive).
  • Birds lack a diaphragm — never wrap the chest tightly (chest excursion = respiration); give fluids orally/SC/IO, never intracoelomically (can drown the bird). Poor clotting → a damaged blood feather can bleed to death; do NOT pull it (styptic/pressure).
  • Aspergillosis = most common fungal disease (spore inhalation, non-contagious, management-driven); avoid corncob & walnut-shell bedding.
  • Overheated non-stick cookware releases polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE / Teflon) fumes that are rapidly fatal to birds+.
⚠ Trap: Psittacosis / chlamydiosis (Chlamydophila psittaci, "parrot fever") is the headline avian zoonosis — an obligate intracellular bacterium stable in dried droppings for months (people inhale fecal dust). Best shelter control = remove feces daily; treat doxycycline for 45 days.

Horses

  • Trickle-feeders needing continuous high-fibre forage. Equine squamous gastric ulcer disease 17–39% in untrained horses, 66–100% in training; colic prevalence >4%/yr.
  • Quarantine 2–3 weeks on intake — latent equine herpesvirus (EHV) and Salmonella shed under transport stress; strangles (Streptococcus equi) is common in auction/rescue horses.
  • Herd animals — house with (or within sight of) other horses; give daily turnout, not stalled 24/7. Wood chewing / object licking = salt/mineral deficiency.
  • Aggression's most common cause is underlying physical discomfort/illness; don't punish stereotypies (cribbing in 2–10%).
+ check: Coggins test for equine infectious anaemia (EIA) is a standard equine intake/movement screen — not covered in Juliana's hub source; verify before relying on it as an exam point.

Wildlife

  • Emergency stabilization only — it's illegal to give long-term care without a state rehabilitator permit (most states allow ≤48 h possession, then transfer); migratory birds/endangered species need a federal USFWS permit (Migratory Bird Treaty Act).
  • Rabies vector species (RVS) = raccoons, skunks, foxes, bats (+ woodchucks). Handle all wild mammals as potential carriers (age is irrelevant); no staff handles RVS without rabies pre-exposure prophylaxis.
  • Baylisascaris procyonis (raccoon roundworm) = standout zoonosis — eggs cause neural larva migrans (blindness, brain damage, death), survive years, resist disinfectants; only high-heat/flame kills them. Opossums are remarkably rabies-resistant (their display is a bluff, not disease).
  • Cat-caught wildlife → antibiotics ASAP (Pasteurella septicaemia; amoxicillin-clavulanate best). Minimise stress (dark/warm/quiet — prey species "die of fright"); avoid imprinting/hand-raising → transfer to a rehabilitator.
⚠ Trap: Rabies spreads only by saliva into a bite, fresh scratch or mucous membrane — NOT by blood, urine or feces, not airborne, can't cross intact skin, and dried saliva is non-viable. Counsel the public toward calm precautions, not panic.

Handling & Zoonoses cross-cutting

  • Never co-house predators with prey — ferrets away from birds/rodents/rabbits; rabbits (a Bordetella reservoir) away from guinea pigs; reptiles away from carnivorous mammals.
  • Bedding traps — pine & cedar shavings harm small rodents/guinea pigs; corncob & walnut-shell promote avian aspergillosis; phenolics/glutaraldehyde are toxic to reptiles.
  • LCMV (lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus) — rodents/guinea pigs; a human teratogen in pregnancy (fetal hydrocephalus + chorioretinitis) → counsel pregnant staff/adopters to avoid rodent urine, saliva & feces.
  • Other key zoonoses — psittacosis (birds), rat-bite fever, Baylisascaris (raccoon), Salmonella/Campylobacter (ferrets, reptiles, guinea pigs), dermatophytosis & sarcoptic mites.
  • Wash hands / change gloves between predator and prey species — rodents smell a predator and panic/bite.
