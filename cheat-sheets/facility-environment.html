Facility & Environment

Facility & Environment · Physical Health of Animal (29%) · Shelter Medicine

The building is itself a biosecurity + welfare instrument: plan it to Capacity for Care, move animals · air · people healthiest-first, and hit the ASV (Association of Shelter Veterinarians) numbers — enclosure minimums, 10–20 air changes/hour, negative-pressure isolation, 18–26.6°C.

Planning & Capacity for Care

  • Record intake (by entry mode & animal type) monthly for ≥2 years to capture seasonality → size to real demand; optimise C4C to prevent overcrowding & overbuilding.
  • C4C (Capacity for Care) — most animals houseable while still meeting each animal's full standard of care (a welfare ceiling, not a cage count).
  • LRR (Live Release Rate) — proportion leaving alive.
  • Space mix — Animal areas ~65% (housing ~45% = largest share, training ~15%, medical ~5%) · Public/support ~35%.

Layout & Traffic Flow

  • Directional workflow — clean/handle healthiest first: puppies & kittens → healthy adults → intake → sick/isolation last. Staff are the top fomite vector.
  • Surgery — site away from isolation but close to housing (short transport).
  • Drop boxes (unmonitored after-hours intake) — UNACCEPTABLE (ASV): welfare, disease, safety, death; use on-call numbers / police or clinic arrangements.

Primary Enclosure Standards

  • Postural rule (ASV) — animal can stand & walk several steps, sit, lie at full length, hold tail erect.
  • Cat — <8 sq ft (0.75 m²) unacceptable; ≥11 sq ft (1.0 m²) ideal.
  • Dog — kennel size varies by body size; aim wider than 4 ft (1.2 m) so nose/tail clear the side walls when turning; ≥97 sq ft (9 m²) → more activity, fewer stereotypies.
  • Double / multi-compartment preferred — separates rest from elimination, allows touch-free spot cleaning.

Cat Housing

  • Double-compartment housing — ↓ stress, facilitates spot cleaning; food · litter · sleep >2 ft apart + enrichment (scratch, climb, perch); allow a hide + elevated space.
  • Hide box = welfare SOLUTION — ≥1 hide box + elevated perch per unit (ASV); hiding ↓cortisol, so never remove the hide box because a cat hides; most settle in 2–5 wk.
  • Cohousing — ≤6 adult cats per enclosure; ≥18 sq ft (1.7 m²) per cat (more than solo housing so they can keep distance).
  • Pre-entry — health/behaviour assessment, no contagious signs, negative FeLV & FIV (feline leukaemia / immunodeficiency virus), vaccine-response time; match age/sex/health/behaviour.
⚠ Trap: cats that fight at initial introduction are ~40× more likely to keep fighting → house apart (watch swapped values 10×/50× or wrong outcome).

Dog Housing

  • Group housing — 2–4 adults per enclosure, pairs preferred; introduce outside the enclosure first; all-in / all-out for full sanitation between groups.
  • Pups/litters <20 wk not cohoused with unfamiliar animals; monitor closely after any new entry.
  • Rest — a soft resting place raised off the floor + a hiding option; off-centre drain keeps a dry rest spot.
  • Doors — barred enclosure doors preferred over plexiglass (airflow); exhaust fan low, near floor drains.

Isolation & TNR Hold

  • Isolation must not open into another housing area — corridor/anteroom for PPE (personal protective equipment) don/doff; no shared species or disease in one iso room.
  • Negative pressure = infectious isolation — air flows IN when the door opens; ≥12–15 ACH (air changes/hour) (up to 20); 100% exhaust outdoors, never recirculated; −2.5 to −8 Pa (pascals) vs corridor, verified on a gauge.
  • Positive pressure = surgery / neonates / immunocompromised — air flows OUT; ≥15 ACH, HEPA-filtered supply.
  • TNR hold — away from general population, dedicated entry/exit, not beside cat housing; overnight only (long-term = inhumane).
  • Makeshift (no true iso) — house contagious dogs ≥25 ft (7.6 m) from unaffected + cover doors.
⚠ Trap: negative pressure = isolation (air in); positive pressure = surgery / immunocompromised (air out) — reversing them is the classic error.

Ventilation & Air

  • Standard — 10–20 room air changes/hour with fresh, non-recirculated air; if an area is adjacent to general-pop housing, treat as semi-isolation and don't recirculate its air into gen pop.
  • Filters — pleated MERV (Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value) 6–8 for standard HVAC retrofits; HEPA reserved for surgery suites (facility-wide is cost-prohibitive).
  • Adjuncts only — UVGI (ultraviolet germicidal irradiation) & air purifiers are never the sole air-quality control.

Noise · Light · Temperature

  • Temperature — 18–26.6°C (64–80°F).
  • Humidity — 30–70%.
  • Noise — chronic >85 dB (decibels) causes physiological stress; dog kennels reach 95–115 dB during barking. Reduce it: acoustic ceiling panels to absorb echoes, quiet latches / rubber bumpers, a solid wall between cat & dog wards, and locate noisy equipment away from animals.
  • Lighting — natural light for circadian rhythm; limit to <1 watt/sq ft; dim/switch off artificial light within 4–6 m (~15 ft) of windows.

Surfaces & Cleanability

  • Floors — nonporous, impermeable, drainable: epoxy or sealed concrete, resinous urethane, MMA (methyl methacrylate), terrazzo; tile only in low-risk areas. Unacceptable: paint, wood, vinyl, standard concrete block (porous).
  • Walls — water-resistant, adequate height, solid; cove the wall-to-floor junction.
  • Drainage — slope to drains, off-centre to keep a dry rest area, no standing water, no shared drainage between units.

📄 Journal Articles

  • Cat dens (hiding boxes) reduce URI & length of stay: shelter cats that avoided upper respiratory infection (URI) were adopted at higher rates and so had a shorter length of stay (LOS); a hiding den was significantly associated with lower URI incidence — so including dens in cat enclosures may have positive welfare, LOS and adoption implications for shelter cats.
