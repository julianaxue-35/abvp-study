Canine Enrichment

Speed

Short petting and play sessions

  • Cortisol falls during 20 to 30 minutes of petting or play, then does not persist an hour later; stacking multiple sessions in the same day adds no further benefit. your notes
  • Sex of the human can matter: women tend to produce greater cortisol reduction than men in some studies. Dogs alone in a novel enclosure vocalise much more than when petted, played with, or just sharing space with a passive stranger. your notes
  • 15 minutes with a person lowers heart rate, raises heart-rate variability, and reduces standing during the interaction. your notes
  • In a pilot head-to-head comparison, environmental enrichment increased dogs' activity (more walking and digging) more than petting did, while petting produced more positive social interactions afterward — the two are complementary, not interchangeable. (Lopes, 2022 — see journal abstracts below)
  • The behavioural-over-cortisol pattern generalises beyond shelters: four weeks of added positive human interaction in commercial breeding dams reduced attention bias toward a threatening stimulus and raised stranger-sociability scores, with no significant change in hair cortisol. (Baquuerio-Espinosa, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2023 — see journal abstracts below)
Exam trap: cortisol drop only in strays A 15 minute petting session lowered cortisol ONLY in dogs admitted as STRAYS, not in owner-relinquished dogs. This subpopulation split is high-yield. your notes

Reading versus exercise (both 15 minutes daily)

  • Both events produced more back-and-forth motion afterward. Exercise gave a greater reduction in door jumping. Reading gave a larger overall reduction in undesired behaviours, both jumping AND barking. your notes

  • In an 8-week shelter-dog walking program for military veterans, dog stress (heart-rate variability) was not significantly raised during the walks themselves, and post-walk stress was actually lower after walks with veterans who had PTSD than after walks with veterans who did not. (Friedmann, Anthrozoös, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Baseline for comparison: in a large longitudinal cohort of owned dogs, typical walk duration roughly doubled from puppyhood to adulthood (about 30 minutes total per day at 16 weeks old versus about 1 hour on weekdays and 2 hours on weekends by 15 months), while the proportion of walk time spent on-lead fell as dogs aged. (Kinsman, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2022 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Activity has a natural rhythm worth working WITH: free-roaming and farm dogs show a bimodal activity pattern, peaking around 5–7am and, less strongly, 4–7pm, but this bimodal pattern was detectable in only 45% of family dogs, whose activity instead tracks the owner's routine with one variable midday peak; family dogs were more often highly active than the free-roaming groups but compensated with longer rest, and activity fell significantly with age, neutering, and higher body-condition score — the authors suggest a more structured daily schedule could benefit pet dogs. (Griss, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)

Foster effects

  • Dogs returning from foster were more obedient, sought social interaction with handlers, responded more readily to cues, jumped on strangers and barked less, and yawned more. Cortisol was relatively unaffected. The case for foster rests on the behavioural changes, not the cortisol. your notes
  • A 1 to 2 night foster stay produced cortisol BELOW the standard shelter baseline. Cortisol rose on return to the shelter, but did NOT exceed the pre-foster shelter baseline. your notes
  • Short outings are not a foster substitute: a 2.5 hour outing significantly RAISED the urinary cortisol:creatinine ratio that afternoon (though it returned to baseline the next day), even as low-activity time fell and high-activity time rose during the outing itself. (Gunter, Animals, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)

Limiting visitors

  • During high-visitor times, dogs spend more time at the front of pen (FOP), standing and barking. A 2014 study that blocked adopter access in the afternoons documented noise levels more than 12 dB lower than pre-intervention. Sedentary behaviour rose in the morning before opening and in the afternoon during the intervention, locomotor activity fell, and the frequency and duration of repetitive behaviours fell in the afternoon. your notes
  • Trade-off: visitor restrictions reduce kennel stress but can lengthen stay and reduce adoptions, so dose them carefully. your notes

Training and counter-conditioning

  • In-kennel training: more sitting, lying down, and quiet behaviour, and less jumping in the kennel. your notes
  • Out-of-kennel training: reduced aggression. your notes
  • Consistency is the rule: once training ceased for 1 week, aggression toward other dogs returned to pre-intervention levels. your notes
  • Counter-conditioning (CC) takes about 20 seconds per dog; operant conditioning takes about 2 minutes per dog. Daily pairings of a door chime with food (3 to 5 times a week for 3 weeks) reduced kennel noise by 15 dB by attenuating the response to an established environmental stressor. your notes
  • Tailor interactions to the individual dog and, where possible, run them OUT of the kennel away from environmental stressors, or in a foster home. your notes
  • Structured socialisation and training pays off even outside a shelter setting: kennel-raised dogs given regular 30-minute socialisation and training sessions across 12 weeks improved on ALL 20 scored welfare behaviours (9 to 53% gains), with positive-valence behaviours rising and negative-valence behaviours (including "distressed") falling. (Ing et al., Animals (Basel), 2026 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Exam trap — early neurological stimulation (ENS) is not proven to reduce puppy stress responses: a controlled trial in 76 commercial-breeding-kennel puppies found no significant difference between ENS, simple holding, and untreated controls on affiliative behaviour or exploratory/vocal stress responses around isolation and transport — don't assume "ENS" is an evidence-backed shelter enrichment technique just because it's commonly recommended (Boone, Animals, 2023 — see journal abstracts below).

Conspecific Social Interaction

Group housing is the under-used welfare lever. Solo housing drives isolation vocalisations and front-of-pen aggression, and breaking a co-housed pair has measurable knock-on effects on the dog left behind.

Safety data for group housing In a 3-month study of 211 dogs in group housing, only 5 incidents occurred, classified by the researchers as light wounds. Group housing is safer than commonly assumed and abolishes stereotypy in this dataset. your notes
ASV group-size caps Do not exceed 4–6 dogs or 10–12 cats per group. Puppies and kittens under 20 weeks should not be group-housed unless they are littermates. ASV Guidelines

Pair separation effects

  • When one dog was removed from a co-housed pair, the remaining dog's running, posture changes, stretching, circling, and figures-of-eight all increased, signalling restlessness. your notes
  • Many of these behaviours declined over the 6 day post-separation period, but play stayed reduced. your notes

Visual contact without interaction

Solo housing with nearby visual conspecific contact but no social interaction may have more complex welfare effects than is currently understood, and further empirical work is needed before this very common shelter arrangement can be called optimal. your notes

Cortisol nuance

No main effects of housing or social contact were found on cortisol values. Cortisol was HIGHEST in dogs in NOVEL housing receiving no social contact, and LOWEST in dogs in TRADITIONAL kennels receiving no social contact. your notes

Playgroup eligibility

Exam trap: less than one third In typical shelters, fewer than 1/3 of resident dogs are suitable and available for daily off-leash interaction with other dogs. Playgroups are useful but cannot be the primary social plan for the whole population. your notes

Toys have varying functional value to the species AND to the individual. Choose by the dog's history and preferences, and rotate to avoid habituation.

Exam trap: soft beats robust for engagement Shelter dogs largely IGNORE robust selections in favour of soft, destructible toys, with the longest engagement on squeaky bones and plush toys. Rotate items if usage drops, since habituation is likely: reintroducing a dog's own preferred scented toy after a break still showed reduced engagement versus a fresh one, confirming habituation happens even to a previously preferred scent. your notes (Sonowal, Applied Animal Behavior Science, 2024 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Chewing itself is functional, not just occupying: it supports dental and oral hygiene, digestive health, bone strength, and stress management, and preference is driven mainly by odour, taste, and mouthfeel — without a preferred chew item, dogs may redirect chewing onto inappropriate objects. (Quinn, Frontiers in Veterinary Science, 2025 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Scent adds engagement on top of toy choice: adding a dog's preferred scent increased engagement time with both its most- and least-preferred toy, with the biggest relative gain (nearly 14-fold) on the LEAST preferred toy. (Howard, Applied Animal Behavior Science, 2024 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Natural rubber feeding toys: dogs preferred yellow over blue and vanilla scent over beef scent, and one month of regular use nearly halved excitation-related behaviour on owner-rated questionnaires. (Boonhoh, Applied Animal Behavior Science, 2024 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Not all enrichment is equal: in a 7-activity pilot, conspecific play and a playhouse produced the greatest gains in relaxed behaviour, while food-based items (interactive toy, stuffed food toy) produced the LEAST behaviour change of the activities tested. (Hunt, Animals, 2022 — see journal abstracts below)
  • In kennelled police dogs, both a lawn area and a hanging toy lowered faecal cortisol metabolites more on a second round of use (a cumulative benefit regardless of item type), and dogs with high baseline stereotypy rates responded better to the lawn than the toy. (Nogueira, Animals, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)

Sensory Enrichment

Auditory, olfactory, and visual interventions have small to moderate effects, with one big exam trap: barking spikes when music stops.

Auditory

  • Classical music: more resting, less standing, more quiet behaviour in the kennel, compared with other genres, human conversation, or no auditory enrichment. your notes
  • Heavy metal: increased body shaking, increased barking, less time resting. your notes
  • Audiobooks: dogs spent more time resting and less time walking, sitting, or standing; promising for variety in the rotation. your notes
  • Habituation is rapid: rotate multiple classical compilations daily, and integrate audiobooks, to keep effects fresh. your notes
  • Preference testing found dogs actively avoid agonistic conspecific vocalisations but show no clear preference or aversion for classical music, relaxing music, or speech over quiet; familiarity mattered more than genre — dogs already routinely exposed to classical music at home spent twice as long in the sound zone and were more relaxed. (Guerineau, Applied Animal Behavior Science, 2024 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Adding a human presence to a reading/audiobook session boosts engagement further: dogs spent more time in their bed and looking toward the audio source when a person was present than during audio alone. (Tuozzi, Animals, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)
  • There is no universal calming effect of "music" as such: results are mixed even within the same species, so music likely needs tailoring to that species' own communication system and to musical structures already known to shift emotional state (calming versus stimulating), rather than assuming any genre works for every animal. (Snowdon, Animals, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)

Feline Enrichment

Feline enrichment follows the same four pillars as canine enrichment but is delivered through cat-specific channels. The strongest single lever is the dose and style of human interaction, and the biggest welfare emergency to recognise is frustration tipping into depression in under-stimulated high-energy cats.

What enrichment is, and its goals

Enrichment is social interaction, physical and mental stimulation, opportunities to perform species-typical behaviours, and choice and control over the environment. It is given the same significance as nutrition and medical care and is never optional. ASV

  • Goals: increase behavioural diversity, reduce abnormal behaviours, increase the range of normal behaviour, increase positive use of the environment, and improve the ability to cope. your notes
  • Each cat's past socialisation is unique and shapes how it uses enrichment, so offer different kinds and let the cat choose. your notes
  • Enrichment can shift whether a cat perceives a situation as stressful, by aiding habituation or by facilitating concealment and escape, and by reducing boredom. your notes

Social enrichment

  • 60 minutes per day of human interaction reduced the Cat Stress Score from day 1 to day 2; 20 minutes per day did not. All human contact should be positive and consistent and avoid excessive restraint. your notes
  • Cats less accustomed to close contact may prefer to interact via a toy or treat, or have a person sit nearby and read aloud, rather than attempt physical contact. For frustrated cats, consider limiting human interaction. your notes
  • With other cats: group-housed cats spent more time in locomotion and social or solitary play than singly housed cats and tended toward a lower cortisol-to-creatinine ratio. your notes
  • Interaction with other species is not recommended, unless a dog and cat were surrendered together and reported to be bonded. your notes
  • The environment-level cortisol signal matches the interaction-dose data above: shelter cats housed in an enriched environment had roughly half the hair cortisol concentration of cats in a standard, low-resource environment. (Wojtas et al., Animals, 2024 — see journal abstracts below)
  • People presence itself can suppress enrichment use: in colony housing, item engagement across all enrichment categories fell when people were in the room, males used vertical surfaces more, cats used vertical surfaces more the longer their length of stay, and cats seized or surrendered from hoarding or cruelty cases used enclosed hiding spaces more. (Libonate, Journal of Applied Animal Welfare Science, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)
  • For post-adoption counselling: how much enrichment an owner actually delivers tracks their attitude and attachment, not just intent — owner attitudes toward play and toy variety predicted play-session duration and toy provision, and higher rates of positive owner-cat interaction were themselves associated with more cat play (a welfare indicator), though not with fewer problem behaviours. (Windschnurer, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2022 — see journal abstracts below)
ASV cross-check: social contact Social isolation is profoundly negative, and social contact should be individually tailored. Poorly socialised animals may not benefit from human contact, with the exception of young puppies and kittens, but may take comfort from their own species. ASV

Cognitive enrichment

  • Provide problem-solving and a degree of control: feeding and foraging enrichment and puzzle feeders. your notes
  • Cats play longer with interactive (human-moved) toys than with solo-play toys, because the movement simulates prey and triggers pounce, stalk, and bat. Rotate toys daily. Adding a cat's preferred scent (catnip or silvervine) further increased interaction with an otherwise-static toy, though it did not add to interaction with an already-moving toy. your notes (Webberson, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2025 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Training is possible but shelter stress makes learning harder and it is time consuming. Frustrated cats given four 10-minute sessions per day for 10 days were more likely to be rated in a positive mood, had higher secretory IgA, and were less likely to develop feline upper respiratory infection (FURI). std ref
  • Optional access to novelty encourages exploration and helps satisfy the need to gain information about the surroundings. your notes
  • Play quantity and quality track welfare: greater playfulness and more games were associated with higher cat quality-of-life scores, and owners reported behavioural changes consistent with distress when play was absent. (Henning et al., Animal Welfare, 2023 — see journal abstracts below)
  • Indoor-only cats show MORE interest in predatory-style play than cats with outdoor access, approaching and searching for prey-like stimuli sooner despite having no live-prey experience — indoor cats need the predatory-play outlet even more, not less. (Pyari, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2021 — see journal abstracts below)
ASV cross-check: feeding enrichment Give feeding-enrichment devices to dogs individually because they are competitive eaters; cats can be given them singly or in amicable groups. Rotate novel items to maintain interest. ASV

Rabbits (brief note)

Rabbits are a minority shelter/lab-adjacent population, but a few 2025 findings are worth knowing.

  • Environmental enrichment reduces negative behaviour in caged rabbits: giving individually-caged laboratory rabbits weekly access to a large floor pen roughly doubled resting/inactivity (31.1% → 55.4%, p < 0.001) and cut negative behaviours from 13.5% to under 5% (p < 0.001), with rabbits spending most floor-pen time on locomotion, exploration, and positive behaviours (Vilardo, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2025 — see journal abstracts below).
  • Maternal enrichment carries over to offspring: enriching breeding does' cages during gestation and lactation improved their female offspring's object-recognition memory and produced heavier weaned kits, though enriched-lineage offspring were also more cautious around novel objects — and moving rabbits from an enriched back to a conventional cage may itself cause a negative emotional state (Nakamura, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2025 — see journal abstracts below).
  • A hiding-place table does not fully protect rabbit welfare during animal-assisted interventions: even with a therapeutic table offering a hiding place, an unfamiliar child's presence still cut exploratory activity and dropped feed intake from over 80% to under 20% of observation time — hiding options help but don't eliminate the stress of AAI contact for rabbits (Součková, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2025 — see journal abstracts below).

Assessment and Measurement

An enrichment program without measurement is just a hope. The exam tests the timeline rules and the practical measurement methods, not theoretical perfection.

How to assess enrichment

  • Population level: What percentage of dogs engage with the enrichment? your notes
  • Individual level: Does this particular dog engage, and does the enrichment shift behaviours that matter for that dog (more resting, less barking)? your notes
  • How and how long? The duration and quality of engagement determine whether to continue or modify the enrichment. your notes
  • Observe from a distance, or have a staff member to whom the dog is habituated record the behaviour. Use paper or electronic records. your notes
  • Evaluate by measuring behaviours BEFORE the enrichment and again AFTER, either shelter-wide (kennel noise) or per individual (barking frequency and duration). your notes
Exam trap: 2 days and 1 week Two stop-rules. If an individual dog shows no direct engagement with a specific item after 2 days, change the type of enrichment. If the broader program produces no positive behavioural change within 1 week at most, modify the strategy. your notes

Behavioral and performance effects on female offspring from rabbit does housed in enriched cages during gestation and lactation
Nakamura GKC, Alencar D, Costa LB, Daros RR · Applied Animal Behaviour Science · 2025 · 10.1016/j.applanim.2025.106576

Abstract. This study examined how environmental enrichment of breeding rabbit does during gestation and lactation affects their female offspring. Prepartum (gestation) enrichment improved the offspring's object-recognition memory, and enrichment during lactation produced heavier weaned offspring. Enriched-lineage offspring showed more exploratory behaviour and 'joyful jumping' (binkies) in an open-field test, but reduced exploration of a novel object, indicating a more cautious response to unfamiliar stimuli. Effects were most pronounced when enrichment spanned birth to weaning, though overall effects were modest. The authors also note that moving rabbits from enriched to conventional cages may induce negative emotional states. The work shows maternal environmental enrichment carries over into offspring cognition, welfare-related behaviour, and growth.

Behavioral effects of floor pens as environmental enrichment for laboratory rabbits (Oryctolagus cuniculus)
Vilardo VB, Pinto MS, de Carvalho e Silva AM, Santori RT · Applied Animal Behaviour Science · 2025 · 10.1016/j.applanim.2025.106629

Abstract. Laboratory animals are indispensable for science, particularly in biomedical research. Their welfare must meet their physical, social and physiological needs. Ethological evaluations are crucial for identifying and correcting abnormal behaviors caused by captivity, often through tools like environmental enrichment (EE). This study evaluated the behavioral effects of implementing floor pens as EE for individually caged laboratory rabbits. A single observer analyzed the behavior of 24 male adult rabbits inside the cages (48 ×80 x 60 cm) before the EE application through all-sample observations over two months. After, the test group (n = 15) was introduced to large floor pens (120 ×180 cm; no ceiling) once a week for two months, with 60 s all-sample observations every 10 minutes. The control group (n = 9) was not introduced to the EE. After this period, all animals were observed once again inside the cages, in order to compare behavioral frequencies before and after the EE application using appropriate statistical tests. The test group showed increased inactivity (31.1 vs. 55.4 %; Student's t-test, t = - 9.34, p < 0.001) and a significant decrease in negative behaviors (13.5 vs. <5 %; Wilcoxon test, V = 119, p < 0.001) after the EE application. Both behavioral categories frequencies aligned more closely to values reported in other related studies. Inside the floor pens, rabbits expressed locomotory, exploratory and positive behaviors for a great portion of time (more than 80 % of total time). These findings highlight the effectiveness of implementing floor pens as EE in reducing negative behaviors, increasing daytime inactivity, and promoting exploration and positive welfare of laboratory rabbits.

Effects of two types of environmental enrichment on the behavior of dogs in shelters
Antonino, J Vet Behav · J Vet Behav · 2025 · 10.1016/j.jveb.2025.05.004

Abstract. The domestic dog, Canis lupus familiaris, is commonly kept in shelters. In these locations, features such as confinement, forced intraspecific and interspecific contact, and lack of environmental stimulation can diminish the welfare of dogs. One way to avoid poor welfare is to implement an environmental enrichment program for dogs in shelters, however, environmental enrichment items should be tested to evaluate their effectiveness and their ease of implementation and safety. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of two environmental enrichment items designed to improve the welfare of dogs kept in a shelter. Fifteen dogs, housed in trios, were studied. Two environmental enrichment items were offered to the dogs: nutritional-related (perforated plastic bottles filled with canine treats) and sensory-related (lavender oil). Behavioral data were collected before, during, and after the items were offered, using focal sampling and instantaneous recording methods, with a sampling interval of 1 min. The effectiveness of the items was evaluated using Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMMs). Food enrichment increased foraging and activity behaviors in the dogs, but not positive social behaviors. On the other hand, olfactory enrichment decreased jumping and vocalizing and increased the display of climbing on the pen's door. Both items proved to be safe for the dogs. It is of utmost importance for shelters to enhance animal welfare by constructing larger and more stimulating bays and implementing environmental enrichment programs for the animals. The results of this study show that an enrichment program can assist in improving the welfare of dogs kept in shelters.

Functional significance and welfare implications of chewing in dogs (Canis familiaris)
Quinn, Frontiers in Veterinary Science · Frontiers in Veterinary Science · 2025 · 10.1111/j.1556-4029.2007.00467.x

Abstract. Dogs chew on both nutritive and non-nutritive items as part of their food acquisition, ingestive behaviour, self-care, and social interactions. Various definitions distinguish chewing from related oral activities, such as gnawing, masticating, and biting. Surprisingly, despite chewing being a ubiquitous behaviour in dogs, its relevance to a dog's comfort, health, and purpose remains unclear. Additionally, the risk of dental fractures or other injuries may lead veterinarians to advise against feeding bones to dogs. Abstract. Human–animal interaction (HAI) such a dog walking is used as an intervention to decrease stress in individuals with Post-traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD). However, it is important to know whether PTSD symptoms of a dog walker and the walk itself stress the shelter dogs. This exploratory project evaluated the impact of a shelter-dog walking program for veterans on stress experienced by the dogs. It also examined whether the PTSD status of the veteran influenced the impact of the walk on the dog. Prior to data collection university, IRB/IACUC approvals were obtained. Shelter dogs (n = 25) were walked by veterans who were participating in an 8-week walking program. Seventeen of the walks (45%) were by veterans who reported PTSD. Dog stress was assessed with heart rate variability (HRV) measured for 24 h before, during, and for one hour after the veteran’s first and last walks. The walks were designed to take 30 min. The study included 38 walks by 25 dogs and their 28 veteran walkers. The dogs were largely male (60%) and mixed breeds (80%).
